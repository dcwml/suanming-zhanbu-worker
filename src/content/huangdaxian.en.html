Wong Tai Sin Oracle Sticks

The Wong Tai Sin oracle sticks come from the Wong Tai Sin Temple in Hong Kong. Wong Tai Sin — the Jin-dynasty Taoist Huang Chuping, famed for answering every sincere plea — has drawn worshippers for over a century. The set numbers one hundred sticks in five grades, each bearing a four-line poem and a classical tale that spells out fortune's ebb and flow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do the Wong Tai Sin oracle sticks come from?

Wong Tai Sin is the Jin-dynasty Taoist Huang Chuping, who attained the Way at Jinhua in Zhejiang and is honored in the Hong Kong temple that bears his name, famous for answering every sincere plea. The temple's hundred-stick set has been in use for over a century: each stick carries a four-line poem and a classical tale, making it one of the most widely circulated temple oracles.

How do I read the grade on a stick?

Sticks fall into five grades: Supremely Auspicious (上上) — fortune favors most endeavors; Very Auspicious (上吉) — more gain than setback; Auspicious (中吉) — steady and promising; Neutral (中平) — even, keep steady and await timing; Very Inauspicious (下下) — heavy going, guard rather than advance. The verdicts then break matters down by marriage, career, wealth and health.

Are the readings authoritative?

The stick texts follow the widely circulated temple version; the readings draw on classical oracle-stick literature and are offered as cultural reasoning for reference only. Please weigh important decisions in light of your own circumstances.
