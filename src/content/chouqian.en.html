How to Choose Among the Three

All three share one folk tradition — shake the tube, let a stick fall, and read how it answers — yet differ in origin and in the matters they serve:

OracleOriginBest-fit questionsStyle
Wong Tai Sin Wong Tai Sin Temple, Hong Kong Career, wealth, worldly pursuits Five grades, each stick with a classical tale
Guanyin Folk Guanyin shrines Everyday matters, big and small Three plain grades, plain-spoken poems
Yue Lao Yue Lao Shrine, West Lake, Hangzhou Love, marriage, reconciliation Nine fine grades, matters of the heart

If unsure: for career and wealth, for the broad stroke of fortune, start with Wong Tai Sin; for the everyday matters of life, use Guanyin; for the affairs of the heart, use Yue Lao.

How to Draw, with Reverence

An online tube is simpler than a temple one, but the reverence is the same. Three steps, as at the shrine:

Center Your Mind

Quiet your mind and fix your question clearly. One matter, one stick — never mixed.

Shake with Sincerity

Hold the question in your heart, shake, and let the stick fall. While nothing about the matter has changed, do not draw again and again.

Ponder the Poem

A stick speaks in spirit, not in the letter: good fortune without pride, hard going without fear — the road remains yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do the three oracles differ?

Wong Tai Sin sticks come from Hong Kong's Wong Tai Sin Temple, read fortune in five grades and pair each stick with a classical tale — best for career and wealth; Guanyin sticks are the most widely circulated, reading three plain grades for everyday matters; Yue Lao sticks come from the shrine by West Lake in Hangzhou, dividing matters of the heart into nine fine grades.

How does drawing sticks differ from hexagram casting?

Drawing a stick matches a question to a pre-written poem: one matter, one shake, one answer. Hexagram casting (I Ching, Plum Blossom) builds a hexagram and reasons through it line by line. For a quick pointer, draw a stick; for layered analysis, cast a hexagram.

Can I draw repeatedly about the same matter?

Tradition asks one stick per matter, drawn in sincerity: while nothing about the situation has changed, repeated drawing is discouraged. Once the matter has genuinely moved on, a fresh draw is appropriate.

Are the readings authoritative?

All three sets follow the widely circulated versions of the traditional oracles; the readings draw on classical oracle-stick literature and are offered as cultural reasoning for reference only. Please weigh important decisions in light of your own circumstances.
