Hour Omens of Lunar September 2026: Two All-Grand Days, Fifty-Two Fire Trines and All-Yang Across the Month

Lunar September — deep autumn, when the metal's voice fades and the waters of winter stir — runs from October 10 to November 8, 2026: thirty days, three hundred and sixty double-hours. This essay charts them one by one, recording only the extraordinary: completed trines, directional unions, all-yang bodies and the Kui Gang day — each verdict cast in an imperial astrologer's antique voice, then explained in plain words. Published on the very first day of the month, it is a record written entirely in advance: every configuration below still lies in the future, to be checked day by day as it arrives.

The Month at a Glance

In the year of Bing Wu (the Fire Horse), the month pillar runs Wu Xu — after Start of Winter on November 7, only the month's last day, November 8, turns to Ji Hai. Frost Descent falls on October 23. Across all 360 double-hours —

  • Not one all-yin chart: the year pillar Bing Wu is wholly yang, so an all-yin chart remains structurally impossible this month, as in July and August — a small lesson in how the calendar works;
  • Eighty-four all-yang hours across half the month: the exact mirror of August's lone all-yang day — the month pillar Wu Xu is a yang pillar holding the warrant for twenty-eight days, so of the fifteen all-yang days (every second day, pillar wholly yang), fourteen yield six all-yang hours each (Zi, Yin, Chen, Wu, Shen, Xu); only on November 8 (day 30), after Start of Winter turns the pillar to the yin Ji Hai, does an all-yang day fail to produce them — August gathered its all-yang hours on the day the pillar turned; September loses them on that very day. The two months face each other like reflections;
  • Sixty-five grand configurations: fifty-two Tiger-Horse-Dog fire trines, six southern fire unions, six western metal unions, plus one hour on the Kui Gang day. On day 10 (October 19), a Bing Yin day, and day 22 (October 31), a Wu Yin day, the day's own Tiger joins the year's Horse and the month's Dog — the trine stands complete in the pillars themselves, and all twelve double-hours carry a grand mark, the fullest days of the month;
  • One Kui Gang day: day 24 (November 2), a Geng Chen day — only one of the four Kui Gang pillars visits this month, with a dedicated section.

Day-by-Day Quick Reference

One row per day. "All-yang day" marks days whose day pillar is wholly yang: on such days the six double-hours with yang stem and branch (Zi, Yin, Chen, Wu, Shen, Xu) would stand all-yang. This month's pillar Wu Xu is a yang pillar, so every all-yang day yields its six all-yang hours — eighty-four across the month — save on November 8 (day 30), when the pillar turns to the yin Ji Hai and even an all-yang day produces none: the exact mirror of the previous month's lone all-yang day. Hours are given in pinyin (Zi = Rat, Chou = Ox, Yin = Tiger, Mao = Rabbit, Chen = Dragon, Si = Snake, Wu = Horse, Wei = Goat, Shen = Monkey, You = Rooster, Xu = Dog, Hai = Pig); lunar dates as month/day of the lunar calendar (9/1 = first day of the 9th lunar month, 9/30 = the 30th); "—" means the day has none.

A Word on Method

Birth-hour configurations draw on the classical logic of stems, branches and the five elements — a cultural framework ancient observers used to relate time and destiny. The verdicts above are written in an astrologer's antique voice for the pleasure of the tradition; they are not fate. What a life becomes still rests on one's own conduct and circumstance. Read, enjoy, and take from it what encourages you.
