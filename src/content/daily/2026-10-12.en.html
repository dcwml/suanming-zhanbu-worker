With autumn metal in command, earth engenders metal, and every Xu, Chou, Wei or Chen earth day of autumn becomes a Mother's Granary — the spirit of the full storehouse — suiting worship and the collecting of wealth; the Jade Eaves keeps the dwelling trim and blesses marriages and the making of the stove. Metaphysics Insight

First among today's auspicious spirits stands Mǔ Cāng 母仓, the Mother's Granary. Its meaning is warm and simple: what engenders me is my mother, and what stores is the granary — whenever the day's element is one that the season's ruling qi engenders, Heaven and Earth hold a fostering grace toward all born under them. The rule turns with the seasons: in spring wood reigns and water breeds wood, so the water days of Hǎi and Zǐ are the Granary; in summer fire reigns and wood feeds fire, so the wood days of Yín and Mǎo; in autumn metal reigns and earth engenders metal, so the earth days of Chén, Xū, Chǒu and Wèi; in winter water reigns and metal breeds water, the metal days of Shēn and Yǒu. Today, Jǐ Wèi — Wèi being earth in deep autumn — falls squarely under the Granary. The old almanacs say such days favor worship, prayers, collecting wealth, adding to the household, herding and planting — every one an act of storing up and nurturing life. Fittingly, the Granary rhymes with the farming calendar: on the third day of the ninth month, with Frost's Descent not yet come, new grain cures in the sun and old stores go into the bin — to set "collecting wealth on an earth day" here is simply to ride Heaven and Earth's movement toward keeping. And note today's columns: collecting wealth stands favored while a grand opening alone is avoided — income entering the bin is storing; a business flung open is spending forth. One and the same money matter, yet keeping and casting each have their hour — this is how finely the old almanac draws its lines.
