On a Jiǎ Wǔ day, Jiǎ is yang wood — first among the ten stems, the wood of the towering tree; Wǔ is yang fire, the fire of high noon, the branch whose fire burns at its peak — Jiǎ wood seated on Wǔ fire makes wood-and-fire shining bright together: a great tree bathed in full sun, its pattern blazing. The month branch is Hài water, and Hài is Jiǎ wood's place of long life: after the Start of Winter the water gathers strength, and the Rén water hidden in Hài feeds Jiǎ wood at its root — with the month's own long-life aid beneath it, today's tree stands deep-rooted and full-leaved. The day god arrives at Qīnglóng 青龙, the Azure Dragon — head of the twelve Yellow-and-Black Path day gods and first of the six Yellow Path lucky ones: the Azure Dragon governs all things auspicious and the aid of noble people; when the Dragon holds the day, all matters may go forward — one of the almanac's rare open, sunlit days. The five auspicious spirits each carry weight: Yuèdé 月德, the Month's Virtue, is the noble-spirit of the month — virtue and majesty together, before whom every baleful spirit gives ground: the nemesis of the whole inauspicious list. Sìxiàng 四相, the Four Phases, governs the flourishing of the four seasons — building and making stand well. Pǔhù 普护, Universal Protection, shields all beneath heaven — prayers for blessing and the dissolving of troubles both find their use. Míngwǔ 鸣吠 takes the harmonious cries of cock-crow and dog-bark, governing the accord of yin and yang and a home alive with sound. Qīnglóng itself is the day god presiding, adding one more layer of nobility. The three inauspicious: Tiānlì 天吏, the Heavenly Clerk, governs harsh officials and litigation — matters of papers and courts are to be shunned: contracts and documents stand favorable today, but entanglements of lawsuits and contested property are not to be entered. Zhìsǐ 致死, Death-Bringing, governs grave illness — visits of mourning, calls on the gravely sick, are best left undone. Wǔxū 五虚, the Five Emptinesses, means wealth-qi floating unsubstantial — the seasons' full qi not yet arrived, the storehouse standing empty: hence today opening storehouses and shipping out goods and wealth are both avoided — where fortune is not yet solid, to open is to leak, and what goes out is slow to come home. The five avoided matters all walk one road: raising the ridge beam and roofing the house are work upon the crown of the dwelling — the beam sits at the roof's peak, high-placed and wind-exposed; on an empty day nothing is to be lifted high. Opening storehouses and shipping out goods are the opening of the wealth-vault — with the Five Emptinesses on duty, what belongs today is inflow, not outflow. Building ships is the great labor of the waters — Jiǎ Wǔ's Na Yin is Gold in the Sand: gold lying in the sandbed, unsmelted, unpolished; to launch great works upon the water sinks the metal deeper. The favorable list numbers twenty-three, near a sweep of the almanac's great matters: betrothal gifts, engagement pact and marriages — the three rites of courtship complete, a first-rank wedding day. Sacrificial rites, praying for blessings, building temples — with the Azure Dragon and the Month's Virtue together, reverence to the gods bears fullest fruit. Moving residence, entering a new home, installing doors, setting the bed, building and repairing, breaking ground, laying foundations — every matter of removal and construction stands open. Opening a business, collecting wealth, traveling, meeting kin and friends, entering school, clearing misfortune, digging ponds, building livestock pens, raising livestock, carving and engraving — a hundred trades advancing at once, all matters well met. The Na Yin is Gold in the Sand: Jiǎ Wǔ's metal lies within the sand — unsmelted by fire, unmet by the polisher's hand, the gold of "panning gold from sand." The lucky day is the sandbank; the matters at hand are the grains of gold — sift, and the gold is yours. Clash the Rat, the Sha lies North: Zǐ and Wǔ stand opposed — keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership with Rat people, and let northern journeys take a slower step.

In wealth, the Wealth God sits Northeast and collecting wealth stands in the favorable column: inbound payments, collecting receivables, closing contracts for payment all run smooth — yet with the Five Emptinesses on duty and storehouses and shipments avoided, today's fortune belongs to inflow, not outflow: receive and bank, yes; large outlays, clearance sell-offs, lending from the vault, no. More in, less out — that is today's way of gathering wealth. In love, today is the rarest of days: betrothal gifts, engagement pact and marriages, all three rites stand favorable — proposals, betrothals, registrations, the wedding feast itself: every one is a day the almanac has stamped with its seal; with the Month's Virtue shining, the marriage carries the blessing of elders. Traveling is favorable — business trips and journeys afar can all set out. Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the snake shed its skin; today the Horse of Wǔ keeps the watch — so let us speak of this horse. First, the root sense of the graph Wǔ: the Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字, the Explanations of Graphs, says: "Wǔ means 'to meet in opposition' (wǔ 啎). In the fifth month the yin qi rises against the yang, breaking out of the earth." Wǔ is the position where yang qi, cresting at its peak, meets and stands against the yin: in the year, Wǔ is the fifth month; in the day, Wǔ is high noon — both the hours when yang climbs to its very summit, and so Wǔ is called "the seat of pure yang." The Horse is paired with Wǔ for just this reason: of all creatures the horse runs richest in qi and blood, and when it gallops its yang flares brightest — a creature cut to the measure of the noon position. Then the horse's most glorious tale — the Dragon-Horse bearing the River Chart: the Zhonghou of the Book of Documents 尚书·中候 and the Book of Han 汉书 both record that when Fúxī 伏羲 ruled the world, a dragon-horse came forth from the River Mèng — horse-bodied, dragon-scaled, bearing the River Chart 河图 upon its back; Fúxī took it as his pattern and drew from it the Eight Trigrams — and the Yijing's line, "The River brought forth the Chart, the Luo brought forth the Writ, and the sages took their pattern from them," means that the fountainhead of the Eight Trigrams was carried in, on a horse's back. Today the Azure Dragon holds the day, and the image of the dragon-horse answers it exactly: the dragon leading the way in front, the horse raising its hooves behind — a lucky-day picture of "the noble one leading, the fine steed taking the van." Last, speak of Bólè 伯乐: his own name was Sūn Yáng 孙阳, a man of the time of Duke Mù of Qín in the Spring and Autumn years, renowned for judging horses — "swift steeds are common; a Bólè who knows them is rare." One essay of Hán Yù 韩愈's, "On the Horse," says plain the everlasting riddle of recognizing and being recognized. Today the Month's Virtue, the noble spirit, shines upon the day — the very image of "Bólè on the road": the noble one can see you, but you must have the saddle made ready too — coat gleaming, bones strong, or the thousand-li talent is left buried between stable and trough. Success comes galloping, but it is never waited for — the dragon-horse bore the Chart, Bólè cast his eye, and the steed itself ran down that stretch of road.
