Relocating · Moving into a house

On a Guǐ Mǎo day the Guǐ water sits upon Mǎo wood — water feeding wood, the qi unfolding gently — yet today's Mǎo branch stands squarely against the month's Yǒu: the day's branch clashing the month's is Yuèpò, the Month Breaker. With the Breaker holding the day, even the Bright Hall of the Yellow Path can borrow only the single sense of "breaking": demolish the old house, tear down the wall — pull down the worn to make way for the new; seek the physician, treat the illness — break the sickness to restore the ease. The Breaker undoes undertakings, and so the almanac says plainly: avoid all other matters and launch nothing great. Dàhào the Great Consumer follows in its train — guard the purse, neither lend nor borrow. Relocating and moving into a house stand in the taboos; let any change of home wait a few days. One day past the Mid-Autumn full moon, its light not yet spent — keep the hearth, tidy the rooms, and close out the stale affairs that have sat too long: quiet clearing of the old is the day's proper work.

Zodiac Fortune · Rabbit 兔

Today's earthly branch is Mǎo, which corresponds to the Rabbit — making the Rabbit the zodiac protagonist of the day. The Rabbit presides over its own branch, yet with Mǎo clashing Yǒu and the Month Breaker on duty, this home game carries a note of restraint: launch nothing great — signed deals, business openings and long journeys are best set down, and the Breaker's own art of subtraction serves best: clear out old belongings, repair the house, close the stale accounts that have sat too long — all these ride the day's qi. Health is the day's proper business: seeking treatment stands in the favored column, so the lingering ailment, the overdue checkup, the postponed regimen — begin them today and the effort pays double; sleep early to nourish the liver, for Mǎo wood fed by Guǐ water is made for quiet recovery. Wealth stays conservative: with Dàhào at the side, neither lend nor borrow — wealth kept is wealth earned. Keep company light: with Yuèyàn and Dìhuǒ on duty, tongues grow sharp, and Roosters above all are in the day's clash — when a quarrel crosses your path, one step back is the whole remedy. The day clashes with the Rooster and the Sha lies West: those born in the Year of the Rooster should keep a low profile in all matters, avoid journeys toward the West, and refrain from quarrels with Rabbits.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday was the Mid-Autumn full moon; today, the sixteenth of the eighth month, the ancients called Jì Wàng — "after the fullness," as when Su Shi's Red Cliff Rhapsody opens on "the sixteenth day of the seventh month." The moon past its fullest begins to wane, the yin creeps upward, and the ancients read such a day as the hinge where fullness turns toward decline. Today's almanac carries a harder-edged name as well: Yuèpò, the Month Breaker — the day on which the day's branch clashes the month's. The eighth month is built on Yǒu; a Mǎo day clashing Yǒu makes the Breaker. In the old cosmology the month's station is where the month's qi gathers, and a day-branch rushing headlong against it is a tide running backward — thus the Month Breaker heads the list of ill days, and great matters are not to be used; only the word "break" itself wins an exception: demolishing houses, tearing down walls, seeking the physician, treating illness — break answering break, sickness drawn out by breaking it, and the day is well met. This logic of "clash" is the bedrock of date-selection: the six earthly clashes (Zǐ–Wǔ, Chǒu–Wèi, Yín–Shēn, Mǎo–Yǒu, Chén–Xū, Sì–Hài) pair off against one another, opposite in direction and at war in element. That today's Bright Hall of the Yellow Path shares the day with the Month Breaker is a question the ancients settled long ago — a Breaker day, even under the Yellow Path, is still judged as broken: auspicious spirits cannot outweigh the root clash, as no skill of the helmsman can mend a boat rowing against the current. With this one layer understood, the short line "avoid all other matters" on the old almanac shows its true weight: four characters carrying a thousand years of reckoning.
