The Na Yin turns out a layer of water — Bǐng Wǔ and Dīng Wèi share "Celestial River Water 天河水," the water of the Milky Way overhead: a band of heavenly river hung above dry fire, rain falling on the flame's own head — within the dryness, a clear moistening. The day god arrives at Qīnglóng 青龙, the Azure Dragon — head of the six Yellow Path gods, the first rank of grand luck among the twelve day spirits: the Azure Dragon governs patrons and celebration, and this ought to be a Yellow Path great-lucky day on which every matter may rise. Yet open the baleful column and the heaviest at its head is Sìfèi 四废, the Four Wastes: since Winter's Start the month's command sits in Hài, and fire — born at Yín, throned at Wǔ — is cut off, jué 绝, at Hài. Bǐng Wǔ, stem and branch all fire, sits squarely in the month where fire is cut off: a stove of red-bright charcoal carried stove and all into an icehouse — the firebox plainly still holds flame, but the cold on every side draws its strength out thread by thread, and neither "thriving" nor "flourishing" will touch it — the five phases without qi, blessing and virtue absent: that is the root of the two characters "waste," the season draining this day's fire dry. The Yellow Path is the sky's timing; the day pillar is the day's own qi — the sky's luck however bright, a wasted day's qi discounts it, which is why the favorable column narrows to its few matters and closes, as ever, with "all other matters set aside." Hence the avoided column's four — marriage, entering a new home, construction, breaking ground — are every one a work of building and founding: on a wasted day the strength is not there, and nothing founded stands. Red matters avoided and white matters favored is today's almanac plainest face: weddings, contracts, market openings and relocations all wait; sweeping, worship, burying and mourning each keep their place. Clash the Rat, the Sha lies North: Zǐ and Wǔ stand opposed — with Rat people today, guard against contention and go slow on partnership; northern journeys take a slower step. The Azure Dragon's Yellow Path with the Four Wastes holding the watch — today's measure is one line: when the sky's timing lends no strength, do the small works that warm the hands — sweep, offer worship, finish and settle, bank the fire into the stove; the great flame of weddings and founding waits for a day when fire thrives.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday's talk was of Yuèpò, the Monthly Rupture — of "clash": the day branch meeting the month's command head-on, the day's qi scattered and nothing gathered. Today's subject is a more obscure figure in the baleful column: Sìfèi 四废, the Four Wastes. The Waste shares no lineage with the Rupture: the Rupture takes "clash"; the Waste takes "cut-off" — jué 绝. The Lìlì defines it in one line: "hours of the five phases without qi, when blessing and virtue do not arrive." What does it mean? Through the four seasons the qi turns with the month's command — in winter months water thrives, and fire, come winter, walks to the far end of the twelve life-stages: fire is born at Yín, throned at Wǔ, and cut off at Hài. Today is Bǐng Wǔ, stem and branch all fire, sitting squarely in the middle of fire's cut-off month — like a stove of red-bright charcoal carried stove and fire together into the icehouse: the firebox plainly still holds flame, but the cold on every side draws its strength away thread by thread, and neither "thriving" 旺 nor "flourishing" 相 will touch it — that is "waste." Each season keeps its own wasted days: metal is cut off at Yín, so spring's wastes are Gēng Shēn and Xīn Yǒu; water is cut off at Sì, so summer's wastes are Rén Zǐ and Guǐ Hài; wood is cut off at Shēn, so autumn's wastes are Jiǎ Yín and Yǐ Mǎo; fire is cut off at Hài, so winter's wastes are Bǐng Wǔ and Dīng Sì — one rule common to all four: the day pillar's five phases stand precisely "cut off" in the month that commands the season, season and day pillar singing against each other in every line. Waste is not the balefulness of fierce gods charging face-on; it is a more thorough thing: every auspicious spirit's power must borrow the day's own qi to unfold, and the day's qi wasted, the spirits too find "blessing and virtue absent" — which is today's lesson worth the most thought: the Azure Dragon holds the Yellow Path, the first rank of grand luck among the twelve day gods, yet the favorable column still opens only ten matters and closes with "all else set aside." Reading an almanac has always meant reading two layers: first whether the day pillar's qi thrives, flourishes, rests or is imprisoned; then which way the spirits face, Yellow Path or Black — when the two layers agree, that is a genuinely good day; if the day's own qi falls first, the sky's luck however bright lends nothing. The old almanac's verdict on the Waste is shorter still, three characters: "every matter barred." Yet carried out to today's columns, the book leaves two narrow seams open: worship and sweeping walk under the Universal Guard's shield, the eight burial matters walk the Crowing and Barking's road of sound — whatever belongs to "gathering in" rather than "letting out," the waste does not waste. So no need for alarm on a Waste day: it is more like a stove's leftover warmth in deep winter — the great fire must not be lit, but it warms both hands: dust and sweep, offer and remember, finish and settle — take in what is due for taking in, and leave the fire-seed meant for letting out to a day when fire thrives.
