On a Rén Xū day, Rén water wears the cap and sash at Xū — and Xū is itself the month pillar of the ninth month, with earth in season and holding power; only Rén is the water of the Vast Sea, its Na Yin named for the image of "gathering every river, boundless as the ocean" — so great a sea no handful of soil can dam. Yet the day's heavenly spirit is the White Tiger on the Black Path — the god of autumn metal's killing air, presiding over arms and mourning — hence six items in the avoid column: felling trees, funeral processions, breaking earth, marriages, burial and cutting channels; all rites of burial and wedding should give way. Fortunately the auspicious list is far from weak: the Void of the Month favors building and petitions, so raising the ridgepole and setting the door proceed together; the Mother's Granary presides over nurture and storage, so collecting wealth, buying livestock and building pens all find their place; in the pattern of the Four strengths, autumn counts Rén and Guǐ as its thriving stems, and a Rén day stands at the very height of the season; the Guarding Day favors keeping what one has; the Heavenly Horse spurs movement — hence travel, relocation, moving house and setting the bed, item after item. On a White Tiger day, worship and prayer are precisely the remedy: offerings, blessings, praying for offspring, consecration and dispelling misfortune all stand in the favorable column — "dispelling" is the almanac's own term for removing calamity; what must be avoided is blood and rites of the dead, and that is all.

Zodiac Fortune · Dog 狗

Today's earthly branch is Xū, which corresponds to the Dog — making the Dog the zodiac protagonist of the day. Xū is also the month pillar of the ninth month: Dog people hold the month's command today, their momentum naturally strong; and the day master Rén water wears the cap and sash at Xū — the cap and sash, a sign of dressing for the occasion and stepping forward, favoring putting affairs in order and taking charge. Yet the White Tiger on the Black Path presses right upon the natal branch: Dog people should keep rather than charge today — the "Guarding Day" in the auspicious list says exactly this — deferring great matters of marriage, burial and ground-breaking, taking care with blades and traffic when out, and refusing quarrels and shows of force. On wealth, however, the day is generous: the Mother's Granary presides over the storehouse, and the favorable column lists collecting wealth, buying livestock and building pens outright — tallying inflows, setting accounts in order and arranging funds all come due. The Heavenly Horse is in the list and spurs movement — travel, moving house and job transfers all run smoothly, only mind the direction: the day clashes with the Dragon and the Sha lies North. Those born in the Year of the Dragon are clashed today: avoid long journeys north and refrain from contending with Dogs. Frost's Descent draws near, and the gap between day and night widens — layer clothing against the chill, sleep early, and store up strength for the winter.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Heavenly Virtue held the Yellow Path; today the rota has already passed to the White Tiger — one yellow, one black, a single day apart, a portrait of the twelve spirits' daily rotation. The old formula for the starting point: in the Zǐ and Wǔ months the Azure Dragon begins at the Shēn day; in Chǒu–Wèi at Xū; in Yín–Shēn at Zǐ; in Mǎo–Yǒu at Yín; in Chén–Xū at Chén; in Sì–Hài at Wǔ. The present ninth month is a Xū month, so the Azure Dragon starts at the Chén day and moves one seat each day after: on the ninth, a Bǐng Chén day, the Dragon took its post; the tenth, the Hall of Light; yesterday, the fourteenth, a Xīn Yǒu day, exactly the Heavenly Virtue — which is why the "Treasured Light" sat in yesterday's auspicious list, for the Heavenly Virtue of the twelve spirits is also named Treasured Light; and today, the fifteenth, a Rén Xū day, the turn falls to the White Tiger — with the day branch and the month pillar both Xū, the White Tiger sits upon the very seat of the month's power, its killing air doubled. The White Tiger is the emblem of the West among the Four Symbols, lord of the seven mansions Kuí, Lóu, Wèi, Mǎo, Bì, Zī and Shēn — the essence of autumn metal — and so presides over arms, punishment and the blood of mourning; the almanac tradition ranks it on the Black Path, and the White Tiger day's standing taboos followed: weddings, funeral processions, burial and breaking earth are all avoided — set today's avoid column of six beside them and the fit is exact. Yet the almanac never closes the road: in the favorable column, offerings, prayers, praying for offspring, consecration and dispelling misfortune stand in plain sight — placating the fierce with worship, lifting misfortune with the rite of dispelling. Folk custom adds the Qilin charm: wedding households paste "the Qilin has arrived here" upon the lintel — the Qilin, the benevolent beast, treads no living grass and eats no living thing, and by benevolence masters ferocity, so the fierce spirit withdraws of itself. Tomorrow, the sixteenth, the rota returns to the Jade Hall — another Yellow Path day. Six yellow, six black, turning day by day: the almanac does not frighten you with the Black Path — it tells you what to avoid, how to dissolve it, and where to seek your fortune.
