Inauspicious Spirits

Yuèpò 月破 · Dàhào 大耗 · Chòngrì 重日 · Gōuchén 勾陈

Marriages 嫁娶 · Burial 安葬

On a Guǐ Sì day, Guǐ is yin water — the water of dew, rain and frost, the softest stroke among the twelve stems; Sì is yin fire, and within it hide Bǐng, Wù and Gēng — Bǐng fire is Guǐ water's Direct Wealth, Wù earth its Direct Officer, Gēng metal its Direct Seal: wealth, office and seal, three in one, all hidden beneath the seat — a fine move, played but not shown. Yet today the branch Sì stands in head-on collision with the month branch Hài — Sì and Hài form one of the six clashes: the day branch striking through the month's foundation, and this is called "Month Break" (Yuèpò), whence the day's name: the month's qi of strength is struck and scattered, nothing holds firm, and great matters done upon it scatter too. The day god arrives at Gōuchén 勾陈, a Black Path inauspicious day: Gōuchén is the god of fields and earth, governing delay, entanglement, lawsuits and the shadow of the courts — a day made for stillness from the start. The five auspicious spirits each carry their own story: Yìmǎ 驿马, the Post-Horse, governs motion — journeys, moves, changes of station — yet with Month Break towering in front, however strong the horse's legs it dares not gallop; journeys afar are best rescheduled. Tiānhòu 天后, the Empress of Heaven, is the water goddess, sheltering those who sail and women in childbirth, guarding safety and peace. Tiāncāng 天仓, the Heavenly Granary, fills the storehouses and sets each thing in its place of keeping. Jìng'ān 敬安, Reverent Peace, brings quietude and calm — Tiānhòu, Tiāncāng and Jìng'ān all walk the single road of the word "safe": they protect peace, not enterprise. Bùjiàng 不将, No-Clash, the foremost marriage spirit, held its post yesterday and holds it again today — yet marriage still falls into the avoid column: the clash of Month Break is not to be dissolved by one kindly spirit; a grand wedding is a matter of rites, and where the clash lies, it is not lucky. Then the inauspicious: Yuèpò 月破, Month Break, heads the list — the month's foundation struck through, the very image of scattering. Dàhào 大耗, the Great Depletion, follows at its heel — on a broken day wealth-qi drains away, so today: no collecting of fortune, no borrowing or lending, no large outlays. Chòngrì 重日, the Day of Return, also stands present — Sì and Hài are the repeat-days of each month, and the old rule says great matters done on them may "come round again"; yesterday the Day of Return held the post under its other name, today it takes over. Gōuchén closes the ranks on the Black Path. The two avoided matters are both grand rites: marriages — the great wedding offends against Month Break and the Day of Return alike, and cannot proceed; burial — the dead go into the earth for peace, but on a broken day the qi scatters, and the departed would not rest nor the living be at ease. The favorable list numbers five, seeming spare, yet holding one thread of life: demolishing the house and breaking down walls — tearing down the old house and the broken wall, borrowing the day's own breaking-force to break what is old; seeking medical care and treating illness — where old evils of sickness have entrenched themselves, the day's breaking-qi strikes and drives them out, and healing stands in its very hour; and "take up nothing else" — four characters that are the almanac's plainest warning for a broken day: beyond these four matters of breaking the old and driving out illness, take up nothing. The Na Yin is Chángliúshuǐ 长流水, Long Flowing Water: Guǐ Sì's water is a slender stream running through the land of Sì fire — after the Start of Winter the water gathers strength while the fire is not yet out, and the thin stream, meeting warmth, does not freeze — a broken day to dredge the channel, clearing the blockage, so the slender water can flow long. Clash the Pig, the Sha lies East: Sì and Hài stand opposed — keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership with Pig people, and let eastern journeys take a slower step.

Zodiac Fortune · Snake 蛇

Today's earthly branch is Sì, which corresponds to the Snake — making the Snake the protagonist of the day: the Snake of Sì keeps its watch, and it falls upon Month Break, the Great Depletion and Gōuchén's Black Path — today's snake is a snake shedding its skin: until the old skin goes, the new body cannot grow; shedding is hard labor, yet it is the necessary road of growth. Only one kind of work suits today — "breaking the old and driving out illness": the image of demolishing houses and breaking walls, brought down into daily life, means clearing out old things, letting the clutter go, dredging what is clogged, closing out old accounts and old affairs — the cabinet long left pressed full, the favors never returned, the old proposals left hanging: to set hands to clearing them today is exactly in season. Seeking medical care and treating illness stand favorable — check-up follow-ups, tending chronic ailments, acupuncture and bone-setting are all in their hour: the toothache long endured, the arrows on last month's test report — go see the doctor today, borrowing the broken day's power to drive out illness. "Take up nothing else" is today's general rule: signing contracts, opening shops, moving house or home, journeys afar — every great matter waits one more day before being weighed. In wealth, the Great Depletion stands on duty, and though the Wealth God sits South, on a day of depletion fortune is not collected: no large outlays, no trusting easy investments, no borrowing or lending out, and keep watch on wallet and papers — the Great Depletion's harm is guarded against in the two words "quick hands." The Post-Horse stands in the list, and the chance of movement remains — interview invitations, word of a transfer, travel plans — yet with Month Break towering ahead: reschedule the journey, slow-sign the contract; an opportunity does not expire in a day. In love, No-Clash stands present while marriage is avoided: proposals, registrations and betrothals take a slower step — even the foremost of marriage spirits cannot carry the clash of Month Break; for the unwed Snake, deepening acquaintance is well, grand gestures not yet. In the body, Sì answers the spleen, and in the Sì hours, nine to eleven in the morning, the Spleen meridian keeps watch — breakfast warm and hot, chewed slowly and well, for one quiet morning meal most nourishes the spleen's earth; Guǐ water answers the kidneys, and after the Start of Winter: lie early, rise late, nourish the kidneys and store essence. Gōuchén governs delay and entanglement: patience first today — no quarreling over idle breath, keep clear of lawsuits and papers' tangles — what entangles today will loosen of itself tomorrow. Clash the Pig, Sha East: with Pig people today, keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership; eastern routes take a slower step.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the dragon lay hidden in the deep; today the Snake of Sì keeps the watch — the only creature in the twelve that renews itself by shedding, which is the perfect opening for today's word: "Month Break." First, how Month Break arises: the almanac counts its days by the twelve Day Officers — Establish, Sweep Out, Full, Level, Settle, Hold, Break, Peril, Success, Gather, Open, Close — twelve officers rotating in turn. The officer matching the month branch is "Establish," and counting onward, the seventh is "Break" — and Break falls exactly on the month branch's clash-position: the month of Hài is established on Hài, and Sì clashes with Hài, so any day of Sì in this month is a Break day. On a Month Break day, the month's own qi of strength is struck and scattered; scattered qi cannot hold a matter together — hence the almanac's near-sweeping verdict for the broken day: the great avoided, take up nothing else. But the wonder of the character 破, "break," lies in its two faces: to strike through is to scatter, which is ill; to borrow the breaking-force and break down the old, is well — demolishing the house and breaking down walls tears down the old house and the crumbling wall that should come down; seeking care and treating illness drives out the chronic ailment that should be driven out. When the old does not go, the new cannot come — the Break day is the almanac's reserved date for breaking with the old and establishing the new. Then the image of the snake: the Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字, the Explanations of Graphs, says: "Sì means 'already.' In the fourth month the yang qi has come forth, the yin qi has gone hidden, and the ten thousand things appear, formed into their patterns." The root sense of Sì is yang already emerged, all things formed — and the snake sheds its old skin and grows a new body, one shed, one change, the very counterpoint of the Break day's "out with the old, in with the new"; the folk also call the snake "the little dragon" — the dragon lies low in the deep, the snake sheds in season: both are the craft of knowing the hour, lying hidden, and changing when the time comes. Last, the auspicious spirit Tiānhòu, the Empress of Heaven: she was Lín Mòniáng 林默娘, of Méizhōu isle in Pútian, Fujian, in the Song dynasty — born with second sight, she spent her life rescuing those lost at sea, and passed at twenty-eight; the islanders raised a temple to her, and dynasty after dynasty heaped on new titles, until the Kangxi reign of the Qing raised her to "Empress of Heaven" — a fisherman's daughter become the sea's guardian honored by all the waters. That she stands in today's list is protection for crossing the broken day in safety: a water goddess for the whole road, watching over a water-fated day — those born of the long flowing water have the Empress of Heaven looking on. The "break" of the Break day was never meant to break your fortune — it is the hour given you to break with the old: settle one old account, tear down one wall of the heart, drive out one chronic ill — take up nothing else, and wait in stillness: tomorrow, the day of Success, comes round again.
