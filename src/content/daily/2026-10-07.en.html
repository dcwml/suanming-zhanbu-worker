On a Jiǎ Yín day, Jiǎ wood sits upon Yín — the branch of its Lin Guan prosperity, where the stem draws its very salary, so the wood qi rises vigorous and full. The Na Yin is Great Stream Water: torrent hurrying toward the gorge, water feeding wood in generous measure. The Azure Dragon — first of the twelve path spirits — holds the Yellow Path today, joined by the Month's Emptiness, the Resolving Spirit, the Five Harmonies and the Cockcrow Pair: a roster of auspicious spirits few days can match. Yet autumn metal is in command, and a Jiǎ Yín day falls squarely under the Four Abandonments — stem and branch alike sunk in decline. Hence the favored column holds only eight undertakings, all of the "closing and seeing-off" kind: the Cockcrow Pair governs matters of the netherworld, lending its strength to encoffinment, mourning rites, breaking earth and burial; the Resolving Spirit dissolves and cleanses, favoring baths and sweeping away of vermin. The avoided seven have their own logic: the Little Consumer squanders wealth, so no opening of businesses; the Return Taboo mars homecomings, so relocation and bed-setting wait; and on an Abandonment day no great rites or great ventures — sacrifices, weddings, roof beams and hospital visits are all better postponed. With Cold Dew arriving tomorrow and metal qi swelling further, the day counsels gathering in, not straining forth.

Metaphysics Insight

Today's almanac presents another telling disparity: the Azure Dragon is first of the twelve path spirits, the most auspicious of the Yellow Path deities — yet on a day under its patronage the favored column holds a mere eight matters, most of them funerary or cleansing, while grand affairs like opening a business or marrying sit in the avoided column. The culprit hides in plain sight among the inauspicious spirits: the Four Abandonments. They follow a fixed verse — spring claims Gēng Shēn and Xīn Yǒu, summer Rén Zǐ and Guǐ Hài, autumn Jiǎ Yín and Yǐ Mǎo, winter Bǐng Wǔ and Guǐ Hài: two stem-branch pairs each season, iron law unbroken. The principle is "qi exhausted, therefore abandoned": when autumn's metal holds command with killing sharpness, plants wither before it — and Jiǎ Yín and Yǐ Mǎo are pillars of pure wood, wood sunk to its deepest decline. Hence the almanac's verdict: on Four Abandonment days, stems and branches stand exhausted and no great venture should launch, weddings, openings and taking office above all. Yet the old date-selectors did not deal in absolutes: qi in decline should be gathered, not released — and so it accords precisely with sending things off. Funerals, laying aside mourning clothes, bathing, sweeping out the house: all are acts of closing and letting go. Add the Cockcrow Pair, patron of the netherworld, and the Resolving Spirit, patron of dissolution, and the day's arrangement falls naturally into place. There has never been a blanket "good day" in the old almanac: the Yellow Path is one measure, the day's own qi another — auspicious spirits and five-phase vigor must both be weighed, and therein lies the fine discrimination of date selection.
