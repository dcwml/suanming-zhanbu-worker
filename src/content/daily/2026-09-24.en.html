On Xīn Chǒu day the Xīn metal stem sits upon Chǒu earth — moist earth breeding metal, the stem taking root, an image of substance carried within the season's clarity. Gōuchén keeps the Black Path, lord of entanglement and delay, yet four auspicious spirits — Mǔcāng, Sānhé, Shíyīn and Jīntáng — attend together, and the ill omen finds its remedy: Mǔcāng, the Mother Granary, governs full storehouses, and so collecting wealth and building granaries stand in the favors — settling accounts, banking receipts and storing up are all in season; Sānhé, the Triple Harmony, gathers things into accord, so gatherings with friends and kin, blessing prayers and praying for an heir all come to fruition; Jīntáng, the Golden Hall, keeps the dwelling clear and bright, which is why construction and groundbreaking stand favored — though with Sǐqì and Wǔmù on duty, undertakings should proceed steady rather than hasty, and moving into a house or relocating should still wait: build the house by all means, but choose another lucky day for the move. Each inauspicious spirit carries its counsel: Fùrì, the Day of Repetition, forbids doing a thing twice — the equinox rites of saluting the moon were held but yesterday, and so sacrificial rites fall into the taboos; building stoves, lighting fires, opening ditches and adding to the household are all best deferred. The equinox is past and the metal qi swells daily: drawing in, storing up and settling what is at hand are the day's proper work — tomorrow the moon comes full, and this is the hour to ready the gifts and await it.

The Ox presides over its own branch, and though Gōuchén holds the Black Path, the four auspicious spirits — Mǔcāng, Sānhé, Shíyīn and Jīntáng — stand in attendance: a day of storing first and surging later. Fortune is the day's brightest quarter: Mǔcāng governs the granary, and collecting wealth and building granaries stand plainly in the favors — settling accounts, banking receipts, taking stock and saving up all run smoothly; only remember that today favors gathering over letting go, so large loans and bold investments are best deferred. Sānhé gathers things into accord, and gathering with friends and kin stands favored: tomorrow is the Mid-Autumn Festival, so today is precisely the hour to call on relatives, ready the gifts and fix the reunion table; blessing prayers, praying for an heir, consecration and vegetarian rites are favored too — incense offered today finds auspicious ears. In career, construction and groundbreaking stand favored — renovations may advance and works may start — though with Sǐqì and Wǔmù on duty, keep the pace steady rather than rushed. Moving into a house and relocating stand in the taboos: put off the housewarming for another lucky date. The day clashes with the Goat and the Sha lies East: those born in the Year of the Goat should keep a low profile in all matters, avoid journeys toward the East, and refrain from quarrels with Oxen.

Metaphysics Insight

The spirit on duty today, Gōuchén, is a name with a storied past. The old almanac sets one heavenly spirit to rotate through each day: Qīnglóng, Míngtáng, Jīnkuì, Tiāndé, Yùtáng and Sīmìng walk the Yellow Path and favor fortune, while Tiānxíng, Zhūquè, Báihǔ, Tiānláo, Xuánwǔ and Gōuchén walk the Black Path and counsel caution — twelve spirits cycling in a six-day round. Yesterday Sīmìng kept the Yellow Path; today the watch passes to Gōuchén of the Black Path. Gōuchén is an ancient star official: the Book of Jin records six stars of Gōuchén all within the Purple Forbidden Enclosure, curved like a hook about the Heavenly Emperor — and the pole star we speak of tonight bears, in the old reckoning, the very name Gōuchén Yī, the pivot round which all stars wheel. A star that stands for majesty and order in the heavens becomes, in the almanac, the word "entanglement": Gōuchén governs disputes over land, lawsuits and matters that drag and stall, so on a Gōuchén day one avoids quarrels and litigation, avoids hasty moves, and lets every plan be settled before it is set in motion. Yet the Black Path is no reason to shut one's doors: with Mǔcāng, Sānhé and Jīntáng standing alongside today, collecting wealth and meeting friends proceed in all good order. The true art of seeking fortune and shunning harm was never "everything goes on Yellow Path days, nothing moves on Black Path days" — it is reading the temper of the hour: Gōuchén favors stillness, so draw in your stores and settle what is at hand, rest well — tomorrow the moon comes full, and the reunion is ready.
