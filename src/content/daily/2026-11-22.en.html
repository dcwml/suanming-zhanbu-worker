On a Gēng Zǐ day, Gēng is yang metal — the metal of axes, swords and halberds; Zǐ holds Guǐ within, the wide waters of midnight — metal generates water, and the day master's qi pours out into it: a look with the edge sheathed and the cleverness showing, metal and water mirroring each other, which the texts call "white metal and clear water," favorable to a lucid mind and a ready tongue. The Na Yin changes to Earth on the Wall 壁上土 — Gēng Zǐ and Xīn Chǒu share it: the plaster on the four walls of a house, keeping one room's warmth in; Minor Snow has arrived, the winter cold begins in earnest, and it is just the season for the wall's earth. Today is Minor Snow 小雪 — the twentieth of the twenty-four solar terms, the second of winter, the sun at two hundred and forty degrees of the ecliptic; the cold is not yet deep and the snow not yet heavy, hence "minor." The day god arrives at the White Tiger 白虎, a Black Path day of ill omen — the rhyme of the Yellow and Black Paths calls the White Tiger "Heaven's Slaughter Star," an image of killing austerity. Judged on those two characters alone, today ought to be written off entirely; yet the favorable list opens with twenty-five matters, as wide a day as any in recent weeks. The reason lies in how the almanac is reckoned: the Yellow and Black Paths are only one of the twelve day-officers, and the almanac weighs auspicious spirits against baleful ones line by line besides — the Qing-dynasty official Xiéjì Biǎnfāngshū 协纪辨方书 sets the rule: "where the auspicious outweighs the baleful, follow the favorable and disregard the avoided." Today seven auspicious spirits stand against only four baleful ones, the auspicious outweighs, and so matters follow the favorable: Tiāndéhé 天德合 — in a Hài month Heaven's Virtue stands at Yǐ, Yǐ combines with Gēng, and the noble qi alights on the day stem; Yuèkōng 月空, the yang position of the month, gives its help to construction and to breaking ground; Guānrì 官日 favors public office and administration; Tiānmǎ 天马, the Heavenly Horse, governs swift travel — journeys, removals and deliveries all draw on its strength; Jíqī 吉期 and Yào'ān 要安 govern gladness and repose; Míngfèiduì 鸣吠对 favors burial — seven auspicious ones together press down the White Tiger's single baleful one, and proposals and betrothals, marriages and prayers, shop openings and contracts, house moves and removals, foundations and roof-raising all open at once. The avoided list keeps only three matters, read by their images: setting up the bed 安床 — Dàshí 大时, Dàbài 大败 and Xiánchí 咸池 go as a trio, and Xiánchí is the popular "Peach Blossom," governing the privacy of the bed — no new bed is set today; building the stove 作灶 — the White Tiger belongs to metal, the stove to fire, and fire coming to conquer metal is precisely to rouse its austerity; warping and weaving 经络 — the setting of looms and the work of spinning and weaving — with Dàshí and Dàbài governing loss and waste, work that spends labor and materials waits a day. Clash the Horse, the Sha lies South: Zǐ and Wǔ stand opposed — the year pillar is Bǐng Wǔ and the day pillar Gēng Zǐ, so between the year and the day runs that same line from Zǐ to Wǔ — with Horse people today, guard against contention above all; southern journeys take a slower step. After Minor Snow the rainbow hides from sight, and heaven and earth close up into winter: today's matters are wide open, but decide early and finish quickly — get what must move moving before the ground freezes hard; that is the measure of a Gēng Zǐ day.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Pig of Hài told how Hài is a root in the ground; today the Hài hours are spent, Minor Snow arrives, and the Rat of Zǐ holds the day — so let us speak of Minor Snow. It is the twentieth of the twenty-four solar terms, the sun at two hundred and forty degrees of the ecliptic, the second term of winter, usually falling around November 22 in the Gregorian calendar. The Explainer of the Seventy-two Manifestations of the Months 月令七十二候集解 explains its name: "Rain falls and is pressed upon by the cold, and so congeals into snow. 'Minor' means not yet abundant." The rain comes down, the cold forces upon it, and it turns to snow; it is called "minor" because the snow has not yet gathered strength: heaven and earth have grown cold, but the cold has not reached its extreme — hence Minor Snow, and not Major Snow. The ancients divided each term into three manifestations of five days each, and Minor Snow's three show the layers of winter's entry most clearly. First, "the rainbow hides from sight": the ancients held the rainbow to be made where the water vapors of yin and yang meet; after Minor Snow the yin stands alone and the yang lies hidden, the vapors no longer meet, and the rainbow withdraws — not to show again until "the rainbow first appears," the third manifestation of Clear and Bright next spring. Second, "the qi of heaven rises and the qi of earth descends": the yang above goes upward, the yin below goes downward, and the two do not meet. Third, "all closes up and winter is made": the qi of heaven and of earth each return to their places and no longer visit, the ways between them shut, and winter properly begins. Read in a row, the three are a timetable of heaven and earth closing their doors. The people's timetable has more of the kitchen in it: around Minor Snow the temperature drops sharply but the ground is not yet frozen — prime time for pickling vegetables and curing meat, as the saying goes, "pickle greens at Minor Snow, cure meat at Major Snow"; in the south households dry fish and lay in winter vegetables, in the north farmers bank up the cellars and the byres against the cold and store fodder for the livestock; and the farming proverb says, "snow filling the sky at Minor Snow, a plenteous year to follow" — snow blanketing the wheat fields keeps them warm and freezes the pests, and the meltwater soaking into the ground feeds the next year's crop. Here too is the relation between the solar terms and the almanac: within a single day the four pillars of year, month, day and hour are fixed by the terms and by the day's own cycle — the month pillar is bounded by the "nodes," and all the days from Start of Winter through Minor Snow to Major Snow belong to the Hài month; while the favorable and avoided matters of each day are reckoned separately by the spirits. Today is the proof by example: Minor Snow arrives, the White Tiger holds the day, and still the favorable list swings wide on the strength of seven auspicious spirits — the terms set the great climate, the spirits set the small timetable, and the two do not interfere. In today's almanac: pickle at Minor Snow, let the Heavenly Horse hurry you on, and today favors movement and early hours; the rainbow hidden from sight, in the season of closing doors, see to what must be done before the door shuts.
