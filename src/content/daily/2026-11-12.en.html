On a Gēng Yín day, Gēng is yang metal — the metal of axes and halberds, stern and decisive; Yín is the wood of early spring's first stirrings, and within it hide jiǎ, bǐng and wù — jiǎ wood is Gēng metal's wealth, bǐng fire its officer, wù earth its seal: wealth, officer and seal all hidden in a single branch, a day of sitting right in the land of riches and rank. The day god in the twelve-spirits round is Tiānláo 天牢, the Heavenly Prison — a Black Path inauspicious day, governing fetters and entanglements, bonds and dim-lit troubles. Yet here lies the great reversal seldom seen of late: in the Hài month the Heavenly Virtue stands at yǐ, and yǐ combines with gēng — today's day stem is gēng metal itself, so the Heavenly Virtue's Union (Tiāndéhé 天德合) arrives in person: the Heavenly Virtue is the month's highest auspicious spirit, and on the day it attends every ill quietly dissolves, and the Black Path's malice falls away in great part. Ten auspicious spirits face five inauspicious ones: Tiāndéhé heads the list; Liùhé 六合 and Wǔhé 五合 arrive as a double harmony, and the day branch Yín forms the Six Harmony with the month branch Hài — heaven and earth in accord, joy filling the gate; Wǔfù 五富 governs abundance of wealth, and opening a market, trading and signing contracts all stand favorable; Jīntáng 金堂 keeps the house at peace, and moving in, relocating, setting the bed, construction, breaking ground and raising beams all stand wide open. Yesterday's riddle finds its answer today: Bùjiàng 不将 is expressly the marriage spirit — it stood plainly in yesterday's list, yet marriages were still avoided, because Death's Spirit pressed the line; today the Heavenly Virtue's Union takes its post, every ill dissolves and Death's Spirit withdraws, and Bùjiàng's especial blessing comes into use — marriages turn to head the fifteen favorable matters, a living lesson in the ebb and flow of luck. The inauspicious camp does not yield wholly: Yóuhuò 游祸, the wandering calamity, rules out prayer and offering — temple visits may wait today; Yín wood, fed by Hài water, is at the height of its vigor, so felling timber is avoided — to cut living wood in season is to go against the breath of growth; digging wells, building the stove and thanking the earth run near to draining the veins of the ground, and with Wǔxū 五虚 on the list, an empty day should not be emptied further. The Na Yin Pine and Cypress Wood holds its post — yesterday the Thunderbolt Fire hid its thunder in cloud, today the Pine and Cypress plants its roots on the cliff: five days past the Start of Winter, grasses and trees are shedding, and only pine and cedar keep green; marriages and contracts, moving in and breaking ground — all take today the image of evergreen through winter, of roots that hold. Clash the Monkey, the Sha lies North: Yín and Shēn stand opposed — keep clear of contention and partnership with Monkey people, and let northern journeys take a slower step.

With the Heavenly Virtue's Union in person, the Black Path malice of the Heavenly Prison is dissolved, and today the Tiger may set its hand to nearly all: opening a market, trading and signing contracts all stand favorable, and Wǔfù 五富 rides alongside with abundance of wealth — grand openings, signings and bargains, payments coming in — all in their hour; a fortune-seeking day rarely seen of late. The Six Harmony of Yín and Hài joins day branch to month branch, and the air of accord is at its fullest: for the unwed, confessions and betrothals stand favorable; for the wed, plan the household and sign its new covenants together. Tiānláo 天牢 still stands in the inauspicious list: read contract clauses word by word, give quarrels and courtroom matters a wide berth, keep everything in writing and on the straight path, and the image of the fetter dissolves of itself. In the body, Gēng metal answers the lungs and large intestine, and after the Start of Winter cold dryness deepens — moistening the lungs and generating fluids, taking less pungent and more sour, is the work at hand; the Yín hours are exactly when the Lung meridian keeps watch — sleep deep to nourish the breath of the lungs. The tiger roars upon the mountain and the person sleeps within the room, each in their place — do not lie awake in the Yín hours scrolling a glowing screen and scattering that breath.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday told how the Chǒu Ox cleaves the earth; today the Tiger of Yín takes its watch — so let us tell why tigers are painted upon doors. How can the tiger guard a house and drive off evil? The Classic of Mountains and Seas keeps a legend of Mount Dùshuò: amid the grey sea stands Mount Dùshuò, and on it grows a great peach tree, its boughs winding and coiling three thousand li; in the northeast gap between its branches lies the "Gate of Ghosts," the doorway through which the ten thousand ghosts come and go. Beneath the tree stand two divine men — one called Shéntú, one called Yùlǜ — who keep review of the ghosts; whenever they find one that harms, they bind it with rushes of reed and throw it to the tigers as food. When the Yellow Emperor heard of this, he raised peach-wood figures at gates and doorways, painted Shéntú, Yùlǜ and the tiger upon the doors, and hung reed rushes to ward off malevolent spirits — and this is the common wellspring of the door gods and the painted tiger of later ages. The Fengsu Tongyi 风俗通义, the Comprehensive Meaning of Customs, says it plainly: "The tiger is a creature of yang, chief of the hundred beasts, that can seize and grapple, blunt and break, and devour ghosts and phantoms." Ghosts belong to the yin; the tiger is yang at its utmost, and yang masters yin — a ghost that meets a tiger does not detour around it; it is eaten. And so the tiger is painted upon the door, and the hundred ghosts dare not cross the threshold. Why is the tiger paired with the Yín hour? At daybreak in the Yín hours, when the night is nearly spent and the sky not yet light, the tiger is at its fiercest, roaring and ranging; the ancients watched the tiger's nature so, and paired it with Yín. The tiger's majesty was borrowed everywhere in the old world: the tally that commands armies was cast in the tiger's form — the hǔfú 虎符, the Tiger Tally; tigers were painted on temple beams and palace rafters to overawe the hundred evils. And so to today's almanac: Tiānláo 天牢, the Heavenly Prison, keeps watch on the Black Path, and the dim image of the prison most dreads ghosts creeping at the gate; while the Heavenly Virtue's Union takes its post and every ill dissolves — one virtue to dissolve, one majesty to press down, just like the two tigers on the door of old: the virtue lives in the almanac, the majesty lives on the lintel; dissolve and press together, and evil keeps far off. Coming home tonight, glance at your own door — even without a painted tiger, keep a tiger in your heart, and all within the door is at peace.
