Today is Xīn Hài (辛亥) — the Heavenly Stem Xīn is a gentle, refined Metal and the Earthly Branch Hài is clear, flowing Water; Metal generates Water, a productive cycle, and the Nayin "hairpin gold" reinforces a tone of fine craftsmanship and elegance. The Yellow-Path deity Míngtáng rules the day, joined by eight auspicious spirits — Tiān'ēn, Yuè'ēn, Sānhé, Yīndé and others — giving the day a clearly favorable tone. The auspicious list is generous, nineteen items in all, most of them beginnings: engagement rites, moving into a new home, drawing up contracts, erecting pillars and raising beams, consecration and prayer — even installing machinery and unveiling a plaque are welcome. Only marriage, burial, breaking ground, grave repair and funeral rites are forbidden, and with the inauspicious spirit Chóngrì (重日) on duty, funeral matters and earthworks should wait. An engagement may well be made today; the wedding itself is better saved for another date.

Folklore

Today's Nayin is "Hairpin Gold" (Chāichuàn Jīn 钗钏金). Among the sixty Nayin of the ganzhi cycle, only Gēng Xū and Xīn Hài bear this name — metal refined by fire and worked into ornaments such as hairpins (钗) and bracelets (钏), worn at the head and the wrist. Tempered in the forge, this metal sheds all roughness; its nature is soft, its grain fine, symbolizing the beauty of adornment and a graceful bearing. Traditional fate-readers held that those whose charts carry Hairpin Gold tend to be elegant in manner, clever with their hands, and fond of beauty and tidiness; when a day itself carries this Nayin, it favors grooming, decorating one's home, and pleasant gatherings. Today is one of only two Hairpin Gold days in the cycle, and with the Joy God visiting the Southwest, it is a fine moment to refresh your home and reunite with friends long unseen.
