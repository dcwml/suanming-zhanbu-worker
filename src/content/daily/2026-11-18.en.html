Inauspicious Spirits

Marriages 嫁娶 · Entering a new home 入宅 · Opening a business 开市 · Setting the marriage bed 安床 · Breaking ground 破土 · Tending graves 修坟

On a Bǐng Shēn day, Bǐng is yang fire — the fire of the sun itself; Shēn is yang metal, metal at its crisp edge — and Bǐng fire seated on Shēn falls, in the twelve stages of long life, upon the stage of "ailing": like the sun at the Shēn hours, its light slanting west, bright still but already folding — hence today's Na Yin, Fire at the Foot of the Mountain 山下火: the light of a sun that has gone down behind the ridge, its last glow spread along the valley floor — light at the sky's edge, not overhead; a fire to gather in, not to let out. The day god arrives at Tiānxíng 天刑, the Heavenly Punisher — a Black Path spirit of ill: "punishment" means penal harm and legal strife, governing lawsuits, contention and obstruction — when the Punisher holds the day, no great undertaking should be raised, and marriages, entering a new home, opening a business and setting the bed are avoided on precisely this count. Yet punishment's qi has punishment's uses: in old almanacs "capturing" meant specifically seizing thieves and binding the unruly, and "hunting" meant taking game in the field — both deeds done by borrowing the very qi of punishment; that both stand favorable today is the Punisher's own day put to the Punisher's own work: using the sha by its nature. The auspicious spirits number three: Mǔcāng 母仓, the Mother's Granary, is the spirit of nourishing and increase — it governs the eating of grain and the quiet tending of all things, favorable for mending the body and keeping a still, clean house. Chúshén 除神, the Sweeping Spirit, governs clearing out the old and washing away the foul — bathing, sweeping the house and clearing misfortune all stand favorable on precisely its strength: when the Sweeper holds the day, washing off dust is washing off gloom. Míngwǔ 鸣吠, the Crying of Dogs, the sound of barking hounds and crowing roosters, ordinarily favors the matters of the grave and burial — but with five baleful ones pressing down today, breaking ground and tending graves still stand avoided: an auspicious spirit present yet its matter not taken is the almanac's standing rule of "let the ill outweigh the good" — when the sha are heavy, the lucky spirits give way. The five baleful: Tiāngāng 天罡, the Heavenly Gang star, governs brawls and lawsuits and sudden adversity. Jiéshà 劫煞, the Robber Spirit, governs plunder and loss — opening a business stands avoided on precisely this count; today the purse is for closing, not for scattering. Yuèhài 月害, the Month's Harm, is the most telling of the five: the month branch Hài and the day branch Shēn stand in exactly one of the six harms, the Shēn–Hài harm — "harm" means damage, discord among kin and injury between superior and subordinate; hence entering a new home and setting the bed stand avoided: household matters go slow, not fast. Wǔlí 五离, the Five Partings, is the family-name of the heaven-and-earth parting days such as Jiǎ Shēn and Yǐ Yǒu, governing separation and estrangement — marriages stand avoided on precisely this count: matters of wedlock flee the parting days. And Tiānxíng itself is the presiding god, already told. The favorable list numbers seven: bathing and sweeping the house — the two acts of cleansing, under the Sweeping Spirit. Clearing misfortune — dispelling the unlucky, washing out the inauspicious. Capturing and hunting — punishment and capture answering one another, the sha put to use. Sealing holes — stopping up cracks and burrows in wall and floor against rats and insects and creeping loss: under the Punisher, today is for stopping up, not for opening. Other matters not advised — nothing else counts today. The avoided list numbers six: marriages sit on the Five Partings; entering a home and setting the bed sit on the Month's Harm; opening a business sits on the Robber Spirit; breaking ground and tending graves sit under five baleful ones outshouting the Crying of Dogs. The Na Yin, Fire at the Foot of the Mountain: yesterday's Gold in the Sand said "tend it," and today's fire says "fold it in" — the fire has gone down the mountain, its flame gathered and its light drawn in: a day for winding up, for cleaning out, for stopping leaks; not for spreading out, not for launching, not for opening the doors. Clash the Tiger, the Sha lies South: Yín and Shēn stand opposed — keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership with Tiger people, and let southern journeys take a slower step. Minor Snow is in sight (Nov 22): in this Hài month the water qi swells by the day and the world begins its storing — the fire under the mountain is to be carried into the stove for winter, and the heart is no different: today is exactly the day to draw things in, take stock, and sweep the dust from the room.

Zodiac Fortune · Monkey 猴

Today's earthly branch is Shēn, which corresponds to the Monkey — making the Monkey the protagonist of the day: the Monkey of Shēn keeps its watch, but under the Heavenly Punisher's Black Path, so today's monkey does not climb for high branches — it crouches down to polish its hands and feet and take inventory of the storehouse: a day for hauling the net in, not casting it out. In career, favor closing and taking stock: sweeping the house and sealing holes stand favorable — tidying the desk and workspace, auditing old accounts and old objects, mending leaks in the workflow, filing what has scattered: all today's proper work. The image of "capturing" answers to business as the art of closing long-pending matters — chasing down overdue payments, reining in slackers, settling cases that have hung fire: the Punisher helps you draw the net. Only opening a business stands avoided with the Robber Spirit at its side: launching a new project, opening a new shop, signing an expansion — all take a slower step today; under penal qi, great launches meet twists — change the date if you can. In wealth, the Wealth God sits Southwest and the Robber Spirit stands on duty: it governs plunder and drain — large outlays, adding to investments, loans and guarantees all stand against today; the purse draws tight. Yet "capturing" favorable points down one lucky road: chasing debts, pressing payment, cleaning out bad accounts — the hand moves smoothest today; the work is not seeking new money but gathering back the old money that has scattered. In love, marriages and setting the bed stand avoided with the Five Partings and the Month's Harm both at the gate: proposals and registrations take a pause today, and between partners the risk is quarrels opening a "parting" crack — the Shēn–Hài harm most of all forbids digging up old grievances and raking old sores; one rake and the harmony bleeds. Dates and small gatherings are not forbidden — only leave the "big talk" alone today; plain company is safe company. In the body, bathing and clearing misfortune stand favorable: a long hot wash to wring out the fatigue; tending old ailments, physiotherapy and massage all run smooth today. The Mother's Granary governs nourishing — eat warm and build up: the Hài month chills on, so add layers, warm the belly, and lie early to feed the yin. Shēn hours answer the meridians' flow: for those long seated, three to five in the afternoon, rise and stretch the shoulders and back. Clash the Tiger, Sha South: with Tiger people today, keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership; southern routes take a slower step.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Goat of Wèi took the step; today the Monkey of Shēn comes on stage — first, the graph Shēn. To the modern eye Shēn is merely the ninth earthly branch, but it is in truth the ancestor of several very old shapes: in the oracle-bone script, Shēn is drawn as a forked bolt of lightning zigzagging across the sky, barbed at both ends — it is the original graph of 電 "lightning/electricity" and the first writing of 神 "spirit": the Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字 says, "Shēn means spirit. In the seventh month the yin qi takes form, binding and stretching itself." The Shēn of the Shēn hours and the shén of the spirits were once one word: the ancients, watching lightning split the sky from no source they could name, took it for the showing of the spirits; later a "spirit altar" radical 示 was added to write spirit alone, and a "person" radical to make 伸, the "stretch" of flexing and extending — out of one lightning bolt branched spirit, lightning, and stretch. Why does the Monkey pair with Shēn? One account: at the Shēn hours (three to five in the afternoon), as the sun drops behind the western hills, gibbons and monkeys cry their sharpest — the ancients, watching the monkey most restless at Shēn, gave it that slot; "apes crying at the Shēn hours" is a standing image of the old poets. Another account: the monkey's quick and mutable nature answers the crisp, cutting, change-bringing qi of Shēn metal. Both accounts stand, and both are the same method — watching the creatures, and giving each hour to the one that fits it best. Then a tale that belongs to the monkey above all — "three at dawn, four at dusk" 朝三暮四: from the Zhuangzi, chapter on the leveling of things. An old keeper of monkeys, portioning out acorns, said to them: "Three in the morning and four in the evening." The whole troop raged. "Very well," said he, "four in the morning and three in the evening." And the whole troop delighted. Zhuāngzī's verdict: "the name and the substance never suffered loss, yet joy and anger were set in motion by them" — the acorn count never changed, yet the monkeys raged and rejoiced over nothing but the order of the words. Over two thousand years the phrase has been worn down to mean "fickle and shifting," but its first edge was deeper: it mocks the mind that lets its feelings be led by names and appearances. Today, with the Heavenly Punisher holding the day and the Robber Spirit at hand, nothing is more to be guarded against than just such being-moved-by-names — opening a business stands avoided, yet seeing another's shop open stirs the itch to follow; the Robber Spirit governs plunder, and to lunge at a hollow label or a flashy margin is today's "three at dawn, four at dusk": the total unchanged, the heart already storming, and in the storm the loss slips in. The monkey's cleverness cuts both ways: spent on inventory and net-hauling and closing things out, it is a gift; spent on wrangling over order and precedence, it is a leak. Today's monkey learns from the keeper: look at the total, not the sequence — and the day is calm.
