Zodiac Fortune · Goat 羊

Today's earthly branch is Wèi, which corresponds to the Goat — making the Goat the zodiac protagonist of the day. The Goat presides under the direct light of Heavenly Virtue on the Yellow Path, ringed by Mother's Granary, Vital Breath and Heavenly Granary: the Granaries govern nurture and reserve, Vital Breath radiates fresh vigor — Goat people should gather with friends and kin and pursue study today; reunions over a small table and lessons resumed are both ways of tending one's fortune. With Dīng fire riding the stem and wood-and-fire rooted in Wèi, expression and action both flow — yet with the Five Voids and Nine Emptinesses at the door, wealth is best stored rather than scattered: the paired granaries are the footnote, and taking stock, budgeting and setting savings aside suit the day, while openings, signings and large flows of money should wait. In love, gatherings with kin and friends stand favored — a blind date or a quiet drink both serve; and with Hidden Virtue presiding, a kindness done in secret adds threefold to the blessing. In health, the Heavenly River Water Na Yin brings moisture, but autumn dryness deepens — drink more water, moisten the lungs and retire early to gather the spirit. The day clashes with the Ox and the Sha lies West: those born in the Year of the Ox should keep a low profile in all matters, avoid journeys toward the West, and refrain from quarrels with Goats.

Metaphysics Insight

Today's presiding spirit is Tiān Dé, Heavenly Virtue — the sixth figure in the rotation of the Twelve Officers. Treasured Light (Bǎo Guāng) in yesterday's list is that same seat under its other name, so the "Bǎo Guāng" in today's auspicious column shining from the very station of the presiding spirit is no coincidence. Heavenly Virtue is the grace of heaven itself, and the old almanac masters reckon it the supreme auspicious spirit of release from misfortune: on its days every malevolent spirit must withdraw and lie hidden, and even should the White Tiger or the Heavenly Punisher of the Black Path appear, their sting is softened by a third. Today's auspicious column also names Yīn Dé, Hidden Virtue — Heavenly Virtue above is the grace heaven bestows; Hidden Virtue in the dark is a field of blessing each person sows for themselves. As the Huáinánzǐ says, "those with hidden virtue will surely receive an open reward." The best-loved tale of hidden virtue is that of Sun Shu'ao and the serpent: it was held in Chu that whoever saw a two-headed snake would die; young Sun Shu'ao, meeting one on the road, killed and buried it lest someone else should see it and perish, then went home weeping to tell his mother — who told him, "those with hidden virtue, heaven repays with fortune." He grew up to become Chief Minister of Chu indeed. Today's favored column — moving coffins, opening graves, removing and donning mourning garments — is given over to yin matters, and on a Hidden Virtue day such matters are rightly tended: to see off the departed with solemn care, to cherish memory of what has passed, is itself another field of blessing laid up for oneself.
