Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Horse of Wǔ raised its hooves; today the Goat of Wèi takes the step — so let us speak of this goat. First, the root sense of the graph Wèi: the Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字 says: "Wèi means 'taste' (wèi 味). In the sixth month the flavors ripen." It adds: "Wood grows old at Wèi — the graph pictures wood with layered boughs and leaves." Wood at Wèi reaches its full age: boughs overlapping, fruit first set, and flavor issuing from it — in the year, Wèi is the sixth month; in the day, Wèi is the early-afternoon hour (one to three), both the hours when things stand ripe in their flavor and begin to fold from fullness toward storing. The Goat is paired with Wèi for just this gentleness — mild, knowing the taste of things, mindful of kindness and observant of rite: the Augmented Writings 增广贤文 say, "The goat has the grace of kneeling to suckle; the crow has the duty of feeding its parents in return" — the lamb folding its forelegs to take its mother's milk is a filial act written into the beast itself. Then the kinship of 羊 and 祥: the Shuowen states it flat — "羊, 祥也: the Goat means Auspiciousness." In old writing, "auspicious" (吉祥) was often set down as "lucky-goat" (吉羊), and on the eave-tiles and bronzes of the Han dynasty the three characters 大吉羊 — "Great Lucky Goat" — appear again and again: the ancients held the goat a beast of good omen; goat and auspicious were one word at the root. Today the weightiest line in the auspicious roster is Sānhé 三合, the Three Harmonies: Zǐ–Chén–Shēn pool the Water frame, Yín–Wǔ–Xū the Fire frame, Sì–Yǒu–Chǒu the Metal frame, Hài–Mǎo–Wèi the Wood frame — a harmony takes the long-life, the imperial-flourishing and the grave-vault branches of one element and joins them: Hài is wood's long life (water moistening the root), Mǎo wood's imperial flourishing (leaf at its fullest), Wèi wood's grave-vault (grown old, returned to root) — meeting, the three make wood a complete frame. Today the month's Hài and the day's Wèi already hold two of the three, missing only Mǎo — like a game of weiqi with two stones placed and the lines already alive: hence the Three Harmonies on duty, governing many hearts as one and plans that come easy. Last, the tale of the Five Goats Bearing Grain: in the age of Zhōu, it is said, the land around Guǎngzhōu (of old called Chǔtíng) suffered famine year upon year — until one day five immortals descended, robed in five colors and riding five goats of five hues, each goat's mouth holding a stalk of fine grain six-eared, blessing the land never to know hunger again; the immortals did not stay, and the goats turned to stone to remain among the people — from that stalk comes Guǎngzhōu's name, the City of Goats, the Five Rams. Planting and sowing stand favorable today, and the legend answers exactly: the Five Goats bearing grain were themselves a prayer of "granting the tiller the good seed" — to sow and set roots today is to take up that stalk from the five immortal goats, and carry it on.
