Today is Yǐ Hài 乙亥 — the stem Yǐ is yin wood, the wood of flowers and vines, supple and generative; the branch Hài is yin water, the water of rivers and deep pools, profound and nurturing. Water gives birth to wood, branch nourishing stem, so Hài water nourishes Yǐ wood like sweet rain upon a seedling, its vitality internal and enduring. Its Nayin is Mountain-Top Fire, fire upon the mountain, its flame small yet persistent, carrying the imagery of civilization gradually revealing itself and the torch passed on unbroken. The presiding deity is Gōuchén 勾陈, an inauspicious spirit of the Black Path, governing entanglement, obstruction and delay — not inherently auspicious; yet two auspicious spirits attend together — Xiāngrì governs affinity and closeness, harmonious union, while Pǔhù governs universal protection, comprehensive and without omission. With both present, part of Gōuchén's stagnation may be dissolved. Six matters are favored: sacrifice, haircutting, installing a stove, bathing, decorating walls and leveling roads — all are matters of cleansing, ordering, repairing the dwelling and inward self-cultivation, fitting the virtue of Pǔhù. Eight matters are banned: marriage, planting, praying for blessings, building bridges, burial, installing gates, felling trees and making ridge beams — for Gōuchén governs obstruction and entanglement, so marriage and bridge-building, which require smooth progress, are unfit; Chóngrì governs repetition and waste, Yóuhuò wandering calamity, so planting and burial, which concern long-term foundations, are unfit; and with Sǐshén and Tiāngāng both present, installing gates and raising ridge beams are especially to be avoided. Six days have passed since End of Heat, autumn metal rising while water qi begins to condense; on a Yǐ-Hài day water and wood give birth to each other, so borrow the union of Xiāngrì and the shelter of Pǔhù to overcome movement with stillness and cultivate the inner to manage the outer.

Zodiac Fortune · Pig

Today's branch is Hài (亥), which corresponds to the Pig — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Pig year sit squarely on the day's own branch — Pig meeting a Hài day is like a fish in water, stem and branch sharing the same qi, naturally homeward and at peace. Yet the presiding deity Gōuchén is inauspicious, with six inauspicious spirits encircling, so today one must act by conserving rather than attacking, by stillness rather than movement. At work, Gōuchén governs entanglement and delay — major ventures should not be rushed, yet matters of repair and maintenance such as decorating walls and leveling roads may advance steadily; sacrifice, bathing and haircutting stand among the favorable matters, so Pigs should favor clearing the mind, calming the spirit and grooming oneself, borrowing the auspiciousness of Pǔhù to wash away vexation. In wealth, the Wealth God stands in the Northeast, so if there are accounts to settle or things to store one may favor the northeast, yet planting and felling trees, which generate wealth, are forbidden. In weddings, marriage stands among the bans — Gōuchén governs delay, and marriage requires smoothness, so today is unfit for discussing or sealing betrothal. Mind, though, that today clashes with the Snake and the Sha lies West: yield a step to those born in a Snake year, and travel westward should be cautious; the Joy God stands in the Northwest, so for quiet meditation one may sit facing northwest; the Fortune God stands in the Southwest, so prayers for the household may face southwest. In health, on a Yǐ-Hài day water and wood give birth to each other, and Hài water governs the kidneys and bladder — Pigs should guard against weakened kidney qi and soreness in the lower back and knees, favoring warm-water foot baths and early sleep to nourish the store.

Folklore

Today's day pillar is Yǐ Hài 乙亥, so today's reading is about the meaning of this stem-branch pair. The heavenly stem Yǐ belongs to yin wood, symbolizing flowers and vines — not the towering uprightness of Jiǎ wood, but the supple, climbing virtue of bending with the wind; Yǐ wood likes the moisture of Guǐ water and the warmth of Bǐng fire, and dislikes being chopped by Gēng metal. The earthly branch Hài belongs to yin water, symbolizing rivers and deep pools — not the rushing current of Zǐ water, but the profound, nurturing depth where dragons lie hidden; within Hài water are hidden Rén water and Jiǎ wood, so Hài is the place where wood is born, water and wood giving birth to each other in an unbroken stream. The Yǐ-Hài combination, with the stem Yǐ wood sitting upon the branch Hài water, is an image of "Seal of Favor" — the Rén water seal within Hài gives birth to Yǐ wood, like a mother nurturing her child, like a spring irrigating a seedling, endless generation. In the study of fate, those born on a Yǐ-Hài day tend to have compassionate hearts, are good at understanding others, possess literary talent and prefer tranquility, yet are also prone to indecision and dependence. The Nayin Mountain-Top Fire adds another layer of imagery: Yǐ wood is like kindling, Hài water like a deep pool — kindling cast into a pool grows wet, yet fire born upon a mountain shines clear; the fire on the mountain is not a blazing wildfire but a long-burning lamp, governing the transmission of civilization and the unbroken passing of the torch. The ancients said, "Yǐ-Hài Mountain-Top Fire — civilization finds its rest," meaning that although this fire is small, it can illumine the night and pass on the flame, favoring culture, education, transmission and inward cultivation rather than outward contention.
