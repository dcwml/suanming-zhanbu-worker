Today is Rén Zǐ (壬子) — both the stem Rén and the branch Zǐ are Water, so the day's Water is overwhelmingly strong; the Nayin "Mulberry Wood" does draw off some of that water to nourish wood, lending a touch of flexibility, but it cannot turn the day's overall tide. The ruling deity Tiānxíng (天刑) is inauspicious, and only four auspicious spirits stand on duty — Tiān'ēn, Jīntáng, Jiěshén and Míngféiduì — against no fewer than eleven inauspicious ones: Yuèhài, Dàbài and Xiánchí among them, plus the notorious Sìfèi (四废, "Four Wastes"), for a summer Rén Zǐ is itself one of the Four Waste days, a time of depleted vitality when affairs tend to falter. Thus every auspicious item belongs to the family of closing and concluding: bathing and haircutting to cleanse oneself, and encoffining, moving the coffin, breaking ground, grave-opening and burial for attending to the departed; festive and building matters — marriage, moving in, installing a stove, raising beams, breaking ground — are all forbidden. Tomorrow brings Start of Autumn and rising Metal; today is best spent in quiet rest, settling old matters, rather than launching anything new.

Zodiac Fortune · Rat

Today's branch is Zǐ (子), which corresponds to the Rat — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Rat year share the day's branch, and both Rén Water and Zǐ Water are of their own kind, which might promise a boost from the day's energy; yet with Tiānxíng ruling inauspiciously and the Four Wastes draining the day, even a native on home ground should hold rather than push. Career-wise, breaking ground, raising beams, moving in and installing a stove are all taboo, so relocation, purchasing property and any construction are firmly out of the question; fortunately breaking ground for burial and interment sit on the auspicious side, so if there is old business to settle or loose ends to tie up, it may be done in keeping with the times. Financially, though the Wealth God dwells in the South, Dàbài and Xiánchí arrive alongside, and money can slip through one's fingers — seek wealth steadily and avoid any gamble. Mind the clash "Horse, South" — steer clear of disputes with those born in a Horse year and of long journeys toward the south; and with the Four Wastes sapping the day's strength, the worst mistake today is to force one's way ahead. Better to rest quietly and wait until after Start of Autumn, when Metal turns favorable, to press forward.

Folklore

Today's Nayin is "Mulberry Wood" (Sāngzhè Mù 桑柘木). Among the sixty Nayin of the ganzhi cycle, only Rén Zǐ and Guǐ Chǒu bear this name — "sāng" is the mulberry, whose leaves feed the silkworm, and "zhè" is the cudrania, whose dense wood makes fine bows and yields a yellow dye. Neither is a towering pillar-timber; both are humble, everyday trees intertwined with the livelihood of common folk. Growing where water is abundant, with water in excess and wood yielding soft, their nature is tough, enduring and unassuming. Traditional fate-readers held that those whose charts carry Mulberry Wood tend to be hardworking and down-to-earth, able to bend and rebound — not conspicuous, yet of genuine use, and best suited to steady, unhurried progress. Today, as this wood holds sway amid the depletion of the Four Wastes, its image of "flexible perseverance" is precisely the day's guiding principle — rather than forcing new ground, one does better to rest, mend and gather strength. Tomorrow is Start of Autumn; when the metal wind arrives, it will not be too late to rise again.
