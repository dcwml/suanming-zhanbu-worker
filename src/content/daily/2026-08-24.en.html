Today is Gēng Wǔ 庚午 — the stem Gēng is yang metal, the metal of axe and sword, firm and keen; the branch Wǔ is yang fire, the fire of high noon at its zenith. Fire restrains metal, branch restraining stem, yet Gēng metal fears no restraint, for only by passing through fire is it forged into a vessel — and its Nayin is Roadside Earth, the plain and unadorned earth along the road, quietly sustaining all things without ostentation. The presiding deity is Báihǔ 白虎, a Black Path day, White Tiger governing harm, mourning and fierce violence, standing among the inauspicious spirits as well — funeral matters, visits to the sick, and anything of bloodshed or strife are best avoided today. Yet the auspicious spirits temper it: Tiānmǎ 天马 on duty governs movement and swift progress, Shíyáng 时阳 and Shēngqì 生气 preside over vitality and growth, Yùyǔ 玉宇 shines as a vault of treasure, and Míngfèi 鸣吠 sounds a good omen — five auspicious spirits attend the day together, enough to soften the White Tiger's fierceness. Thirteen matters are therefore favored: marriage, accepting betrothal gifts and pledging betrothal for the great rites; consecration and sacrifice for sincerity; travel and haircut for daily affairs; breaking ground and setting the bed for the household; releasing water, digging channels, planting and welcoming people for one's livelihood, all may proceed. Ten matters are banned: moving into a house, raising beams and roofing forbid construction and relocation; encoffining, burial and visiting the sick forbid funeral and illness matters; installing a stove, installing gates, acquiring livestock and felling trees should all wait for another day. Yesterday was End of Heat itself — the summer heat has ceased and Metal qi gathers strength, and on this Gēng metal day the metal qi is especially abundant — act as metal does: advance sharply when advance is right, and store up with measure.

Zodiac Fortune · Horse

Today's branch is Wǔ (午), which corresponds to the Horse — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Horse year sit squarely on the day's own branch, and moreover the auspicious spirit Tiānmǎ 天马, the Heavenly Horse, is on duty — Horse meeting Horse day, movement and stillness alike in their season, a day good both for pressing forward and for setting out. At work, the Heavenly Horse governs movement and change, and travel stands on the favorable list — business trips, inspections, taking up a new post, going out to seek opportunities all fit today; anyone whose affairs have long stalled should set things moving now, for a horse covers a thousand li and motion brings success. Shēngqì and Shíyáng together govern growth, so starting a new plan or opening a new venture also moves with the times. In wealth, the Wealth God stands in the due East where the sun rises — favor the east when seeking money and doing business, and digging channels and releasing water govern irrigation and flow, so those in trade will do well to keep their cash moving and uncorrupted. In love, marriage heads the favorable list and both accepting betrothal gifts and pledging betrothal are favored — Horses with marriage in mind may settle the engagement today and fix the ceremony for an auspicious day; only because the presiding deity White Tiger is fierce, rites should be solemn and orderly rather than rushed. Mind, though, that today clashes with the Rat and the Sha lies North: yield a step to those born in a Rat year, keep important matters away from the due north, and with moving into a house, burial and encoffining all among the bans and visits to the sick especially forbidden, a phone call of concern is better than going to see the sick in person. In health, the Horse belongs to fire and yesterday was End of Heat — the fire qi retreats and the metal qi arrives; at this turn of the season Horses should temper the fire within — sleep early and rise early, hold back temper and impatience, moisten the lungs and care for the skin, that metal and fire temper each other and body and mind rest at ease.

Folklore

The presiding deity today is Báihǔ 白虎, the White Tiger, which also stands among the inauspicious spirits — so today's reading is about this star itself. When the ancients observed the heavens, they divided the twenty-eight mansions along the ecliptic into four groups of seven, traced each into the figure of a spirit beast, and so gained the Four Symbols — the Azure Dragon of the East, the White Tiger of the West, the Vermilion Bird of the South and the Black Tortoise of the North. The western seven mansions — Kuí 奎, Lóu 娄, Wèi 胃, Mǎo 昴, Bì 毕, Zī 觜 and Shēn 参 — form the shape of a tiger, hence the name White Tiger; in the five elements it belongs to Metal, and in the four seasons it presides over autumn. The qi of autumn is severe, drawing all things to ripeness and harvest, so the White Tiger also governs armies, weapons and punishment — in old times campaigns were launched in autumn and capital sentences carried out after the autumn assizes, the "autumn hunts" and "autumn executions" of the records all taking their meaning from this seasonal severity. In the shen-sha system of almanac calculation, the White Tiger descends to preside over days: on a White Tiger day harm, bloodshed and mourning matters are inauspicious — visiting the sick and attending funerals are taboo, quarrelling is taboo, and hazardous journeys are most taboo of all. Yet the White Tiger is not a purely malign spirit: its nature is solemn and its function severe, and used in season it becomes authority itself — the bronze tiger tallies by which ancient generals mustered their troops took their name from it, so that the White Tiger is at once a fierce star and the emblem of military might. The way to meet a White Tiger day is therefore to avoid and to transform rather than to confront: keep away from its sharp edge by having nothing to do with funerals, sick visits or disputes, and enlist the auspicious spirits to dissolve its fierceness — today's Tiānmǎ governs movement and Shēngqì governs growth, and with auspicious spirits in support the inauspicious qi clears of itself. Yesterday was End of Heat, and the autumn qi has now arrived — the White Tiger is precisely the spirit of the season, and its lesson for today is plain: advance sharply where advance is right, and hold still where stillness is due.
