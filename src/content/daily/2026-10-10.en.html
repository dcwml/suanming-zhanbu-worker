Metaphysics Insight

Today is the first day of the ninth lunar month — a new moon, a fresh cycle — and the brightest mark on today's almanac is Míng Táng 明堂, the Bright Hall, one of the twelve deities that take turns ruling the days and one of the auspicious "Yellow Path" spirits. The old phrase "Yellow Path lucky day" has astronomical roots: the ancients traced the sun's apparent yearly course across the sky — the ecliptic — then divided that belt into twelve stations and assigned a deity to each, rotating day by day. Azure Dragon, Bright Hall, Golden Chest, Heavenly Virtue, Jade Hall and Commander are the six Yellow Path spirits that preside over good fortune; Heavenly Punishment, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Celestial Prison, Black Tortoise and Hooked Chen form the six Black Path spirits of ill omen. The Bright Hall holds an honored place among the twelve: in ancient times it was the hall where the Son of Heaven governed the realm and received his lords, and date-selection takes from it the image of "light, uprightness and all things proceeding smoothly" — hence a Bright Hall day favors marriages, family reunions, collecting wealth and making agreements, but dislikes construction and earthwork — note the avoided column today, with roof beams, doors and stoves plainly listed, matching the old maxim that the Bright Hall is unkind to building work. When reading the almanac, remember this rhyme: "Azure Dragon, Bright Hall and Golden Chest; Heavenly Virtue, Jade Hall and Commander bring luck" — whenever one of these six rules the day, it is a Yellow Path lucky day. Yet even a Yellow Path day is not good for everything: the favored and avoided lists must still be weighed, together with one's own zodiac clash — today the Bright Hall shines, yet travel and burial are avoided and those born in the Year of the Pig should still mind the clash. That is the essence of date-selection: choose the day for the task, and the hour for the person.
