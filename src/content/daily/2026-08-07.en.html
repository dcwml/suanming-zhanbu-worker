Zodiac Fortune · Ox

Today's branch is Chǒu (丑), which corresponds to the Ox — today's featured zodiac. Those born in an Ox year share the day's branch, with Míngtáng 明堂 ruling auspiciously and the auspicious spirits Tiāndé 天德 and Tiān'ēn 天恩 on duty, so the underlying tone is steady and favorable; yet today's auspicious list consists mostly of sacrifice and funeral matters of closure, capped with "all other matters best avoided", while the inauspicious column reads "nothing is suitable" — so even as the day's featured native, Oxen should hold rather than push. Career-wise, launching new projects, signing major contracts or opening for business is all out of place today; tying up loose ends, sorting papers and clearing accounts, on the contrary, fits the day perfectly. Financially, the Wealth God dwells due South, but Xiǎohào 小耗 governs small losses — keep the purse strings tight and avoid large investments or expansion. In relationships, mind the clash "Sheep, Sha East" — steer clear of disputes with those born in a Sheep year and of long journeys toward the east, and with Guījì 归忌 disfavoring travel, long trips are best postponed. Start of Autumn has arrived: Metal begins to rise, and Chǒu earth feeds it at some cost of its own qi, so Oxen should conserve their strength — early to bed, early to rise, and greet the autumn gently.

Folklore

Today is Start of Autumn (Lìqiū 立秋) itself. The twenty-four solar terms are the true backbone of the Chinese calendar — lunar months are bounded by the terms, and Start of Autumn is the first term of autumn and the beginning of the Shēn month, which is why today's month pillar shifts from yesterday's Yǐ Wèi 乙未 to Bǐng Shēn 丙申: in the Four Pillars the day pillar runs on its sixty-step cycle, while the month pillar always changes at the solar terms. The character "autumn" (秋) originally means "the ripening of grain"; from this day yin qi begins to grow and Metal rises in strength, as all things turn from growing to gathering in. Folk custom marks the day with tiē qiūbiāo 贴秋膘 — "fattening up for autumn", eating heartily to recover from the summer heat and lay in strength for the colder months — and with yǎo qiū 咬秋, "biting the autumn", eating watermelon on this day to banish lingering summer heat. In the almanac, Start of Autumn day itself often carries the air of a hinge — the old qi spent, the new not yet in charge — which is precisely why today's list reads "nothing is suitable" and its auspicious items are all matters of closure: the almanac's own way of writing about a change of seasons.
