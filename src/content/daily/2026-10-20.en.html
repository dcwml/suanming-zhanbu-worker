Yesterday Bǐng Yín opened the furnace and raised the flame; today Dīng Mǎo lets the fire settle — Dīng is the flame of lamp and hearth-core, and Mǎo is standing timber for fuel, wood feeding fire without pause, so the Na Yin remains Fire in the Furnace: the sixty Jiazi turn their Na Yin two days at a time, and in this one furnace yesterday was the lighting, today is the tending. Gōuchén holds the Black Path over the day: it presides over land and earth and over lingering, dragging affairs, so matters close to the home and its soil — moving in, relocating, digging a well, building the stove — all stand set aside. Yet the auspicious column carries a rare vein of union: the day branch Mǎo and the month branch Xū form the Liuhe six-harmony of Mǎo-Xū, and the Six Harmony spirit attends because of it, joined by the Five Union — two unions in a single day, presiding over affinity and the joy of marriage. Shèngxīn, the Sagely Heart, favors worship and prayer; Tiān'ēn, Heaven's Grace, favors kindness conferred and good done openly. And the most telling entry sits in the inauspicious column: Xiánchí, the Peach Blossom — the year branch Wǔ belongs to the Yin-Wu-Xu fire trine, and wherever the trine sees Mǎo, there stands the Peach Blossom; today is a Dīng Mǎo day, the very ground of its season. With the Peach Blossom at the door and "marriage rites" at the head of the favorable column, the affection is being put to its right use: the Two Unions stand as matchmakers, the Sagely Heart as the blessing, and a true match may be made. But Xiánchí is also called the "Ruining Spirit" — let the feeling run loose and it turns to wine, romance and drained wealth, hence the Little Devourer, the Great Ruin and the Five Void following close behind, with opening a business and transactions under avoidance: let love be used rightly, and keep a firm hand on money. Míngfèiduì attends day after day — encoffining and breaking earth stand open still, and the work of farewell runs unbroken from yesterday into today.

Rabbit people sit on peach-blossom ground today: the year branch Wǔ raises the Yin-Wu-Xu fire trine, and wherever the trine sees Mǎo stands Xiánchí, so both romance and rapport run high. The Six Harmony and the Five Union attend together, and the day branch Mǎo quietly joins the month branch Xū — proposals, engagements, reunions and mended friendships all find their hour, and for the unmarried, a true match may well appear. Yet the Peach Blossom's feeling turns on a single thought: if you are already paired, keep your measure — what wine and flirtation drain is exactly the wealth the day guards least. The Little Devourer, the Great Ruin and the Five Void stand about, opening a business and transactions are both avoided: hold off on investments, contracts and large purchases, and mind your belongings when out. Gōuchén presides over dragging delays: matters in hand may stall and circle, and urgency buys nothing — steady, stepwise progress is the surest pace; leave moving house for another day. The day clashes with the Rooster and the Sha lies West: keep clear of contention and major ventures with Rooster people, and let long journeys west wait. Marriage rites, travel, repairs and setting the bed all stand open in the favorable column — spend the Peach Blossom's bright side on affection and harmony, and today is a good day.

Metaphysics Insight

Today's inauspicious column holds the best-named star in the whole almanac — Xiánchí, the Pool of Xianchi. The name comes from myth: the Huainanzi says "the sun rises from Yang Valley and bathes in the Pool of Xianchi" — before each day's rising, the sun first bathes in Xianchi, the bathing pool of heaven. The spirit is derived from that word "bathe": the Yin-Wu-Xu fire trine is born at Yín, and by the time the cycle reaches Mǎo it stands at the twelfth-stage ground of "Bathing" — fullness washing itself, like one newly bathed. Hence the four-line formula: Yin-Wu-Xu sees Mǎo, Shēn-Zǐ-Chén sees Yǒu, Sì-Yǒu-Chǒu sees Wǔ, Hài-Mǎo-Wèi sees Zǐ — and the Pool is thus fixed upon the four cardinal branches Zǐ, Wǔ, Mǎo, Yǒu, where the qi runs pure and concentrated. That is why it bears a second name: the Peach Blossom. The Peach Blossom presides over romance, charm and rapport — yet in the art of fate reading it is also called the "Ruining Spirit," for to bathe is to stand near water, and where feeling gathers, its right use is marriage and human warmth, while its excess becomes the drag of wine, pleasure and name. So the old readers, meeting a Peach Blossom in a chart, asked first whether it stood inside the wall or outside: in the year or month, it is the blossom within the wall, the tenderness of husband and wife; on the day or hour, the blossom outside the wall, within anyone's reach. Today the Peach Blossom keeps its season, and the favorable column holds marriage rites and prayers — the almanac's way of spending the blossom where it belongs; and with the Little Devourer and the Great Ruin listed close beside it, the old book adds its one-line reminder: let love be faithful, and keep your purse strings drawn.
