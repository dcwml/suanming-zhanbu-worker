On a Jiǎ Chén day the Jiǎ wood sits upon Chén earth — damp earth that nourishes the root, the wood's qi quietly fed — and the day's branch Chén forms the Chén–Yǒu Six Harmony with the month's Yǒu: yesterday's clashing qi of Mǎo against Yǒu turns today into harmonized qi, the pattern passing from break to union. The Six Harmony governs union and Bù Jiāng serves weddings above all, so marriage, betrothal gifts and betrothal agreements head the favored column; the Mother's Granary tends the household, and moving into a house, relocating and setting the bed are all well met. Though the Heavenly Punisher of the Black Path holds the day, with five auspicious spirits in attendance its harm is held in check — yet it presides over law and discipline, so opening a business and opening the granary stand in the taboos and the moving of funds may wait; burial, too, is avoided, for a day of union is no friend to the netherworld.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday we spoke of the six clashes; today let us look at their mirror — the Six Harmonies. The earthly branches form six paired harmonies: Zǐ–Chǒu, Yín–Hài, Mǎo–Xū, Chén–Yǒu, Sì–Shēn, Wǔ–Wèi. A clash sets two branches opposed in direction and at war in element, like armies arrayed against each other; a harmony is yin and yáng delighting in one another, qi drawing toward qi — the ancients took for its image the sun and moon conjoined: hé, to harmonize. Today's day branch Chén and month branch Yǒu are precisely one of these pairs: Chén and Yǒu unite into metal. Yesterday Mǎo clashing Yǒu made the Month Breaker; today Chén harmonizing with Yǒu makes the Six Harmony — beneath the same month built on Yǒu, clash one day, union the next, and between break and union the almanac's verdicts stand poles apart: yesterday "avoid all other matters," today marriage, moving into a house and relocating all troop into the favored column. In the art of date-selection a Six Harmony day presides over union of every kind — weddings, betrothals, sworn brotherhood, peace talks — all drawing on its qi. The favored column carries another, rarer spirit as well: Bù Jiāng, "No Bridal Escort" — the Celestial Treasure Almanac says, "Bù Jiāng is a day of firm and yielding, yin and yáng fitting one another; on such a day one may marry" — free of the escort's harm, yin and yáng in their proper seats, it is the first-rank nuptial spirit of the old almanac. With the Six Harmonies understood, the heading of today's favored column — marriage, betrothal gifts, betrothal agreements — shows itself no polite stock phrase, but a day of union deduced by a thousand years of calendrical reckoning beneath the arrangement of stems and branches.
