The Na Yin is Lampshade Fire 覆灯火 — Jiǎ Chén and Yǐ Sì share it: the fire of a lamp, its flame held inward, lighting a room without running to wildfire; yesterday's foil of gold upon a vessel becomes today a single lamp before the hall — its light is not vast, but it lasts. The day god arrives at Sīmìng 司命, a Yellow Path auspicious day: after two days under the Black Path — Tiānláo 天牢, then Xuánwǔ 玄武 — the tone turns from dark to bright. Five auspicious spirits stand in list, and the heaviest is Yuèdé 月德 at the head — in a Hài–Mǎo–Wèi month the Monthly Virtue sits on Jiǎ, and today's day stem sits squarely upon it: where the Monthly Virtue stands, the baleful spirits fall back — hence with three inauspicious ones in list, the avoided column holds only six matters while the favorable holds sixteen, opening even to burial and the coffin — the Yellow Path plus the Monthly Virtue is a combination that holds the hall. Metaphysics Insight

For days the talk has been of the branches' alliances; today changes the subject — to the two characters at the top of the almanac, Sīmìng 司命, and the rota they belong to: the Yellow and Black Paths. The twelve day-gods of date selection take turns holding the day: Qīnglóng 青龙, Míngtáng 明堂, Jīnkuì 金匮, Tiāndé 天德 (also styled Bǎoguāng 宝光), Yùtáng 玉堂 and Sīmìng 司命 — six of the Yellow Path, governing luck; Tiānxíng 天刑, Zhūquè 朱雀, Báihǔ 白虎, Tiānláo 天牢, Xuánwǔ 玄武 and Gōuchén 勾陈 — six of the Black Path, governing harm. Twelve gods rotating one to a day in fixed order, six bright and six dark in alternation — which is why the almanac's lucky days and unlucky days trade places every few days. Readers have seen it these past days: Tiānláo the day before yesterday, Xuánwǔ yesterday — two Black Path days pressing down; today the rota reaches Sīmìng, and the Yellow Path takes the stage at the turn. "Yellow Path" is at bottom an astronomical word: the sun's apparent track across the sky is the ecliptic, and the Book of Han's Treatise on the Heavens says "the sun has its middle course" — the middle course is the ecliptic; to crown a lucky spirit's day "Yellow Path" borrows the dignity of the sun's own road for an auspicious name. As for the reckoning that sets the rotation, it runs by the month's branch and the twelve branches, the various schools' formulas differing only in detail — enough here to say it turns like a watch. Sīmìng today deserves its own account, because "Sīmìng," the Arbiter of Destiny, is one name over two gods. The first stands in the Songs of Chu: among the Nine Songs, Qu Yuan set apart two pieces — "The Great Arbiter of Destiny" 大司命 and "The Lesser Arbiter of Destiny" 少司命 — hymns to the god who holds the span of human life: "each life has its appointed measure; who could contrive the partings and meetings?" — a destiny fixed to each person, beyond the strength of men: the early Chu imagining of the Arbiter. The second god sits in the kitchen of every household: the Stove God's full style is "Arbiter of Destiny of the Eastern Kitchen, the Stove Sovereign who Settles Blessings," and Ge Hong of the Jin dynasty wrote in the Baopuzi that "on the moon's dark night, the Stove God too ascends on high and reports men's faults" — the Stove Sovereign watches one household's good and evil and reports to heaven at the month's end; the year's customs of sending him off on the twenty-third of the twelfth month and welcoming him home at New Year's Eve escort exactly this god. The two Arbiters share one name but not one office: the Yellow Path's Arbiter holds records and the span of years, a lucky spirit of the rota; the kitchen's Arbiter keeps one hearth's smoke and weighs a family's deeds — the folk long folded the two into one, and the inscription "Arbiter of Destiny of the Eastern Kitchen" beside the Stove King's portrait is the mark of that folding. The amusing part: today's almanac sets building the stove among the avoided — the Stove Arbiter's day, and yet no touching of stove earth? It shows the almanac's real working: the Yellow Path's rotating Arbiter and the kitchen's Arbiter are not one god; the old stove-building craft has its own rules and its own days to avoid, standing in for neither the Yellow Path's luck nor its lack — the twelve gods keep their own watch, the Stove Sovereign keeps his own audit, each to his own post, neither standing in for the other. To today's almanac: the Yellow Path's Arbiter holds the day, and the doors of documents, offerings and wedding gladness stand wide, with the Monthly Virtue and the Resolving Spirit adding their force; only Xiǎohào and Tiānzéi guard the money gate — opening a market and trade take a slower day. Today the Dragon returns to the great marsh, and today's people hold their glad occasions — as for the stove in the kitchen, leave it standing; let the Arbiter finish his watch in peace.
