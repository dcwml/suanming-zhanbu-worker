Today is Yǐ Chǒu 乙丑 — the stem Yǐ is yin wood, the soft wood of vines and blossoms, pliant yet enduring; the branch Chǒu is damp earth, the tomb storehouse of metal, where qi freezes and gathers in. Yǐ wood sits upon Chǒu earth — wood overcoming earth — while the month command in Shēn metal stands at full strength and cuts the wood back, so today's wood qi is held in check and cannot unfurl: that is why the day's favors lean toward gathering, settling and closing. The auspicious list reads breaking ground for graves, burial, moving the coffin, encoffining, sacrifice, hunting and catching, removing and donning mourning dress, and the almanac's own verdict is "all other matters best avoided" — beyond these, no great undertaking should be begun. The presiding deity is Míngtáng 明堂 of the Yellow Path, upheld by the auspicious spirits Tiān'ēn 天恩 and Mǔcāng 母仓 — Míngtáng stands bright and open, so even matters of burial and remembrance may proceed with solemn calm. Only Xiǎohào 小耗 and Guījì 归忌 lurk among the inauspicious: Xiǎohào governs small losses and Guījì warns against journeys and homecomings, so guard against petty leaks of wealth and refrain from long travel. In sum, the qi of this day is stored and quiet, solemn and restrained: matters of burial and ancestral remembrance may proceed accordingly, while weddings, housewarmings and grand openings should wait for another day. And though today is Qīxī 七夕 — the Double Seventh festival of love — marriage stands explicitly on the unfavorable list, so couples planning to betroth or wed may do well to choose another day.

Zodiac Fortune · Ox

Today's branch is Chǒu (丑), which corresponds to the Ox — today's featured zodiac. Those born in an Ox year sit squarely on the day's own branch and so receive today's qi most directly: Yǐ wood bears down on Chǒu earth while the Shēn metal of the month drains their strength, so the Ox's fortune today is steady yet somewhat held back — favoring stillness over motion. At work, the day favors wrapping up, settling accounts and matters of burial and remembrance: any long-planned closing or clearing task may be finished today, while openings, trading and signing contracts all stand on the unfavorable list — new ventures and large investments should wait for another day. In wealth, Xiǎohào 小耗 presides over small losses: mind petty spending, resist impulse purchases and think twice before lending money, while Guījì 归忌 warns against travel and homecomings, so long journeys are best deferred. In love, marriage is explicitly unfavorable — and although today is Qīxī, Oxen planning a proposal or betrothal should stay calm rather than hurried, letting affection deepen at its own pace. One caution: today clashes with the Goat and the Sha lies East — avoid journeys far to the east, and settle any dispute with those born in a Goat year gently. The Joy God stands in the Northwest, the Wealth God in the Northeast and the Fortune God in the Southwest — favor these directions when you go out about business. In health, Chǒu is damp earth and the autumn air grows cool: Oxen should keep the spleen and stomach warm, go easy on cold and raw food, and those with weaker limbs or muscles should especially avoid overwork and damp chill.

Folklore

Today falls on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month — Qīxī 七夕, the Double Seventh festival. Qīxī grows from the legend of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl: the Weaver Girl, a heavenly granddaughter who wove the clouds into brocade, married a mortal cowherd and was parted from him by the Queen Mother with the River of Heaven — the Milky Way — so that only on this one night each year may the two cross a bridge of magpies to meet. The ancients watched the heavens: the Weaver Girl's star (Vega) rides high in the northern sky in autumn, and already the classic Shījīng sings of her — "three-cornered stands the Weaving Maid, seven journeys she makes each day" — a name older than the Qin dynasty itself. By the Han dynasty Qīxī had become a night of qǐqiǎo, "begging for skill": palace ladies threaded seven needles on open terraces, while women in village homes laid out melons and fruit, lit incense beneath the moon and prayed to the Weaver Girl for nimble hands and a happy marriage — hence the festival is also called the Skill-Begging Festival or the Maidens' Festival. Curiously, today's day pillar is Yǐ Chǒu 乙丑 and its zodiac is the Ox — the Cowherd's old companion — so an ox, it seems, keeps a seat at the heavenly reunion. Yet folk festivals and the almanac's favors run on separate logics: Qīxī may be a tender day in the human calendar, but today's almanac sets marriage plainly on the unfavorable list — date-pickers choose wedding days not by festivals but by the Yellow Calendar's auspicious spirits. That, precisely, is the interesting place where heaven's timing, earth's advantage and human accord each follow their own measure.
