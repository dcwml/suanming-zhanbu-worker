Today is Rén Shēn 壬申 — the stem Rén is yang water, the water of rivers in full flood, forceful and unimpeded; the branch Shēn is yang metal, stern and cutting metal. Metal produces water, branch feeding stem, so Shēn metal is the wellspring of Rén water — where the source runs deep, the stream runs long; and its Nayin is Sword Metal, metal tempered to an edge, its keenness on display. The presiding deity is Tiānláo 天牢, the Heavenly Prison, one of the six inauspicious spirits of the Black Path, governing the imagery of confinement and bondage, under which affairs should meet many hindrances; yet the auspicious spirits Yuèdé, Yuè'ēn, Sìxiàng and Wángrì attend together — Yuèdé, the Month Virtue, is the great virtue of the month's command, able to dissolve inauspicious spirits and turn peril to safety, and it is precisely the counterweight to the inauspicious qi of Tiānláo. Seventeen matters are therefore favored: marriage, accepting betrothal gifts, pledging betrothal, sacrifice, praying for blessings and offspring, travel, removal, erecting pillars, moving into a house, relocating, collecting wealth, raising beams, acquiring livestock, encoffining, burial and opening the tomb — wedding rites and matters of gathering-in alike unimpeded. Six matters are banned: planting, digging wells, breaking ground, setting the bed, breaking earth for graves and purchasing property — for Yuèjiàn, the Month Build, is the deity in command of the month, and Tǔfú, the Earth Talisman, governs injury to the earth's veins when ground is broken, so all matters of piercing the soil or sowing crops should be avoided; and Wǔlí, the Five Separations, governs parting and dispersal, so setting the bed — a matter of settling in — stands at odds with it and is likewise forbidden. Three days have passed since End of Heat and White Dew is not yet come, the metal qi at its peak — act today by borrowing the grace of Yuèdé to soften the prison of Tiānláo: press forward with weddings and relocations, hold back from breaking ground or sowing.

Zodiac Fortune · Monkey

Today's branch is Shēn (申), which corresponds to the Monkey — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Monkey year sit squarely on the day's own branch — Monkey meeting a Shēn day is like coming into its own place; yet the presiding deity Tiānláo is inauspicious, so only by the grace of Yuèdé, the Month Virtue, may things unfold smoothly. At work, Yuèdé is the great virtue of the month, governing the help of benefactors and the turning of peril to safety — Monkeys will often find trouble turning to fortune, and should seize matters that ask for documents and good faith such as weddings, pledges and signing contracts; collecting wealth stands among the favorable list, so trading, managing money, signing for and receiving payment all go well, and since the Wealth God stands in the due South, those seeking money or doing business may favor the south; travel too is favored, so business trips and visiting clients are unimpeded. In weddings, marriage, accepting betrothal gifts and pledging betrothal all stand among today's favorable matters — Monkeys with marriage in mind may fix the date or hold the betrothal today; only since Tiānláo governs bondage, the rite is better kept simple than elaborate, and it steadies the pledge to lean on the clean grace of Yuèdé. Moving into a house and relocating are also favored, so moving home may be set for today. Mind, though, that today clashes with the Tiger and the Sha lies South: yield a step to those born in a Tiger year; and while the Wealth God too stands in the South, the Sha lies there as well — tell apart seeking wealth from avoiding the Sha: going south for money, choose an auspicious hour and do not linger; breaking ground, breaking earth for graves, planting and digging wells are all among the bans, so any matter of piercing the soil or sowing is unfit today; and setting the bed is likewise forbidden, so installing a new bed should wait for another auspicious day. In health, End of Heat has passed and the autumn metal is rising; Shēn is metal and prone to dryness — Monkeys should guard against autumn dryness harming the lungs, drinking warm water and avoiding fried and spicy food, so that the lung metal stays moistened and one meets the gathering-in order of autumn.

Folklore

The presiding deity today is Tiānláo 天牢, the Heavenly Prison, one of the six inauspicious spirits of the Black Path — so today's reading is about this name itself. The twelve presiding deities — Qīnglóng 青龙, Míngtáng 明堂, Tiānxíng 天刑, Zhūquè 朱雀, Jīnkuì 金匮, Tiāndé 天德, Báihǔ 白虎, Yùtáng 玉堂, Tiānláo 天牢, Xuánwǔ 玄武, Sīmìng 司命 and Gōuchén 勾陈 — take duty in fixed rotation day by day, and six of them — Tiānxíng, Zhūquè, Báihǔ, Tiānláo, Xuánwǔ and Gōuchén — are inauspicious, together called the Six Inauspicious Spirits of the Black Path, and the days on which they preside are the Black Path inauspicious days. Of these six, Tiānláo carries the most shuttered imagery: Tiānláo is heaven's own prison, governing confinement, bondage and hardship, answering to matters of litigation, punishment and imprisonment, so when it presides, affairs are by nature hindered and one's freedom curtailed. Yet today the auspicious spirit Yuèdé, the Month Virtue, is present — the Month Virtue is the great virtue of the month's command, vast in power, able to dissolve every inauspicious spirit, so though Tiānláo is fierce, Yuèdé loosens its bonds and the favorable list runs in fact to seventeen matters — a clear showing of the power of a virtue spirit to dissolve. The ancients, in speaking of fate, had the saying "virtue covers the seven slaughter spirits" — fierce spirits though they be, when virtue meets them they dissolve; as a state with penal law but no benevolence is harsh, yet with benevolence to temper it, the penalties do not harm the people. An old almanac lays the auspicious and inauspicious side by side, the favorable and forbidden both written out — this very thing teaches one not to lose heart at the sight of ill, nor grow idle at the sight of good: Tiānláo presides yet Yuèdé attends — this is just that principle.
