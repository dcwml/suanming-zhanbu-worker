Yǐ seated on Chǒu is an orchid planted in a garden bed, its roots fed by the moist earth. Today the heavenly spirit is Xuánwǔ, the Dark Warrior, on the Black Path: the Dark Warrior is the water god of the north, presiding over thieves, private scheming and all that is dim and unclear; with the Black Path in power the great undertakings stand aside, hence the note at the foot of the favorable column — "all other matters set aside." Yet the three auspicious spirits are all of the generous kind: Heaven's Grace presides over favors from above, exactly fitting the paying back of thanks; the Mother's Granary presides over nurture and increase, giving strength to the rearing of creatures and the tending of plants; Fortune Arising presides over the first stirring of blessing. Hence today's favorable column holds the works of repair and reverence: dispelling misfortune, sweeping away calamity and closing out old matters; worship and offerings, the return to one's source — for today is the ninth day of the ninth month, the Double Ninth, and honoring ancestors and elders is precisely the day's fitting work; repairing walls and leveling roads, the ordering of one's grounds; building livestock pens, riding on the Mother's Granary's care. The six avoids each have their spirit behind them: with the Death Spirit in place, burial stands aside; Yuán Wǔ — the same Dark Warrior under an older name — presides over the dim and unclear, so marriages, opening a business and trading risk obscurity in what is signed and sworn; the Month Sha and the Month Void preside over depletion, and moving into a new home and enrolling in school are both out of season. Read across, the two columns hold one fine distinction: worship stands favorable while burial is avoided — worship is reverence held in the heart, burial is the breaking of the ground; today the reverence may be paid, the earth kept still. To climb the heights and honor the ancestors on this Double Ninth is the one door the ancients left open in a Black Path day.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday's lines closed on Jiǎ Zǐ reopening the board and the Heavenly Prison teaching one to settle before striving — and today is the ninth day of the ninth month, the Double Ninth: a festival built on meeting harm with avoidance. The Yijing counts one, three, five, seven and nine as the yang numbers, and nine is their utmost, the "old yang"; the ninth day of the ninth month doubles nine upon nine, hence Chóngyáng, the Doubled Yang, also called "Doubled Nine." When yang reaches its extreme it turns, and so the ancients chose this day to climb high and go far, meeting adversity with motion. A story in the Xù Qíxié Jì (Continued Records of Harmonious Mysteries) by Wu Jun of the Liang dynasty tells it: Huán Jǐng of Rǔnán studied under the adept Fèi Chángfáng, and one day Chángfáng warned him: "On the ninth day of the ninth month a great calamity will strike Runan. Have your family sew pouches filled with dogwood, tie them to their arms, climb to the heights and drink chrysanthemum wine — the harm can be turned aside." Huán Jǐng led his whole household up the mountain; at evening they returned to find every fowl, dog, ox and sheep about the house dead where they stood — the calamity's breath had taken the beasts in the household's place. From then on the customs of the ninth day — wearing dogwood, drinking chrysanthemum wine, climbing the heights — spread through the land, and the Xījīng Zájì (Miscellaneous Records of the Western Capital) adds that in the Han palace "on the ninth day of the ninth month they wore dogwood, ate peng-er cakes (蓬饵) and drank chrysanthemum wine, and it was said to lengthen life." "Nine-nine" (jiǔjiǔ) echoes "long-lasting" (jiǔjiǔ), and so the Double Ninth took on the meanings of praying for long life and honoring the aged — the Elders' Day of today keeps the same date. What is striking is that this logic of aversion shares one root with the almanac: when harm holds the day, do not meet its edge head-on — turn it aside with climbing, fragrance and wine. Just so today: the Dark Warrior holds the Black Path and "all other matters are set aside," yet worship and offerings stand squarely at the head of the favorable column — on a dark day no great works are raised, but the reverent heart is never laid aside. To climb high, honor the ancestors, revere the old and pray for long life: that is the one door left open in this Black Path Doubled Ninth.
