On Dīng Hài day, the day stem Dīng (Fire) sits atop Hài (Water), where Water conquers Fire to form the Officer star; Hài harbors Rén and Jiǎ, bringing mutual generation of Officer and Seal, so Dīng Fire, though weak, has support. White Dew has just passed and Yǒu Metal commands the season; Metal produces Water, making Hài Water clear and strong. Dīng Fire is like a lamp flame — it burns bright when nourished by Wood but dims when overcome by Water. Today Xuánwǔ of the Black Path presides, governing obscurity, theft and scheming by petty people; yet six auspicious spirits — Xiāngrì, Yìmǎ, Tiānhòu, Tiānwū, Fúdé and Yào'ān — gather simultaneously. Though the inauspicious spirits are present, the auspicious qi is also strong, creating a pattern of "seeking good fortune through movement." A broad range of matters are favored: opening a market, trading, signing contracts, hanging plaques, consecration, travel, moving into a house, relocating, setting the bed, moving fire and raising the ridge beam — eleven matters in all — especially favorable for opening businesses, relocating and long-distance travel. Avoid installing a stove, funerals, haircutting, boat travel, marriage and burial. The overall tone is "act while upholding rectitude": travel and business ventures are all acceptable, but one must guard against underhanded schemes and wasteful loss; all matters should be conducted openly and aboveboard, avoiding hidden dishonesty.

Metaphysics Insight

Xuánwǔ (the Black Tortoise) is one of the inauspicious spirits among the twelve daily presiding deities, grouped with the Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Hook Array and Heavenly Prison as a Black Path spirit. It governs obscurity, secrets, theft, petty people and water-related misfortune. Its name derives from one of the Four Symbols of ancient cosmology — the Black Tortoise (a composite of turtle and snake) — which occupies the north, corresponds to Water, and is black in color; hence "Xuán" (black, hidden) and "Wǔ" (martial, protective). In date selection, a Xuánwǔ day often points to hidden matters, undisclosed dealings, thieves lying in wait, underhanded schemes and ambiguous documents, so openly aboveboard affairs are generally discouraged. Yet Xuánwǔ is not entirely without use — matters of secret planning, covert investigation and accumulating hidden virtue can actually receive its assistance; this is the saying that "the Black Path is suitable for hidden affairs." Today, as Xuánwǔ presides, although five inauspicious spirits — Wǔxū, Bāfēng, Dàshà, Chóngrì and Yuánwǔ — surround the day, six auspicious spirits provide transformation: Yìmǎ governs movement, making travel and relocation auspicious; Tiānhòu governs unseen assistance, with noble people supporting from the shadows; Fúdé governs blessings and fortune, turning danger into safety; Tiānwū governs shamanic healing, making treatments and prayers effective; Yào'ān governs stability, ensuring peace in dwelling and occupation; Xiāngrì governs mutual suitability, making all things well-matched. Together these six convert Xuánwǔ's dark qi into a strategy of "openly repairing the plank road, secretly advancing through Chencang" — outward conduct must be upright and transparent, while behind-the-scenes planning can find support. Just as The Art of War states, "War is the way of deception"; used for righteous purposes it brings good fortune, used for evil it brings ruin.
