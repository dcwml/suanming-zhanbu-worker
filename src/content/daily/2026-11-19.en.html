On a Dīng Yǒu day, Dīng is yin fire — the fire of lamp and candle, of the hearth's steady flame; Yǒu is yin metal, metal come to its finished form — and Dīng fire seated on Yǒu falls, in the twelve stages of long life, upon the very stage of "birth": by the rule of the yin stems' long life, Dīng fire is born at Yǒu — like a lamp that kindles only as night comes down, the wick catching just as the daylight fails — hence today's Na Yin, again Fire at the Foot of the Mountain 山下火: yesterday's Bǐng Shēn fire was "a sun gone down behind the ridge, its glow folded in"; today's Dīng Yǒu fire is more like a lamp lit at the Yǒu hours — the sky's light ending as the lamp's begins, lighting one corner, warming one room: a fire for kindling, not for blazing; for small flames, not for great ones. The day god arrives at Zhūquè 朱雀, the Vermilion Bird — a Black Path spirit of ill: the Vermilion Bird dwells in the south of the Four Emblems, the southern mansions strung together into the shape of a bird, of the fire element, governing quarrels of the tongue and strife over documents — when the Vermilion Bird holds the day, whatever turns on words, on written deeds, or on lawsuits, prudence comes first. Yet today six auspicious spirits assemble against four baleful ones — a day of "ill stars under control": Mǔcāng 母仓, the Mother's Granary (told yesterday), the spirit of nourishing and increase, feeding the grain and tending all things — opening a business and trading draw their gain on its strength. Shíyáng 时阳, the Hour's Yang, the spirit of the seasons' flowing light, governs smooth-running fortune: travel and opening a business move on its breeze. Shēngqì 生气, the Quickening Breath, governs all things sprouting and life renewing — praying for blessings and for offspring stand favorable on precisely this spirit. Shèngxīn 圣心, the Sage's Heart, is Heaven's own compassion, and prayers offered on its day go answered — sacrifices, blessing-prayers, prayers for offspring, fasting rites and consecration stand favorable in one unbroken string, all the Sage's Heart at work: today is a prayer-day rare in a whole month. Chúshén 除神, the Sweeping Spirit (told yesterday), clears the old and makes room for the new — clearing misfortune and dismantling stand favorable on its hour. Míngwǔ 鸣吠, the Crying of Dogs — and Yǒu is the Rooster: the crowing falling on a rooster's day, the sound doubly true; it ordinarily favors opening the earth for burial, and breaking ground and groundbreaking stand favorable today on precisely its strength — yet placing in the coffin and burial still stand avoided, for the Five Partings stand at hand and the lucky spirit gives way: the great matters of the grave are not taken. This is the almanac's fine art of "spirits checking spirits, matter by matter." The baleful four: Zāishà 灾煞, the Calamity Spirit, governs ruin and sudden loss — raising timber invites accident, hence felling timber, raising the ridge beam, construction, entering a new home and installing doors all stand avoided on this count. Tiānhuǒ 天火, the Heavenly Fire, governs blaze and candle-flame harm — work that wakes fire is forbidden: building the stove stands avoided on precisely this count, and felling firewood keeps clear too. Wǔlí 五离, the Five Partings, the family-name of the heaven-and-earth parting days (Jiǎ Chén, Yǐ Sì and their kin — told yesterday as Jiǎ Shēn), governs separation — meeting kin and friends stands avoided on this count, and placing in the coffin, burial, and cutting hair follow it, for to cut hair is to part from one's own body: a "parting" image as well. And Zhūquè is the presiding god, already told. The favorable list numbers sixteen: marriages and the capping — the Sage's Heart weds, the Quickening Breath sends sons; the capping and hair-pinning is the coming-of-age rite, and wedding and capping favorable on one day are both the great rites of a life. Sacrifices, blessing-prayers, offspring-prayers, fasting rites, consecration — five matters in one string, the Sage's Heart presiding: wishes spoken today carry best. Travel — the Hour's Yang walks the road with you. Opening a business, trading, signing contracts, hanging plaques — the Granary breeds the money and the Hour's Yang warms the market; only the Vermilion Bird governs documents and the tongue: though contracts stand favorable, read the clauses close and keep the tone gentle — this is how a controlled day is taken. Clearing misfortune and dismantling — the Sweeper's work: old things, old bonds, old ailments, old gloom, all may be taken apart and let go. Groundbreaking and breaking ground — the Crying holds the day, and the earth may be opened. The avoided list numbers ten: felling timber, ridge beams and construction sit on the Calamity Spirit, and firewood keeps clear of the Heavenly Fire besides. Coffin and burial sit on the Five Partings. Hair-cutting and meeting kin sit on the Five Partings too — today the feast may be held, but the long journey to visit waits: the Sage's Heart is here, yet so is the parting qi. Entering a home and installing doors sit on the Calamity Spirit; the stove sits on the Heavenly Fire. Clash the Rabbit, the Sha lies East: Mǎo and Yǒu stand opposed — with Rabbit people today, guard against contention above all: the Vermilion Bird governs the tongue, and a quarrel-star on a clash-line needs one word to catch fire; eastern journeys take a slower step. Minor Snow three days out (Nov 22): the Hài month's water swells and winter's storing deepens — let the lamp give warmth, not blaze: do today's work while the sun is up, do not let it drag, and come home when the lamp is lit — that is the measure of a Dīng Yǒu day.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Monkey of Shēn showed how Shēn was first a bolt of lightning, the first writing of the spirits; today the Rooster of Yǒu takes up the graph Yǒu — and it is a wine jar. In the oracle-bone script, Yǒu is drawn as a pointed-bottomed, round-bellied wine vessel with a neck and a lid, its mouth sometimes opened into an "eight" shape — the lid lifted, the aroma spilling out. The Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字 says: "Yǒu means 'readiness' 就. In the eighth month the millet ripens and may be brewed into slow wine." Yǒu is the original graph of 酒 "wine": first there was Yǒu alone, naming both the jar and the liquor it held; only later did scribes add the three-dot water radical to make 酒, parting water from wine — and the Yǒu sitting inside 酒 is that very jar. Nearly every graph that takes Yǒu as its radical speaks of fermentation: 酿 to brew, 酵 to leaven, 醇 mellow, 酌 to pour, 醉 drunk, 醒 sober, 酹 to pour a libation — out of one jar of wine grew a whole family of characters. Why does the Rooster pair with Yǒu, the hours of five to seven in the evening? Two old accounts. First: at the Yǒu hours the sun drops behind the western hills and dusk gathers — exactly when "the chickens go to roost," 鸡栖于埘, the birds taking to their perches: the Book of Songs sings it in "The Gentleman at Service" — "the chickens rest on their roost-planks; the sun is setting." To retire at sundown fits Yǒu. Second: Yǒu belongs to metal, and the rooster's crow at dawn rings like struck metal; the ancients used the cock-crow in place of the watch-drum — "one cock's crow, and all the world turns white." The rooster is the timekeeper-official, and the timekeeper-bird is mated to the timekeeper-branch: a natural match. Then a tale that belongs to the rooster above all — "dumb as a wooden rooster" 呆若木鸡: from the Zhuangzi, chapter on the fullness of life. Jì Xǐngzǐ kept fighting cocks for the king. After ten days the king asked: "Are the cocks ready?" — "Not yet. They are vain and puffed up, riding on their own spirits." Ten days more: "Not yet. They still startle at every sound and shadow." Ten days more: "Not yet. Their eyes glare and their anger has not settled." Only at the fourth ten-day span did he report: "They stand like roosters carved of wood — their virtue is complete." Other cocks so much as glimpse them and turn and run. Today the phrase means to be struck dumb with fright, but its first sense is the exact opposite: the wooden rooster's dullness is the fighting cock's highest reach — no showing off, no answering cry, no furious stare; the whole bird's essence gathered down into the bone, still as a mountain — "no other cock dared take it up; they turned away." And the Zhuangzi notes the last stage of the training before this one was precisely the glare of unspent anger — the bird that has learned stillness is the bird no one dares engage. Today the Vermilion Bird holds the day, lord of quarrels — and nothing suits it better than that other cock, the one that startles at sounds and shadows, eyes glaring, first to crow challenge: the bright voice, the hot temper, the rush to call out — it looks like winning, but the edge shows all too soon and the qi leaks away with the noise. Today's rooster learns from the wooden one: read the contract close and do not spend a breath on idle heat; yield three parts of the argument and do not grab the quick retort; gather the fire down into the bone — one whose virtue is complete, no one dares provoke.
