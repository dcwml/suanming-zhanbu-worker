Nothing is favorable

On Yǐ Yǒu day, the day stem Yǐ (Wood) sits atop Yǒu (Metal) at its extinction point, where Metal dominates and Wood withers. White Dew has just passed and Yǒu Metal commands the season, making Metal qi fierce and Wood heavily restrained. Although Yùtáng of the Yellow Path presides over this day, joined by seven auspicious spirits including Yuèdéhé, Guānrì and Liùyí, seven inauspicious spirits — Yuèjiàn, Yuèxíng, Tǔfǔ, Yānduì, Zhāoyáo, Wǔlí and Xiǎoshí — gather simultaneously. Auspicious and inauspicious forces clash, with the sha qi prevailing. Today only three matters are favored: sacrifice, travel and house sweeping; all else is best avoided. Major undertakings such as marriage, groundbreaking, opening a market or burial should be postponed. The overall tone is to conserve and hold steady, avoiding rash action.

On this Yǐ Yǒu day, the Rooster sits in its own position, bringing ample energy yet also hidden depletion, as Yǐ Wood is restrained by Yǒu Metal. Career matters should focus on internal organization and cleaning up loose ends rather than launching new ventures. The Yuèdéhé spirit offers some protection against mishaps, yet Yuèjiàn and Yuèxíng warn against careless words and unexpected injury. The Wealth God resides in the Northeast, offering modest financial prospects in that direction. Romance is clouded by Yānduì, making discussions of engagement or marriage inadvisable. Today clashes with the Rabbit and brings sha to the East — those born in the Year of the Rabbit should exercise caution and avoid heading east.

Metaphysics Insight

Yùtáng is one of the auspicious spirits among the twelve daily presiding deities, ranking alongside Qīnglóng, Míngtáng, Jīnkuì and Sīmìng as one of the "Five Yellow Path" benefactors. It governs scholarly honors, noble patronage and the prestige of jade and gold. The ancient text Huainanzi states: "The way of heaven is round, the way of earth is square; the sage king patterns himself after them to establish the Bright Hall." Yùtáng originated in ancient astronomy as the Yùtáng star group within the Purple Forbidden Enclosure, later extended to mean the imperial council chamber, symbolizing nobility and dignified authority. In fortune-telling and date selection, a Yùtáng day is most favorable for examinations, seeking office, signing contracts and document matters, as it indicates unseen noble assistance that doubles the result with half the effort. Yet today, although Yùtáng is present, it is encircled by Yuèjiàn, Yuèxíng and other inauspicious spirits, weakening its auspicious power and creating a "danger within good fortune" pattern. This illustrates that date selection cannot rely solely on the daily presiding spirit; a comprehensive assessment of all spirits, stems, branches and solar terms is necessary to properly seek advantage and avoid harm.
