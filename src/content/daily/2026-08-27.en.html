Folklore

The 15th day of the 7th lunar month marks the traditional Zhongyuan Festival (known as the Ullambana Festival in Buddhism and colloquially as the Ghost Festival). The name Zhongyuan comes from Daoism's "Three Origins" doctrine — the Upper Origin (15th of 1st month) where the Heaven Official bestows blessings, the Middle Origin (15th of 7th month) where the Earth Official pardons sins, and the Lower Origin (15th of 10th month) where the Water Official relieves disasters. Tradition holds that on the 15th day of the 7th month, the Earth Official descends to judge virtue and forgive souls; people hold rituals, make offerings to ancestors, conduct universal salvation ceremonies, and release river lanterns to guide spirits, expressing filial piety and boundless compassion. In Buddhism, the Ullambana offering originates from the filial legend of Mulian rescuing his mother, honoring the monastic community to repay ancestral kindness. In traditional almanac selection, the 15th of the 7th month frequently highlights sacrifice, prayer, dispelling evil and peaceful burial rites. With Tiāndé, Chúshén and Yīndé among the eight auspicious spirits today, Tiāndé represents supreme kindness, Chúshén purges misfortune, and Yīndé silently protects all realms, perfectly echoing the spiritual essence of Zhongyuan's sin-pardoning and virtue-accumulating tradition.
