The day's spirit in the twelve-spirits round is Jīnkuì, the Golden Treasury — a Yellow Path auspicious day: the Golden Treasury is a chest for storing gold, governing wealth kept safe and blessings laid down; yet the auspicious spirits number only two — Yìhòu, the Benefactor of Descendants, and Jīnkuì itself — and this pair guards "settling" and "farewell," not "founding" and "welcoming the new." Hence the favored column opens with setting the bed and offerings and sacrifice, keeps pond-digging and wall-mending among the works of repair, and lists the four funerary matters together — placing in the coffin, moving the coffin, breaking ground and opening the tomb — for Jīnkuì and Yìhòu are the chief auspicious spirits of funerary choosing in the almanac. Metaphysics Insight

Today is the thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month — the moon has traveled to the end of its light, and the old calendar calls it Huì, the dark of the moon. The Shuowen dictionary says: "Huì — the moon's exhaustion." The lunar calendar fixes its days by the moon's face: the first day is Shuò, the new moon, hidden and lightless; the seventh or eighth shows the half wheel, the First Quarter; the fifteenth is Wàng, the full moon; the twenty-second or third gathers back the half wheel, the Last Quarter; and on the month's last day comes Huì, when the moonlight is gathered up and no moon hangs in the sky — one new moon, one full, one dark, and the moon's full circuit makes one lunar month. In the Tang dynasty Huì was a great day: on every dark day officials and commoners went out to feast and sing, a festival called the Huì Day; and on the first month's dark day came the "Sending Off of Poverty" — sweeping the spirit of want out the door. Han Yu's "Essay on Sending Off Poverty" tells of five poverty ghosts — poverty of wit, of learning, of letters, of fate and of friendship — clinging fast and refusing to go, while the master readies cart and boat, prepares food and drink, and respectfully sees them on their way; and from then on, sending off poverty became the loudest stroke of the dark day's folklore. The Stove God too ascends on the dark day to report on the household's good and evil, so the old families kept a small rite before the stove at Huì. The dark day closes; the new day begins — tomorrow is the first of the tenth month, both the new moon's birth and the Cold Clothing Festival: the dark day sends off poverty, the new day sends winter clothes — one "sending" follows upon another. And today's almanac answers the same theme everywhere: the day's spirit is Jīnkuì, the chest that stores gold; the Na Yin is Earth on the Roof, the roof above and the earth beneath; and with Start of Winter past, heaven and earth are both entered into winter's storing — the moon hides its light, the gold enters the chest, the ten thousand things return to the storehouse: three foldings of "store" upon one day. So gathering-in is better than letting-out, and the avoided column's nine matters are all "moving" and "adding" — the almanac and the moon speak one sentence today: storing is the day's best posture.
