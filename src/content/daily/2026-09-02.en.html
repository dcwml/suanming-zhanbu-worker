Today is Jǐ Mǎo 己卯 — the stem Jǐ is yin earth, the earth of gardens and fields, warm and nourishing; the branch Mǎo is yin wood, like vines and flowers, supple and growing. Wood controls earth, yet Mǎo wood opens up Jǐ earth, carrying the imagery of "cultivating earth to nourish wood" — though controlling, it is no vicious battle, but rather an affinity between wood and earth. Its Nayin is City Wall Earth, the rampart earth that guards a nation, with Mǎo wood rooting upon it, making the foundation firm. The presiding deity Zhūquè is a Black Path inauspicious spirit, governing quarrels, disputes, documents and false alarms, yet today thirteen matters are favored, thanks to three auspicious spirits — Tiān'ēn, Yìhòu and Wǔhé — attending together: Tiān'ēn governs heavenly grace and pardon; Yìhòu governs benefit and later gain; Wǔhé governs harmony and smooth social relations. Folklore

Today's presiding deity is Zhūquè, so today's reading is about the lore and meaning of the Vermilion Bird. Zhūquè is one of the twelve heavenly deities of the almanac, belonging to the Black Path of inauspicious spirits, governing quarrels, disputes, documents, false alarms and fire. The name Zhūquè originates in one of the ancient Four Symbols — the Southern Vermilion Bird, the essence of fire, shaped like a phoenix, crimson in color, governing summer, fire and the south. Yet in the almanac's spirit system, Zhūquè is not an auspicious bird but a spirit governing quarrels and litigation: on any day when Zhūquè presides, verbal conflicts, document errors and unease are likely, especially litigation, negotiation and contract disputes. Zhūquè stands in opposition to Qīnglóng: Qīnglóng is the wood of the east, governing growth and celebration; Zhūquè is the fire of the south, governing rising flames and quarrels. Wood generates fire, so Qīnglóng generates Zhūquè, and the auspice of a Qīnglóng day can nurture the fire qi of a Zhūquè day. When Zhūquè presides, if accompanied by auspicious spirits such as Tiān'ēn and Wǔhé, quarrels may turn into social relations and disputes into harmony — just as today: though Zhūquè is inauspicious, Tiān'ēn governs grace and Wǔhé governs harmony, greatly reducing its force and instead favoring alliances, betrothal and social affairs. The ancients said: "On a Zhūquè day, be careful with words, guard documents, and avoid fire." Today's favored matters mostly concern alliances, sacrifice, opening markets and moving into houses — social and ritual affairs — which can use the auspice of Tiān'ēn and Wǔhé to dissolve Zhūquè's quarrels; while breaking ground, digging earth and digging wells are avoided, using the inauspice of Tǔfú and Wǔxū to escape the drain of wood and earth. Ten days after End of Heat, autumn metal grows strong; metal controls wood while wood generates fire, so Zhūquè's fire qi is restrained by metal and cannot blaze out of control — between auspicious and inauspicious, all depends on human choice.
