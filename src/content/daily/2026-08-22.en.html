Today is Wù Chén 戊辰 — the stem Wù is yang earth, the earth of city walls and high mountains, massive and carrying all things; the branch Chén is likewise yang earth, and besides the storehouse of water, moist soil that nourishes all growing things — stem and branch one qi, a double measure of earth, earth qi flourishing and firm. Yet the Nayin of this very day is Great Forest Wood: upon deep earth a forest of great trees grows, wood mastering earth even as it fulfills it — only deep soil can anchor towering timber, only a forest shows the earth's vitality, conquest and completion entwined, each needing the other. The presiding deity Jīnkuì 金匮 is one of the six Yellow Path gods, governing storage and ingathering; and at the head of the auspicious spirits stands Tiāndéhé 天德合, a virtue-star overhead that turns misfortune to fortune — Sānhé 三合 presides over union and achievement, Tiānxǐ 天喜 over joyous occasions, Tiānyī 天医 over medicine and healing, Xùshì 续世 over continuing generations, Mǔcāng 母仓 over nurture, and Tiān'ēn 天恩 bestows grace from above: eight auspicious spirits assembled together, so that the favorable list runs to twenty-five items — opening for business, signing contracts, relocating, building, raising beams and planting, nothing amiss. Only remember: the favors are broad but the bans are strict — relocating is favorable yet moving into a house is not, so shifting and settling may proceed while the formal housewarming waits for another day; accepting betrothal and pledging are favorable yet marriage is not, so the rites of engagement may go ahead while the wedding itself awaits a finer hour. With Yuèyàn 月厌, Dìhuǒ 地火, Sìjī 四击 and Xuèjì 血忌 among the inauspicious spirits, and End of Heat arriving tomorrow as summer heat recedes and metal qi gathers in, today is a day to advance with the steadiness of earth and to store away with the stillness of wood.

Zodiac Fortune · Dragon

Today's branch is Chén (辰), which corresponds to the Dragon — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Dragon year sit squarely on the day's own branch, receiving today's qi in direct accord, and with a Yellow Path day and eight auspicious spirits shining upon them, this is one of those rare days when advancing or retreating both fall in one's favor. At work and in wealth, opening for business, trading, signing contracts and setting up machinery all stand on the favorable list, and with Sānhé 三合 presiding over union and achievement, negotiating partnerships, sealing agreements and launching new projects will move far more smoothly today; Jīnkuì 金匮 governs storage and ingathering — collecting payments, tidying accounts and drawing scattered funds home to the vault is precisely its emblem. In love, Tiānxǐ 天喜 presides over joyous occasions, and while accepting betrothal and pledging stand among the favors, marriage stands among the bans — Dragons preparing a wedding will do well to settle engagements and betrothal rites today, but choose another auspicious day for the ceremony itself; there is no need to rush everything into one. Today clashes with the Dog and the Sha lies South — yield a step when working with those born in a Dog year, and keep important matters away from the due south if you can. The Joy God stands in the Southeast, the Wealth God in the North and the Fortune God in the Northeast — favor these directions when you go out about business. In health, today is the last day before End of Heat: earth qi runs strong while summer damp has not yet cleared, and the Dragon belongs to earth, answering to the spleen and stomach — eat light, warm and gentle, spare the cold and raw, and guard the middle qi; once End of Heat passes, dryness will rise, so add a few moistening foods to ease the turn of the season.

Folklore

The Nayin of today's Wù Chén 戊辰 is Great Forest Wood (大林木). The sixty Stem-Branch pairs are matched with thirty Nayins, every two neighboring pillars sharing one, six to each of the five elements and thirty in all; Great Forest Wood belongs to the pillars Wù Chén 戊辰 and Jǐ Sì 己巳, and stands third among the thirty, close behind Sea Metal and Furnace Fire. The wood Nayins number six — Great Forest Wood, Willow Wood, Pine-Cypress Wood, Plain Wood, Pomegranate Wood and Mulberry Wood — each taking the image of a plant, each with its own temper: Willow is soft and bends with the wind, Pine-Cypress is firm and outlasts the frost, Plain Wood spreads low and humble, Pomegranate shows its red blossoms to be admired, and Mulberry feeds silkworms with its leaves and makes bows of its timber — Mulberry Wood, as we said days ago, is a plant of daily folk life; only Great Forest Wood takes for its image woods lush and flourishing, trees rising to touch the sky, the grandest temper of the six, called by the ancients timber worthy of ridgepoles. The nicest point lies in today itself: the day pillar Wù Chén belongs to earth by element, yet to wood by Nayin — upon deep earth a forest of great trees grows, wood mastering earth even as it fulfills it; without deep soil no towering tree could root, without forests no soil would hold its water, conquest and completion, each fulfilling the other. Look again at today's favorable list: building, raising beams, planting, setting up machinery — raising beams calls for the finest timber, planting nurtures the sapling into a tree, everything that uses wood and works the earth stands among the favors, as if the old almanac were answering the image of Great Forest Wood. The old fate-books often say that those bearing a Great Forest Wood destiny carry an air of sheltering and supporting others, ambitions reaching to the sky, like a great tree that draws a hundred birds to perch — and that, too, is the Nayin's lesson for people: to become a ridgepole, one must first strike roots deep.
