The Na Yin is again Lampshade Fire 覆灯火 — Jiǎ Chén and Yǐ Sì share it: yesterday one lamp before the hall, today the flame carries on burning; only today the day branch Sì clashes the month branch Hài, striking the "Monthly Rupture" 月破 — the month's Hài water and the day's Sì fire stand head-to-head, and the day's qi scatters rather than gathers, exactly like a lamp in a draft: the flame is still there, but the light sways — which is why the almanac cuts the favorable column to its narrowest, seven matters only, closing with the note "all other matters set aside." The day god arrives at Gōuchén 勾陈, a Black Path day: where yesterday Sīmìng held the Yellow Path to close the rota's bright turn, today Gōuchén takes the watch and the tone turns dark — Gōuchén governs entanglement and delay: land disputes, old matters that hang on, anything that pulls many parties into the rope tangles worse the more it is worked today. Four baleful ones stand in list, and the heaviest is Yuèpò 月破 at the head: on a day whose branch clashes the month's command, every matter whose soul is the word "gather" stands avoided — so the two great turns of a life, marriage and burial, sit squarely in the avoided column, and even matters not listed there — opening a market, moving house — fall inside the four closing characters, "all else set aside." Dàhào 大耗, the Great Consumer, follows the rupture: when qi scatters, wealth scatters with it — rupture scatters, consumption drains, two names for the two faces of one thing — hence today signs no contracts, settles no large sums, makes no investments; keep the purse tight. Where the Heaven Virtue stands, the baleful spirits lose their edge — hence while the avoided column holds the wide gates of marriage and burial, the favorable keeps four straight roads open: worship, clearing away misfortune, seeking care and treatment — under the Heaven Virtue's guard, prayers find their answer and healing finds its force. A Black Path day of Gōuchén with rupture and consumption beneath it — today's measure is one line: what tears down may proceed; what builds up, pause — clearing sickness and ill luck, demolishing what is due for demolition, all ride the day's own qi; weddings, burials, contracts and market openings wait for a luckier hour.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday's talk was of Sīmìng and the Yellow Path — of the twelve day-gods and their watch-rota; today the rota reaches Gōuchén and the Black Path — but the weight in today's almanac does not rest on Gōuchén. It rests on the head of the inauspicious list: Yuèpò 月破, the Monthly Rupture. The Monthly Rupture is the day whose branch clashes the month's branch — the month's command, the month-branch, runs between Winter's Start and Minor Snow in Hài, and Hài clashes Sì, so every Sì day inside a Hài month is a rupture day. Whence comes the clash? The twelve branches pair off face to face — Zǐ against Wǔ, Chǒu against Wèi, Yín against Shēn, Mǎo against Yǒu, Chén against Xū, Sì against Hài — six pairs in all, called the Six Clashes: two branches standing opposite, directions at odds and qi at war, pictured of old as two armies drawn up against each other, point meeting point. The month's command is the master of the month's qi — through the month, the qi gathers at the month-branch; the day-branch marches straight at it, and the month's gathered qi is knocked apart at the front — scattered, not gathered: the root sense of the word "rupture." The old notes say it plainly: "On a day of Monthly Rupture, though no other baleful sign stand, all matters are set aside." The soul of human affairs is the word "gather": a wedding gathers the good of two families, a burial gathers the departed's rest, a market's opening gathers the qi of trade, a contract gathers the trust of both sides — on a rupture day the qi itself scatters, nothing gathers and nothing stands, and so the old custom barred every great matter from the day. After rupture comes Dàhào, the Great Consumer: qi scatters, and wealth scatters behind it — rupture scatters, consumption drains; two names for the two faces of one thing — hence on a rupture day, above all, no great movement of money. Then why does demolishing a ruined house and tearing down walls stand squarely in the favorable column? Here is the almanac's own clever turn: pulling down a house, razing a wall, dismantling — these are "breaking" works, and what a breaking work wants is exactly the word "scatter" — set rupture against what needs rupturing, ride the day's own qi, and the work goes at half the effort and twice the force. Seeking care, treatment and clearing away misfortune stand favorable by the same logic: curing an illness is breaking an evil, clearing misfortune is sweeping out ill luck — every matter of "tearing out the old" borrows the rupture's force and takes it rightly. The same thought runs in the twelve Officers of the day-cycle — Jiàn 建, Chú 除, Mǎn 满, Píng 平, Dìng 定, Zhí 执, Pò 破, Wēi 危, Chéng 成, Shōu 收, Kāi 开, Bì 闭 — twelve names rotating one to a day against the month's branch, and the seventh among them, Pò, "the Breaker," is set by the very same rule: the day whose branch clashes the month's — Monthly Rupture and the Officer's Breaker are one post under two names: every school of date selection, whatever its system, agreed that this one day's qi gathers nothing and scatters everything. Today's almanac carries the thought out to the letter: the favorable column's seven matters — worship, clearing, demolishing a ruined house, tearing down walls, seeking care, treatment — are all the trade of "tearing out the old," closed by "all other matters set aside"; the avoided column's marriage and burial are all the great works of "building up." The Heaven Virtue sits on the Yǐ stem, and within the rupture stands a guard: healing and release have a god watching over them, sweeping and clearing have a patron standing near. The Snake walks a rupture day like a lamp in the wind — the flame need not go out; shield it and it holds. The same rule holds for everyone: the old must go before the new can come — break what is due for breaking, sweep what is due for sweeping, leave the great matters for a luckier day, and that is the best way to get on with this one.
