Folklore

Today's presiding deity is Tiānxíng, so today's reading is about the lore and meaning of the Celestial Punishment. Tiānxíng is one of the twelve heavenly deities of the almanac, belonging to the Black Path of inauspicious spirits, governing punishment, litigation, disputes and bloodshed. The name Tiānxíng originates in the concept of ancient criminal law — heaven inflicts punishment upon the mortal realm, governing official troubles, imprisonment and penal injury. On any day when Tiānxíng presides, most matters are inauspicious, especially litigation, breaking ground, marriage and travel. Tiānxíng stands in opposition to Tiāndé: Tiāndé is the grace of heaven, governing pardon and dissolution; Tiānxíng is the punishment of heaven, governing chastisement and discipline. One virtue, one punishment — one reward, one penalty — embodying the ancients' understanding of the Heavenly Way's "reward the good, punish the wicked." In the rotation of the twelve deities, Tiānxíng often coincides with inauspicious spirits such as Yuèpò and Dàhào, doubling its force; but if mitigated by auspicious spirits such as Tiāndéhé and Jiěshén, its severity may be reduced. Today Tiānxíng presides, joined by Yuèpò, Dàhào and Yuèxíng — three inauspicious spirits — yet Tiāndéhé, Yìmǎ, Tiānhòu, Shèngxīn, Jiěshén and Wǔhé, six auspicious spirits, also attend — auspicious and inauspicious in contention, with resolution within the inauspicious. The ancients said: "On a Tiānxíng day, be still rather than active, favor the old over the new." This fits today's favored matters of bathing, demolishing a house and tearing down walls — removing the old to comply with Tiānxíng's meaning of clearing away the old, while new endeavors are set aside to avoid the spirit's punitive force.
