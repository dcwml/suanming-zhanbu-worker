Today is Xīn Yǒu 辛酉 — the stem Xīn is yin metal, the metal of pearls, gems, needles and awls, lustrous and keen; the branch Yǒu is likewise metal, and Xīn sits upon Yǒu in its official position of strength: with autumn metal in season, this is fine material for cutting and reshaping, and the Nayin, Pomegranate Wood, adds its toughness and solidity — a day suited to sweeping away the old and setting up the new. The auspicious spirits Guānrì 官日, Jíqī 吉期, Chúshén 除神 and Míngfèi 鸣吠 attend together: Guānrì favors official business, contracts and agreements, Jíqī is a fortunate hour for all undertakings, and Chúshén carries the Removal officer and governs cleansing away what is worn out — so dismantling, construction, breaking ground, laying foundations, raising beams, setting beds, installing doors, digging canals and digging ponds are all placed on the favorable list, together with encoffining, breaking ground for graves and opening the tomb. Yet the inauspicious spirits Xiánchí 咸池, Wǎngwáng 往亡, Wǔlí 五离 and Yuánwǔ 元武 stand in the way — Xiánchí governs clandestine romance and murky affairs, Wǎngwáng bodes ill for journeys, and Wǔlí portends separation — so marriage, travel, taking up an official post, moving into a house, relocating, planting, welcoming people into the household and installing a stove are all placed on the unfavorable list; and with Xuánwǔ 玄武, a Black Path spirit governing murkiness and quarrels of the tongue, presiding over the day, act in the open rather than in secret. In sum, the qi of this day favors substance over show and work over wandering: construction and renewal are most auspicious, while weddings and long journeys should wait.

Zodiac Fortune · Rooster

Today's branch is Yǒu (酉), which corresponds to the Rooster — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Rooster year sit squarely on the day's own branch with metal qi in command, and with the auspicious spirits Guānrì 官日, Jíqī 吉期 and Chúshén 除神 lending support, this is a day for sweeping away the old and setting up the new — dismantling and rebuilding, raising beams, setting beds, installing doors, digging canals and ponds may all be set in motion; seek fortune toward the Wealth God in the due East. Mind three cautions: first, today clashes with the Rabbit and the Sha lies East — leave room in dealings with those born in a Rabbit year, and postpone long journeys eastward; second, marriage, travel, taking up an official post, moving into a house, relocating, planting and installing a stove are all on the unfavorable list — weddings and relocations should wait for another day; third, the presiding deity Xuánwǔ 玄武 is an inauspicious spirit of the Black Path governing murkiness and quarrels of the tongue — Roosters may meet misunderstood words and overlooked paperwork today, so read every contract clause by clause and do not rely on verbal promises. In health, autumn metal turns severe, and strong metal wounds wood — Roosters may feel tension in the sinews, liver and gallbladder; stretch the neck and shoulders, retire early, and avoid late-night feasting and carousing.

Folklore

Among today's inauspicious spirits stands the name Xiánchí 咸池 — but what is it, exactly? Xiánchí began as the name of a celestial constellation: in ancient star-lore it marked the palace of the imperial consorts and the place where the sun bathes, a gathering of waters and tangled affections — which is why later date-selection lore calls it the Peach Blossom Sha 桃花煞: on a day when Xiánchí presides, clandestine romances, tangled entanglements and quiet losses of fortune tend to gather, and the old rules accordingly place marriage on the unfavorable list of a Xiánchí day. Today's list agrees: besides Xiánchí, Wǎngwáng 往亡 bodes ill for journeys and Wǔlí 五离 portends separation among kin — with all three present, weddings and long journeys alike are best deferred. Curiously, though Xiánchí is ranked an inauspicious spirit, folk custom does not wholly condemn it: in destiny-reading, a strong Peach Blossom often means charm and fine company; the trouble of Xiánchí lies not in having many affections but in keeping them in the dark — open and upright, it becomes good company; covert and furtive, it becomes scandal. That is the deeper lesson the almanac leaves in this spirit: matters of the heart should be conducted in the open and in good order. If today moves you to declare affection or settle a lifelong bond, better to set a proper date and observe due ceremony than to pass your feelings along in secret — that is the true way of seeking fortune and avoiding harm.
