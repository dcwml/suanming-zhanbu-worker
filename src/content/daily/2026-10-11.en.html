Metaphysics Insight

Among today's inauspicious spirits sits one of particular charm — Fù Rì 复日, the Repeated Day. "Repeated" means exactly that: doubling. The old date-selection texts say of this day that "good deeds are doubled in goodness, and dark deeds doubled in darkness": a joyous occasion falling on a Repeated Day carries the promise of double happiness — joy upon joy; but a mournful occasion falling on the same day risks the calamity returning — grief upon grief. That is why the almanac's strictest prohibitions on a Repeated Day all concern funerary matters — note today's avoided column, where funeral processions, burial, grave repairs and tombstones stand listed together. The day is identified by its stem sharing the nature of the month's branch: the ninth month is governed by Xū, Yang Earth, and Wù is likewise Yang Earth — month and day stem double the same qi, so any Wù day of this month is a Repeated Day, as today's Wù Wǔ plainly is. Curiously, the Repeated Day is not simply unlucky: precisely because of its "doubling" nature, matchmakers of old would sometimes seek it out for weddings, courting the omen of "double happiness arriving at the gate". The same spirit is a blessing on one occasion and a taboo on another — that is the essence of date-selection: fortune and misfortune depend on the person and the task. To read the almanac is not to look up one blanket label of lucky or unlucky, but to ask whether a particular undertaking suits this particular day.
