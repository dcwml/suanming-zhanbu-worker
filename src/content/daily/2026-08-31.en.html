Today is Dīng Chǒu 丁丑 — the stem Dīng is yin fire, the fire of lamps and candles, soft and civilized; the branch Chǒu is yin earth, the earth of fields and gardens, bearing great virtue. Fire engenders earth, stem nourishing branch, Dīng fire engendering Chǒu earth, carrying the imagery of "fire and earth engendering each other" — the light of civilization shining upon the great earth, nurturing all things. Its Nayin is Stream-Bottom Water, the clear spring of a mountain ravine, pure and flowing, governing wisdom and circulation. The presiding deity is Míngtáng 明堂, an auspicious spirit of the Yellow Path, governing brightness, integrity, clarity and illumination — a greatly auspicious day. Three auspicious spirits attend together: Yuèdéhé governs widespread virtue and dissolving calamity, Mǔcāng governs abundant harvests and nurturing all things, and Míngtáng governs brightness and integrity with all matters proceeding smoothly. Four matters are banned: opening a market, trading, moving into a house and marriage — all are pioneering and celebratory affairs, inconsistent with the day's solemn and convergent atmosphere. Folklore

Today's presiding deity is Míngtáng, so today's reading is about the lore and meaning of the Bright Hall. Míngtáng was the place where ancient emperors proclaimed their policies and teachings — round above and square below, symbolizing the way of heaven and earth, the sacred ground where the Son of Heaven held court with feudal lords, issued edicts and sacrificed to ancestors. Among the twelve heavenly deities of the almanac, Míngtáng belongs to the Yellow Path of auspicious spirits, governing brightness, integrity, clarity and illumination; on any day when Míngtáng presides, all matters are greatly auspicious, especially sacrifice, construction, burial and breaking ground. The name Míngtáng originates in ancient ritual institutions: the "Record of Rites: Míngtáng Positions" records that Míngtáng was the "temple of the Son of Heaven," where the Duke of Zhou held court with feudal lords, established rites and music, and issued standards of measure, bringing the world into great submission. Later it corresponded with astronomy: the ancients believed there was a "Míngtáng" star official in the Purple Forbidden Enclosure, governing peace under heaven and the flourishing of policy and education. In the study of fate, Míngtáng is often mentioned alongside Yuèdéhé — Yuèdé is the virtue of the greater yin, governing the dissolution of calamity and widespread grace; Yuèdéhé makes this virtue even more manifest, complementing the brightness and integrity of Míngtáng. Thus on a Míngtáng day, there are signs of upright conduct, hidden noble assistance and smooth progress in all matters. Today Míngtáng presides, joined by Yuèdéhé and Mǔcāng — truly a day of "good governance and harmonious people," fit for seizing the auspicious hour to accomplish solemn matters such as sacrifice and burial.
