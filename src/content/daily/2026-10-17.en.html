On a Jiǎ Zǐ day, the first of the sixty — yesterday Guǐ Hài played out the sixtieth and final move of a completed round, and this morning Jiǎ Zǐ opens the new board with move one: the head of the ten stems joined to the head of the twelve branches, the reckoning of days having turned a full circle — a new cycle begun. Jiǎ wood, a tree that towers, sits upon Zǐ water; Zǐ is the direct seal of Jiǎ, a seal at the day's own seat giving life to the stem, like a great tree finding its spring, root-breath self-sufficient. The Na Yin is Gold Within the Sea — gold newly quickened, hidden deep beneath the waves, its treasure-light gathered inward, given to no display. Today the heavenly spirit is the Heavenly Prison on the Black Path — the spirit of confinement and hindrance, with yin vapor holding power; opening a business, travel and all great undertakings of building are set aside. Yet the six auspicious spirits are all of the gentle kind: Heaven's Grace presides over favors from above, for returning thanks and bestowing kindness; the Seasonal Virtue answers the harmony of the four seasons; the People's Day is what the people find fitting, especially favoring the daily work of human feeling; Fortune & Virtue and Universal Protection preside over peace and blessing. Hence today's favorable column holds the affairs of human bonds, household and solemn settling: presenting betrothal gifts and pledging the engagement, the first act of fixing a marriage accord; meeting friends and kin, the ordinary commerce of the heart; bathing, haircuts, cutting garments and the capping and hairpin rites, the tidying of one's person; setting the bed, the ordering of rest; removing and donning mourning garments, opening the vault, moving the coffin, burial and building a tomb in advance — the whole work of the grave brought to its proper peace. The ten avoids each have their spirit behind them: with the Return Taboo in place, travel and moving into a new home are both shunned — the Return Taboo governs journeys out and back, and today one does best to stay by one's own hearth; with Heavenly Fire and the Grand Sha on duty, breaking ground, installing doors, raising the ridgepole and thanking the earth are all set aside, guarding against fire's harm and earth's calamity; the Calamity Sha presides over regret and mischance, so even prayers for blessings can wait; and in the Prison's confining air, opening a business is not the day's work. Read across, the two columns show one fine distinction: betrothal gifts and the pledge stand favorable while marriages stand avoided — the pledge is an accord, the wedding the full rite; the accord may be made, the great ceremony kept for another day. Burial and building a tomb in advance may proceed while repairing graves is avoided — laying a new grave rides on Fortune & Virtue and Universal Protection, but disturbing an old one offends Heavenly Fire and the Grand Sha; the new and the old follow two clearly parted ways.

Zodiac Fortune · Rat 鼠

Today's earthly branch is Zǐ, which corresponds to the Rat — making the Rat the zodiac protagonist of the day. Rat people hold their own branch today, and on Jiǎ Zǐ itself — the head of the sixty, with their natal branch in the seat of the day: Zǐ water, the direct seal, gives life to Jiǎ wood, and the natal branch receives the breath of water feeding wood, its footing naturally firm. The Heavenly Prison's Black Path favors stillness over motion: opening a business and far journeys are both avoided, today is not for grand advances — holding fast and putting things in order come first. Yet betrothal gifts and the pledge stand in the favorable column, exactly fitting the work of accord — negotiating a partnership, fixing an engagement, sealing an agreement; what is signed today is signed on firm ground. Bathing and haircuts stand favorable too: tidying one's person and greeting the new suit the opening of a new cycle. The Joy God and the Wealth God both dwell in the Northeast — for visits, errands and signings, face that direction; with Fortune & Virtue and Universal Protection guarding the household, put the home in order and settle its rest. Only remember: the day clashes with the Horse and the Sha lies South. Those born in the Year of the Horse are clashed today: avoid long journeys south and refrain from contending with Rats. Nine days past Cold Dew, Xu earth holds the season while the water air deepens daily, and Zǐ water gains as metal's breath grows — keep the waist and belly warm, sleep early, tend well the water of your natal branch, and let the new board unfold unhurried.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday's closing lines spoke of the almanac arranging every day into a living game of go, advancing and retiring — and last night Guǐ Hài closed a board exactly: Guǐ Hài is the sixtieth of the sixty, the final move; this morning Jiǎ Zǐ comes down, and the count begins again at one. This "first stone" deserves a closer look. Pair the ten heavenly stems with the twelve earthly branches — yang stems with yang branches, yin with yin — and the least common multiple of ten and twelve is sixty: from "Jiǎ Zǐ" to "Guǐ Hài" forms one full circuit, called "one jiǎzǐ," sixty years. Tradition says the Yellow Emperor charged his minister Dá Náo to fathom the affinities of the five phases and observe the pivot of the Dipper, and so first made the stems and branches; archaeology supplies harder proof — a complete table of the sixty, carved on the oracle bones of Yǐn Ruins. The Shang reckoned their days by the cycle, one cell per day, unbroken round upon round, more than three thousand years without interruption — the longest known sequence of dated days on earth. From the Eastern Han the stems and branches were used for years as well, and with year, month, day and hour all carried in the cycle, the very foundation of the later Four Pillars was laid from coil upon coil of the jiǎzǐ; and the "sixty years of the flowery cycle" that name a person's sixtieth birthday come from this same round. The ancients honored Jiǎ Zǐ above all: Jiǎ is the head of the ten stems, yang wood that towers, the urge of growth; Zǐ is the head of the twelve branches, yang water returning to the sea, the source of storing-up — a day whose stem and branch are both firsts carries the image of all things made new, and custom held it fit for sweeping out the old. Yet today's heavenly spirit happens to be the Heavenly Prison, the Black Path rising again after the Jade Hall's Yellow Path — and here the almanac's two systems stand revealed: the stems and branches circle in sixty, the twelve spirits in twelve, each turning its own ring, running side by side on the same day, their auspice weighed together. The new cycle has opened, but the Prison counsels "in a new game, steady before swift" — so on this first of the sixty, what stands favorable are the binding and tidying works of pledges, betrothals, bathing and haircuts, while the grand undertakings of markets, journeys, breaking ground and ridgepoles all stand avoided. A new cycle begun does not mean all things attempted at once: first settle the person, then establish the life; first straighten the cap and gown, then plan the far road. That is the lesson of a Jiǎ Zǐ day wearing the Heavenly Prison.
