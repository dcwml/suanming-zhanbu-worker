Acquiring property 置产 · Setting up the bed 安床

On a Wù Zǐ day, Wù is yang earth — earth of ramparts and embankments; Zǐ hides within it the guǐ water of midnight's wide waters — earth overcoming water, that water becomes the day master's wealth: seated in the land of wealth, it wears by nature the look of one who holds the soil and steers the flood. Yet the land of wealth is also the land of wasting: in the inauspicious list Dàbài 大败 governs depletion and Dàshí 大时 governs dissipation, so the seated-wealth master must all the more guard his wealth — the avoided column writes two matters, acquiring property and setting up the bed, and that is exactly the sense of it: heavy assets of field and house may wait today, large sums stay put, and the bed keeps its old place. The day god in the twelve-spirits round is Báihǔ 白虎, the White Tiger — a Black Path day; yet four auspicious spirits stand guard in force, a lineup seldom seen of late: Guānrì 官日 favors the public door and public affairs — reporting, interviews and signings all gain the edge; Tiānmǎ 天马 governs swift galloping — journeys, relocations and taking up posts all ride its strength; Jíqī 吉期 is the auspicious hour of all matters, weddings and celebrations exactly in season; Yào'ān 要安 governs peace and smoothness, anchoring every affair — four auspicious ones together press down the White Tiger's killing air, and the favorable column opens seventeen matters wide: from marriages and journeys to breaking ground and moving in, from construction and planting to burial and mourning garments, all things may proceed — one of the roomiest days of recent weeks. The Na Yin is Thunderbolt Fire — fire of lightning hidden between soil and water, bursting forth in answer to the word "movement," calling in kind with the Heavenly Horse's gallop and the favorable column's breadth: today's momentum lies in moving, not in keeping — matters long dragged, works long stalled may take a push today. Xiánchí 咸池, the peach-blossom spirit, stands in the same list as the White Tiger, and setting up the bed avoids it above all — Xiánchí governs private passions of the bed and the burden of wine and excess, and a newlywed bed chooses a luckier day still. Clash the Horse, the Sha lies South: Zǐ and Wǔ stand opposed — and between the year pillar Bǐng Wǔ and the day pillar Wù Zǐ this same Zǐ-Wǔ line stretches too — keep clear of contention and partnership with Horse people, and let southern journeys take a slower step.

Zodiac Fortune · Rat 鼠

Today's earthly branch is Zǐ, which corresponds to the Rat — making the Rat the protagonist of the day, and this is the Rat's own watch in the round of branches: in the deep hours of Zǐ, the Rat takes the night shift. The White Tiger of the Black Path presses from above, yet the auspicious spirits stand unusually ready: Tiānmǎ 天马 governs swift galloping, so the Rat moves with luck today — journeys, moves, job changes and running errands all go smoothly, and matters long stalled may take a push forward. Guānrì 官日 attends, and public affairs flow the smoother — interviews, reports to superiors and dealings at official windows all find help. Jíqī 吉期 is the auspicious hour of all matters — weddings, engagements and celebrations may pick this day. Yào'ān 要安 governs calm and smoothness: the mind rests steady, and household affairs proceed in their order. The White Tiger stands in the list: keep one extra guard against quarrels and accidental knocks — drive by the rules and take it slow, and be the more careful returning late at night. Acquiring property and setting up the bed are avoided: sign no large contract in land or house today, move no large savings, and let the bed keep its old place — no need to turn its facing. Clash the Horse, the Sha lies South: Zǐ and Wǔ stand opposed — avoid partnerships and quarrels with Horse people, and let southern journeys take a slower step. In the body, Wù earth answers the spleen and stomach, Zǐ water the kidneys and bladder — with water's season deepening after Start of Winter, damp earth burdens the spleen: keep food warm and gentle, take little that is cold and raw, and refuse the late night: at midnight's Zǐ hour the first stirring of yang is born — sleep early, and let that spark of light from the Rat's bite of chaos fall into your own rest.

Metaphysics Insight

Today is a Wù Zǐ day with the Rat on watch — so let us tell why the Rat stands first among the twelve zodiac animals. The twelve begin with Zǐ, and that begins with the Zǐ hour: the ancients divided the day-and-night into twelve double-hours, each named with an earthly branch, and the midnight stretch from eleven at night to one in the morning is the Zǐ hour, first of the twelve — for midnight is both the day's end and the day's beginning: the tail of yesterday and the head of today lie stacked in the same hour, and yin and yang cross at exactly this point. For the pairing of animals and branches, the folk's most widely told account is the tale of even and uneven toes, set down in detail in the Ming's Qi Xiu Lei Gao (《七修类稿》): beasts' toes run in odd and even counts, odd belonging to yang and even to yin — and the Rat has four toes on its forefeet (even, yin) and five on its hind feet (odd, yang), one body holding odd and even together, the very image of the crossing of yin and yang in the Zǐ hour; so the Rat stands first, paired with Zǐ. After it the Ox bears four toes, the Tiger five, the Hare four… alternating in turn, matching the yin-yang order of the twelve branches one by one, arranged into a perfect circle. The folk tell another tale besides — "the Rat bites open heaven": when heaven and earth were not yet divided, chaos hung whole like an egg; it was the rat of the Zǐ hour that bit the chaos open, letting out one thread of light — yang qi began to rise, and heaven and earth first split apart — "the Zǐ Rat opens heaven, the Chǒu Ox cleaves the earth," and so the zodiac takes the Rat for its head. One account speaks of yin and yang, the other of the world's first breaking-open; the two stand side by side, and together they lift this unremarkable little beast into the seating of the gods of beginnings. And so to today's almanac: day and hour each carry a Zǐ, two stacked together, with the Heavenly Horse urging movement and all matters on the favorable list — when midnight's affairs are done, sleep early and sound, and invite this odd-and-even creature of one body into your dream: rest the spirit well, and tomorrow the Heavenly Horse sets out again.
