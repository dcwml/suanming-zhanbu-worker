Today is only suitable for "removing the old" such as dissolving past troubles or demolishing broken walls; it is not suitable for any "establishing the new" such as opening for business, marriage, moving into a house or signing contracts. All major matters should be postponed until another day.

Metaphysics Insight

"No other matters" (馀事勿取 Yúshì wù qǔ) is one of the most special terms in traditional almanacs. Literally it means "do not undertake any remaining matters." It forms a subtle pairing with "Nothing is suitable" (诸事不宜 Zhūshì bùyí) — when the "Yi" column lists only a handful of items while the "Ji" column states that nothing is suitable, "No other matters" actually defines an extremely narrow safe zone: one should only do the explicitly listed items, and all other activities fall under taboo. This term originates from the ancient date-selection system of the "Twelve Deities" (建除十二神 Jiànchú shí'èr shén) and the heavenly spirits' auspiciousness system. The "Yi" and "Ji" entries in almanacs are not randomly listed but are derived from comprehensive calculations based on the day's stem-branch, heavenly spirits, auspicious and inauspicious spirits. When a day's inauspicious forces outweigh the auspicious, or when the day branch clashes with the month or year branch, the stern warning "Nothing is suitable" appears. At such times, even the few "Yi" items are merely "expedient measures" — for example, "Dissolving bindings" can remove old troubles, and "Demolishing walls" can tear down dangerous structures; both are "removing the old" rather than "establishing the new." The ancients believed that on days with chaotic qi, any creative act is prone to obstruction due to the lack of harmony between heaven and earth; it is better to retreat and preserve, waiting patiently for an auspicious day. Today on Gēng Yín, Metal and Wood clash while Jiéshà and Xiǎohào arrive together — precisely such a day where stillness is favored over movement, and breaking down is favored over building up.
