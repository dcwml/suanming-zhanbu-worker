On a Yǐ Mǎo day, Yǐ wood sits upon Mǎo — the branch of its Lin Guan prosperity, where the stem draws its very salary — so stem and branch stand as one body of wood. The Na Yin is Great Stream Water: a stream running a thousand li, watering wood from an ample source. Yet autumn metal is in command, and Yǐ Mǎo, like yesterday's Jiǎ Yín, falls squarely under the autumn Four Abandonments — stem and branch alike sunk in decline. Hence the favored column holds only sacrifice and funerary matters, sealed with the note "no other matters"; and the avoided column is empty — not because everything is permitted, but because on an Abandonment day nothing else may rise, so the ban dwells silently in the list itself. Gouchen, a Black Path spirit of delay and entanglement, holds the day; yet the Six Harmonies and the Five Harmonies favor concord, the Sacred Heart bestows mercy, and the Cockcrow Pair presides over the netherworld — so encoffinment, moving the coffin, breaking earth, opening the vault, burial, removing and donning mourning clothes all find their strength, and even preparing a tomb in one's lifetime accords with the Cockcrow Pair. Today Cold Dew arrives and the month's command passes from Yǒu to Xū: metal qi sharpens, the dew nears frost — a day for stillness and gathering, for remembrance and rites; all else may well wait.

Metaphysics Insight

Attentive readers will notice that today's Month Pillar has changed from yesterday's Dīng Yǒu 丁酉 to Wù Xū 戊戌 — and the switch is none other than today's Cold Dew. The ganzhi calendar reckons months by solar terms, not by lunar month-days: Lì Chūn opens the Yín month, Jīng Zhé the Mǎo month, and so on in order; the month governed by Cold Dew and Frost's Descent corresponds to Xū. The Month Pillar follows the term, not the day — from the moment the term arrives, the month's command changes for the whole world. This is what fate-reading and date-selection call the "month command": a child born before today's Cold Dew bears the Dīng Yǒu month pillar, while one born a few hours after the term bears Wù Xū — a difference of mere hours, yet the month's command is already another matter. The basis lies in the sun: the ecliptic longitude advances fifteen degrees with each term, and Cold Dew stands at the 195th degree; the ancients fixed such moments by measuring the gnomon's shadow and watching the Dipper's handle, fine to the quarter-hour. The "Seventy-Two Explanations of the Monthly Ordinances" says of Cold Dew: "The ninth-month term: the dew grows cold, and soon will congeal." White Dew's dew is white and newly formed; Cold Dew's dew is cold and near frozen — one character apart, and heaven and earth have passed from cool into cold. Its three pentads: first, the guest wild geese arrive — the last formations winging south; second, sparrows enter the great waters and become clams — as birds vanish, clam shells crowd the shore; third, chrysanthemums put forth yellow blooms — the one flower to open against the cold. The farming proverb runs: "At White Dew, bare not your body; at Cold Dew, bare not your feet" — past today, sleep no longer with bare feet against the chill.
