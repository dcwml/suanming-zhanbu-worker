On a Wù Xū day, Wù is yang earth — the earth of city walls and of the open ground; Xū is yin earth, the close of autumn's harvest, the gate through which the fire qi returns to its storehouse — so Wù earth seated on Xū is earth upon earth, thick and settled. Yet the Na Yin falls on Wood on the Plain 平地木 — Wù Xū and Jǐ Hài share it: the wood of the open plain, not the towering timber of crags — a tree growing of itself on flat country, matching no peak for height and making its own broad shade. Thick earth, and wood rising from it: after the earth's virtue of carrying all things comes a quiet life of its own. The day god arrives at Jīnkuì 金匮, the Golden Cabinet — a Yellow Path spirit of fortune: of the six Yellow Path gods — the Azure Dragon, the Bright Hall, the Golden Cabinet, Heavenly Virtue, the Jade Hall and the Messenger of Fate — today the Golden Cabinet holds the day. The Golden Cabinet is Heaven's coffer of gold, the storehouse that holds treasure and seals documents: it governs marriage, receiving wealth, and keeping wealth — and the Golden Cabinet delights in "storing," not "scattering": money entering the cabinet grows firm, money leaving it grows thin, and this one word decides how today's avoided list is read. The auspicious pair: Yìhòu 益后, the Guardian of Descendants, the spirit that shelters children's children — setting the marriage bed and making bed curtains, all matters of continuing the line, stand squarely under its care; and the Golden Cabinet itself, presiding, already told. The favorable list numbers eight: making bed curtains, cutting garments, marriages, setting the marriage bed — four matters in one unbroken string, a complete course of wedding preparations: first the festive garments are cut, then the joyous curtains sewn, the rite held, the new bed set — all complete in a single day, a full-lucky marriage day rare in the whole month, with the Guardian of Descendants seeing the match made and the line continued, and the Golden Cabinet seeing the betrothal gifts fill the coffer: two spirits attending the wedding together. Placing in the coffin, moving the coffin, breaking ground — three matters of the grave: Xū is the tomb-storehouse of the twelve branches, the gate where fire qi returns to its vault; on a tomb-storehouse day the coffin is moved and the earth opened, and each thing finds its proper place. Marriage and the grave's work favorable on one day — one for the house of the living, one for the house of the dead, each keeping its own hour: this is the almanac's fine art of "matter by matter." Building livestock pens: the Guardian of Descendants governs increase, and thriving livestock is increase too, squarely under its shelter. The avoided list numbers five: acquiring property — buying land and houses: the Five Empties stand at hand, a day when the seasonal qi does not arrive, and wealth paid out in one great sum buys an estate hollow and unripe; and the Golden Cabinet keeps rather than scatters — today money belongs in the cabinet, not out of it: acquiring property stands avoided on precisely this logic. Yuèshà 月煞 and Yuèxū 月虚, the Month's Bane and the Month's Emptiness, govern hidden loss and secret wasting — building boats stands avoided on these two: a great work and a great outlay, hollow and unripe; let it wait a day. Xuèzhī 血支, the Blood Branch, governs blood and the needle's cut — needling, bloodletting, anything that breaks the skin waits today; cutting garments does move the shears, yet it is a small matter of cloth, guarded by the two lucky spirits, and does not fall under this prohibition. The Five Empties sit again on well-digging: a well draws on the seasonal qi for its spring, and a well dug on an empty day fills thin and hollow — the well waits. Juéyáng 绝阳, the Severed Yang, marks the broken breath between one yang and the next — consecration stands avoided on precisely this count: consecrating an image calls the living yang down into it, and on a day of severed yang there is no yang to draw — opened, yet without light. Building the stove is avoided too: Xū is the fire storehouse, the gate where flame goes to its tomb, and to build a stove today is to seat fire inside the vault — the stove-fire will not burn bright. The old folk rule "no stove on a Xū day" comes from exactly this image. Clash the Dragon, the Sha lies North: Chén and Xū stand opposed — with Dragon people today, guard against contention above all: the Golden Cabinet holds a day of weddings and gladness, and no quarrel should be allowed to spoil it; northern journeys take a slower step. Minor Snow two days out (Nov 22): the Hài month's water swells and winter's storing deepens — the plain's wood does not vie in height, and the golden coffer does not show its coin: today's work favors gathering, storing, keeping low — that is the measure of a Wù Xū day.

Cutting garments and making bed curtains stand favorable too: tailoring, dresswork and home arranging may go forward freely. In love, marriages and setting the marriage bed stand favorable together, with the Guardian of Descendants governing the line: proposals, registrations, weddings and setting up the bridal room — all month's-best choices today; the Golden Cabinet itself governs marriage, and for the Dog this is the natal day meeting the wedding day, a double gladness. Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Rooster of Yǒu showed how Yǒu was first a wine jar; today the Dog of Xū takes up the graph Xū — and it is a great axe. In the oracle-bone script, Xū is drawn as a long-hafted weapon with a broad, outward-curving blade, nearly the same shape as 戊 Wù, differing by one stroke of the edge — Wù is the axe of the wide blade, Xū the axe of the wider one; and the family has two more kin: 戚 Qī, the axe hung with toothed notches, and 钺 Yuè, the great ceremonial axe, emblem of kingship. Wù, Xū, Qī and Yuè share one root: all four are axes. The axe was weapon, instrument of justice, and the token of military command — "to bear the yuè" meant to hold the army, and in the Book of Documents King Wu goes out with three hundred war chariots, his left hand resting on the yellow axe: that yellow yuè is the pledge of Heaven's mandate itself. The Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字 says: "Xū means 'to perish' 灭. In the ninth month the yang qi grows faint, all things reach completion, and the yang descends into the earth." The sense has already turned from the axe's edge to the season's turn: autumn ends, winter begins, the yang qi sinks below the ground, and the harvest goes into the storehouse — the axe into its sheath, the grain into the granary, the fire into its vault (Xū is precisely the fire storehouse). Today the Golden Cabinet holds the day, and the lid it lifts and closes is the lid of this very season. Why does the Dog pair with Xū, the hours of seven to nine in the evening? It is the meeting of dusk and settling night: the sun wholly gone, the dark not yet deep — folk knock off, come home, bar the door and light the lamp, and the dog begins the night's patrol — the dog is the household's first watch-drum, clocking on at the Xū hours: the beast that keeps the gate mated to the branch that keeps the night. Then the tale that belongs to the dog above all — "the Yellow Ear carries the letter" 黄耳传书: from the Book of Jin, the biography of Lù Jī. Lù Jī was a man of Huáting in Wú commandery — today's Songjiang, Shanghai — a talent long detained in Luòyáng on office, his letters home to Jiāngnán long cut off. One day he said in jest to his beloved dog, Yellow Ear: "No letter has come from my house. Can you carry one and bring back word?" The dog wagged its tail and answered with a bark. Lù Jī wrote the letter, sealed it in a bamboo tube, and strapped it to the dog's neck. Yellow Ear foraged for himself along the way, raced south down the post roads, crossed the River, and reached home, carrying the tube in his jaws and crying the news; the family opened the tube and read — and the dog barked again, as if asking something more: so they wrote a reply, sealed it in the tube, and the dog bore it back to Luòyáng. Once in ten days he made the journey, and it became a standing thing. And when Lù Jī died, Yellow Ear carried earth for his mound, cried out, and died of grief. Later ages took "Yellow Ear" as the very name of a messenger, paired with "the wild goose bears the letter": the goose carries the thoughts of the far-off, but Yellow Ear carries the voice of home. Today the Golden Cabinet presides — a golden coffer, like a letter sealed in bamboo: stored, yet able to pass between hands. The Five Empties stand at hand and property stands avoided — a great estate bought on this day is hollow and unripe, and less substantial than a single letter home. The Dog of Xū keeps the night watch, and what it keeps is the golden coffer's door: today's dog is best at going home, at carrying letters, and at putting everything worth keeping into the cabinet.
