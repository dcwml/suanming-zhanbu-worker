Consecration 开光 · Planting 栽种

On a Xīn Yǒu day, Xīn metal sits at its own salary ground in Yǒu — with the ninth month's Xū earth engendering metal, the metal qi is in season, at its post, pure and strong — while the Na Yin is Pomegranate Wood, wood conceived within metal, strength graced with refinement. Today the Heavenly Virtue holds the Yellow Path, and more: in the ninth month both the Heavenly and the Monthly Virtue rest on Bǐng, and Bǐng joins hands with Xīn — so the Union with the Heavenly Virtue and the Union with the Monthly Virtue descend together, in the words of the old texts, "the day they attend gathers a hundred blessings, and every evil scatters of itself." The Official's Day favors public business and documents; the Remover presides over clearing away the old — sweeping the house, mending walls and the rites of mourning fall to its office; the Treasured Light blesses weddings, setting the bed and making the stove; and with the Crying Hound in attendance, every rite of burial — encoffining, moving the coffin, breaking earth, opening the grave, burial itself — finds its favor: a rare day in the year for laying the dead to rest. The inauspicious list — the Month's Harm, the Heavenly Officer, the Deadly, the Blood Branch and the Five Departures — stands feeble beneath the twofold Virtue. What alone is forbidden: consecration and planting — the Five Departures' parting breath ill suits a seedling's taking root, while the Blood Branch and the Deadly bar rites of blood and spirit-invocation, so consecration keeps to another day.

Metaphysics Insight

The spirits seated at the head of today's auspicious list are Tiān Dé Hé 天德合, the Union with the Heavenly Virtue, and Yuè Dé Hé 月德合, the Union with the Monthly Virtue. Virtue, dé, shares its sense with "to obtain" — what each thing rightly receives from Heaven is its virtue. Take the Monthly Virtue first: it is the thriving stem of the month's triple-harmony season — for the Yín–Wǔ–Xù months the stem is Bǐng, for Shēn–Zǐ–Chén it is Rén, for Hài–Mǎo–Wèi it is Jiǎ, for Sì–Yǒu–Chǒu it is Gēng. The logic lies in the life-gates: Bǐng fire is born at Yín, thrives at Wǔ and is laid to rest at Xù — one breath flowing through the whole fire season, so the ninth month's Monthly Virtue belongs to Bǐng; Rén is born at Shēn, Jiǎ at Hài, Gēng at Sì — one principle throughout. The Heavenly Virtue, meanwhile, keeps a monthly rota — Dīng in the first month, Rén the third, Xīn the fourth, Jiǎ the sixth, Bǐng the ninth, Yǐ the tenth — moving with each month's pillar. In the ninth month the Heavenly Virtue, too, rests on Bǐng. A "union" is the day whose stem joins the virtue-stem in harmony: Bǐng and Xīn unite — so every Xīn day under a Xū month receives both unions at once, as today. The old texts say of the virtue spirits: "where they govern, evils melt away; the day they attend, a hundred blessings gather." Hence days of union are fit for building, marrying and worship — even hemmed in by dark spirits, those can do little harm. Yesterday's Post-Horse showed the triple harmony at its clash point, breath spurred into motion; today's twofold Virtue shows it at its life-gate, breath gathered into blessing — motion and nurture, the two faces of the almanac.
