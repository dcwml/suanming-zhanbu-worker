Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the Jade Hall's noble mansion took its seal; today the Heavenly Prison stands in session — the Yellow Path's noble hall is followed at once by the Black Path's jail, and the twelve sky-spirits' rotation yields to no one's feelings. The Black Path's six — Tiānxíng 天刑, Zhūquè 朱雀, Báihǔ 白虎, Tiānláo 天牢, Xuánwǔ 玄武 and Gōuchén 勾陈 — each presides over severity and restraint, and the Heavenly Prison's name is the most literal of all: there are jails in heaven too. In the old star-charts, six stars rank beneath the bowl of the Northern Dipper, named the Heavenly Prison, charged with restraining the violent; and within the bowl four more stars, Tiānlǐ 天理, are called "the prison of the noble" — the human world has its jails, and heaven's officialdom keeps the same establishments, its very prisons sorted into noble and common ranks. The day-choosers took this image into the calendar: on a Heavenly Prison day, confinement, obstruction, lawsuits and penal affairs preside, and the old almanacs read it as a day for stillness and keeping in. Yet today's almanac offers a most instructive sight: with the Prison in session, the favored list opens twenty-two undertakings — opening businesses and releasing wealth, betrothal pledges, moving house and setting beds, burial and mourning laid aside, nothing withheld — while the avoided list carries a bare five entries. The prison gates stand wide — has the calendar made an error? It has not: the virtues hold the field. Five days ago the story was of the Union with the Heavenly Virtue and the Union with the Monthly Virtue: a virtue-spirit is like a high official who cannot attend every day in person; when the union-spirit arrives, it is as if an envoy rode ahead to carry word. Today the officials themselves arrive in full — the Heavenly Virtue rhyme runs "in the ninth month at Bǐng, in the tenth at Yǐ": the ninth month (the Xū month) keeps its Heavenly Virtue at Bǐng, and today's day stem is exactly Bǐng; the Monthly Virtue is taken by the trines, "in Yín-Wǔ-Xū months at Bǐng," so the Xū month's Monthly Virtue too is Bǐng — a double virtue in one stem; and the Seasonal Virtue takes its seat by the seasons, "in spring at Wǔ, in summer at Chén, in autumn at Zǐ, in winter at Yín" — autumn's seat is Zǐ, and the day branch is exactly Zǐ. Heavenly Virtue and Monthly Virtue on the stem, Seasonal Virtue on the branch — stem and branch both crowned, three virtues arriving within one day: in the day-choosers' eyes, the very top of the arrangements. The Canon of Times and Taboos, Examined and Harmonized, says that where virtue stands, "the many maligns submit of themselves" — if an envoy's carried word could put the maligns to flight, what of a hall full of the officials themselves? The Heavenly Prison is staffed, but its gate hangs open; the Disaster Sha, the Great Sha, the Return Taboo and the Water-Dragon Toucher ring the grounds and all lie hidden beneath the light of virtue — and five auspicious spirits attend in support: the People's Day, the Heavenly Shaman, Blessed Virtue, Universal Protection and the Crowing-and-Barking Answer, each with its own charge — the people's affairs, worship, fortune, shelter and burial. A Black Path day in the old almanac has never been a synonym for "nothing may be done": the sky-spirits' rotation is the surface, the contest of spirits is the substance — a Black Path day on which every gate stands open is no slip of the calendar, but the visible hand of virtue holding the field. Five days ago a Xīnwèi day told of the unions; today a Bǐngzǐ day tells of the officials' arrival — envoy and principal, two lessons in succession; as for the day the Commander of Destiny comes to close the Yellow Path's roster — that is a tale for days to come.
