Folklore

Today is a Month-Broken day (Yuèpò 月破) in the Chinese almanac. What does that mean? The twelve earthly branches stand in opposing pairs — Yín against Shēn, Mǎo against Yǒu, Zǐ against Wǔ — forming the "six clashes". When the branch of the current day clashes with the branch of the current month, the day is called Month-Broken: the day that breaks the month. Today is a Yín day within a Shēn month, Yín facing Shēn directly, hence the name. Month-Broken days rank among the most inauspicious in the almanac; old calendars typically mark them "nothing is suitable", allowing only demolition work — breaking down houses, tearing down walls — on the auspicious side: the qi of breaking may be spent on breaking, but if spent on building, it rebounds to harm. Here lies a layer of the old almanac's wisdom — before raising a new house one must clear the old ground, so on a day of dispersal and breakage, sweeping away old tasks, old habits and old grudges gives the "breaking" of Month-Broken something useful to break. Nor is a Month-Broken day wholly bereft of favor: today's auspicious spirits include Jiěshén 解神 and Wǔhé 五合, a reminder that the almanac's verdicts always leave a door of relief open.
