On a Yǐ Yǒu day, Yǐ wood sits upon Yǒu metal — metal cuts wood, and the day master itself wears the look of restraint. The day's officer in the twelve-officers round is "Open" — an Open day would ordinarily welcome ventures and undertakings, yet four inauspicious spirits stand ranged about it — Zāishà the Calamity Star, Tiānhuǒ the Heavenly Fire, Wǔlí the Five Partings and Zhūquè the Vermilion Bird — and the blessings cannot outweigh the harms, so the favored column holds only one matter, dispelling and releasing, with all else set aside. The avoided column is empty — not because everything is permitted, but because the four characters "no other matters" have gathered every ban into themselves: Wǔlí governs partings, so marriage, alliance and far journeys stand aside of themselves; Tiānhuǒ governs conflagration — as winter hearths and heaters wake, keep careful watch over flame and wire; Zāishà governs calamity — let no risky bravado be attempted today. The auspicious spirits, meanwhile, stand ninefold, headed by Tiāndé, the Heavenly Virtue of noble help that untangles misfortune — the hardest of backers for the very word "releasing"; Chú Shén, the Spirit of Sweeping, presides over clearing away the old, answering "dispelling" at its root; Shēngqì and Shèngxīn govern quickening and blessed prayer; Mǔcāng, the Mother's Granary, keeps granary and table — "mend the winter on Start of Winter," the folk saying goes, and a warming meal is exactly the day's propriety; and though Míng Wèi has to do with the netherworld, no funerary matter opens in the favored column — the day plainly means sweeping out the old to welcome the new. Today Start of Winter arrives and the month's command passes from Wù Xū to Jǐ Hài — the season of water opens its reign; yesterday, Jiǎ Shēn, was the day before the term, the ancient "Four Severances," when autumn metal's qi expires — and across these two days the Na Yin keeps one single name, Spring Middle Water: well-spring water that stays warm through winter, never freezing, born to answer the term. Clash the Rabbit, the Sha lies East: Mǎo and Yǒu stand opposed — keep clear of contention and partnership with Rabbit people, and let eastern journeys take a slower step.

Metaphysics Insight

Today is Start of Winter — one of the Four Beginnings (立春, 立夏, 立秋, 立冬), which with the two equinoxes and two solstices make the Eight Nodes, the great hinges of heaven and earth. The annotation to the seventy-two pentads says: "Winter is the end — the time when the ten thousand things are gathered in." The "beginning" founds the season; "winter" is the closing and the storing — spring bears, summer grows, autumn harvests, winter stores, and at this hinge heaven and earth carry the year's qi into the granary. The three pentads of Start of Winter read: first, the waters begin to ice — the surface sets thin and not yet firm; second, the earth begins to freeze — the soil stiffens yet does not crack; third, the pheasant enters the great water and becomes the clam — the ancients, watching pheasants grow scarce as great patterned clams grew fat by the shore, romantically concluded that the wild birds had slipped into the river and turned to clams — and inside that phenology hides the storing imagination of winter: feathered things sinking into the water, yang sinking into hiding while yin ascends. The old rites kept this day with gravity: the Book of Rites records that on Start of Winter the Son of Heaven led the three ministers, the nine Excellencies and the grandees to the northern suburbs to welcome winter, and on returning rewarded the families of those fallen in service and gave relief to orphans and widows — the welcoming of winter carried mercy in the same breath, for winter tests every living thing, and the season begins by tending those most in need of warmth. In almanac terms, Start of Winter is the hinge where the month pillar changes: Wù Xū gives way to Jǐ Hài, the season of water takes command, and even the round of the twelve spirits reshuffles with the new month — which is why the Vermilion Bird mounts the Black Path again today; and yesterday, Jiǎ Shēn, the day before the term, was one of the ancient "Four Severances," when autumn metal's qi expires. The grace note is that across these two days the Na Yin keeps one name — Spring Middle Water: well-spring water, warm through winter, never freezing, inexhaustible — the finest footnote to the character "store": storing is not sealing shut, but drawing in and keeping. And so the favored column holds only "dispelling and releasing — no other matters," which is precisely what winter-storing looks like written into the almanac: sweep the clutter out, gather yourself in, and wait quietly on the nourishment of the water's season.
