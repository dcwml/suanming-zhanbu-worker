Folklore

Today's presiding deity is Qīnglóng, so today's reading is about the lore and meaning of the Azure Dragon. Qīnglóng is the foremost of the Four Symbols, the god of the East, associated with the wood element, the color azure and the image of the dragon — hence the name Azure Dragon. Among the twelve heavenly deities of the almanac, Qīnglóng belongs to the Yellow Path of auspicious spirits, governing joy, nobility, smooth progress and wealth; on any day when Qīnglóng presides, all matters are greatly auspicious, especially marriage, opening a business, travel and construction. The name Qīnglóng originates in ancient astronomy: the ancients divided the constellations near the ecliptic into four groups, and the seven eastern mansions — Jiǎo, Kàng, Dī, Fáng, Xīn, Wěi and Jī — when connected form the shape of a dragon, hence called the Azure Dragon. Later matched with the five elements, the East belongs to wood and wood's color is azure, so the name was fixed as Qīnglóng. In the Taoist pantheon, Qīnglóng is a guardian deity protecting the East; in feng shui, the Qīnglóng position refers to the left side of a dwelling, governing nobility, male offspring and career — the so-called "left Qīnglóng, right Báihǔ" — and the Qīnglóng side should be high and winding, avoiding low collapse or damage. The Qīnglóng deity in the almanac, though not the Four-Symbols Qīnglóng itself, takes its imagery of joy and celebration: the dragon is chief among scaled creatures, able to be hidden or manifest, small or great, short or long, ascending to heaven at the spring equinox and diving into the abyss at the autumn equinox, symbolizing infinite change and free rising and falling. Thus on a Qīnglóng day, there are hidden signs of noble assistance and joyous arrival. Today Qīnglóng presides, joined by Sānhé, Fúshēng and other auspicious spirits — truly a day of "dragon and phoenix bringing prosperity," fit for seizing the auspicious hour to accomplish great matters.
