Today is Bǐng Yín 丙寅 — the stem Bǐng is yang fire, the sun at high noon, blazing and brilliant; the branch Yín is yang wood, and wood feeds fire, so the flames have their root, while the Nayin itself is Furnace Fire: stem and branch alike turn into fire, and today's fire qi may be called flourishing indeed. Yet the season stands in a Shēn month, and Yín clashes head-on with Shēn, making today a Month-Broken (Yuèpò 月破) day — the day's branch clashes with the month's branch, the qi scatters and will not gather, and so the day is reckoned among the great drains. That is why today's favors lean entirely toward breaking and healing: breaking down houses, tearing down walls and treating illness are favorable, while the almanac's own verdict reads "all other matters best avoided"; praying for blessings, accepting betrothal gifts, pledging betrothal, marriage, moving into a house and burial all stand on the unfavorable list — nothing that raises or begins anything is in season. The presiding deity Tiānxíng 天刑 rules the Black Path, and Yuèpò 月破, Dàhào 大耗 and Yuèxíng 月刑 assemble among the inauspicious spirits, so the force of clash and break runs heavy; yet among the auspicious spirits Yìmǎ 驿马, Jiěshén 解神 and Wǔhé 五合 still leave a door of relief — Yìmǎ governs movement, but on a Month-Broken day movement is best spent on sweeping out the old rather than launching a great journey; Jiěshén governs resolution, and treating illness to rid the body of ailment answers precisely to the way of "building by breaking." If you have long meant to clear out old clutter, repair an aging home or seek medicine for a lingering complaint, ride today's momentum and make a clean sweep of it; for weddings, housewarmings, grand openings and contract signings, choose another day.

Zodiac Fortune · Tiger

Today's branch is Yín (寅), which corresponds to the Tiger — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Tiger year sit squarely on the day's own branch and so receive today's qi most directly: Bǐng fire is fed by Yín wood and stands at its place of birth, so spirit and vigor are by no means weak; but the day branch clashes with the month command Shēn metal, and with Yuèpò 月破 and Dàhào 大耗 both on duty, the Tiger's qi today scatters rather than gathers — Shēn month is, moreover, the very month that clashes with Tigers, so the whole month carries a tremor of its own, and today especially favors standing still and holding fast. At work, launching new projects, signing contracts or opening for business is all out of step with the day; clearing backlogs, settling leftover problems and mending mistakes — Jiěshén 解神 presides over resolution — on the contrary, turns today's "breaking" to good account. In wealth, Dàhào warns of large losses: hold the purse strings tight, shun big investments and lending, and when tempting speculations call, step back three paces. In love, accepting betrothal gifts, pledging betrothal and marriage all stand on the unfavorable list, so Tigers with wedding plans may proceed unhurried; happily, Wǔhé 五合 sits among the auspicious spirits, so settling old grievances, mending relationships and everyday sociability are all favored. One caution: today clashes with the Monkey and the Sha lies North — avoid long journeys toward the north, and settle any dispute with those born in a Monkey year gently. The Joy God and the Wealth God both stand in the Southwest, the Fortune God in the Northwest — favor these directions when you go out about business. In health, Bǐng Yín is a day of blazing fire, and blazing fire scorches metal and dries the lungs while the last of summer's heat still lingers: Tigers should clear heat and moisten the lungs, go easy on spicy and grilled food, keep early hours and a level temper — a quiet heart settles the qi of itself, the best remedy for a Month-Broken day.

Folklore

Among today's auspicious spirits stands Yìmǎ 驿马 — the Post Horse, perhaps the best-known of all the spirit-stars in traditional date-picking and fate-reading. Its logic grows out of the three harmonies of the five elements and the six clashes of the earthly branches: the water trine Shēn-Zǐ-Chén is born at Shēn, and it is Yín that clashes with Shēn, so the Post Horse of Shēn-Zǐ-Chén stands in Yín; the fire trine Yín-Wǔ-Xū is born at Yín, and it is Shēn that clashes with Yín, so its Post Horse stands in Shēn; the metal trine Sì-Yǒu-Chǒu has its Horse in Hài and the wood trine Hài-Mǎo-Wèi in Sì — all by the same rule: the Post Horse is simply the branch that clashes with the first member of the trine. Date-picking lore holds that "what is clashed is set in motion": in old China the courier posts sped documents along by relays of galloping horses, and only horses kept ceaselessly running could deliver swiftly — hence the branch that moves things took the Post Horse's name, governing travel, relocation, journeys to take up office and the brisk pursuit of fame and fortune. In fate-reading, a Post Horse in one's natal chart betokens a restless, journeying life; when a passing year or a luck cycle clashes the Horse awake, the sign commonly answers as a move of house, a trip abroad, a transfer of post. In date-picking, Post Horse days favor journeys, departures, taking office and removal. The curious thing about today is that Yìmǎ keeps company with Yuèpò 月破: the Post Horse governs movement and Month-Break governs breaking, so today's moving qi is best spent on sweeping out the old and setting in the new — clearing clutter, repairing an aging house, treating a stubborn ailment all answer well, while spending it on a rashly undertaken long journey would likely bring toil without reward. Here lies the old almanac's fine art of weighing fortune: auspicious and inauspicious spirits may stand side by side, and all turns on which outweighs which — and toward what end the movement is aimed.
