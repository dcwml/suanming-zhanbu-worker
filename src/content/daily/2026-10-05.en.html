On a Rén Zǐ day, Rén water sits atop Zǐ water — stem and branch of one kind, twin waters layered upon each other like autumn river swells rolling on; the Na Yin is Mulberry Wood, the mountain mulberry drawing deep from the water, its roots growing only firmer. Siming holds the Yellow Path, with Heavenly Grace, Yang Virtue, the Seasonal Virtue and the People's Day lending support — the underlying tone is smooth and orderly. Yet the Departed Journey bars distant travels outright, the Heavenly Clerk, the Fatal Hour and the Death Spirit menace lawsuits and illness, and the River Chief too ranks among the month's darker spirits; hence the favorable column opens only worship, bathing, wall repairs and road leveling — all small works of cleanliness and care that tidy one's surroundings, with no other matters to undertake. Fasting rites, weddings, relocation, travel, raising beams and moving into a new house should all wait for another day. With Cold Dew near, metal qi gathers strength and the waters begin to still — an auspicious day kept deliberately for small uses, hoarding one's edge and gathering momentum: such is the almanac's fine sense of measure.

Metaphysics Insight

Siming holds the Yellow Path today — the bearer of the oldest name among the twelve day-spirits. "Siming" (Director of Destinies) was not originally a term of date-selection at all, but the revered deity of life and death in ancient Chu: the Nine Songs of the Chu Ci devote two separate hymns to him, the Grand Siming and the Younger Siming. The Grand Siming "rides the pure qi and commands yin and yang," holding in hand the allotted span of every human life; in the Younger Siming's hymn ring the lines "No sorrow deeper than parting in life, no joy sweeter than a new friend" — sung for over two thousand years, among the tenderest verses Qu Yuan ever wrote. In Han times, court and household alike offered sacrifice to Siming, and folk tradition made the Kitchen God an officer of his registry — the "Siming of the Eastern Hearth," who recorded every good and ill deed of the household and reported it above. The later almanac-makers invited this keeper of registers into the twelve rotating day-spirits, ranking him beside the Azure Dragon, the Bright Hall, the Golden Cabinet, the Heavenly Virtue and the Jade Hall among the six Yellow-Path auspices. A spirit so clear-sighted and so precise with the brush grants a quiet, discriminating luck rather than a sweeping one: worship to express reverence, bathing to express purity, wall repairs and road leveling to express order — while clamorous rites, wedding feasts and distant travels are none of a registrar's liking, and all stand avoided. To understand Siming is to understand why today's almanac is "auspicious yet sparse in affairs" — auspicious in laying foundations in order, sparse out of measured reverence.
