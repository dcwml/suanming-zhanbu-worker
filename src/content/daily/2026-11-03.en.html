Xīn Sì follows Gēng Chén: yesterday the yang stem of metal opened the new, today the yin stem of metal carries it on — Gēng Chén and Xīn Sì share the White Wax Metal Na Yin, the freshly smelted metal made into a pair; Xīn is the suppleness of yin metal, a metal best worked and polished into form, seated upon the Bǐng fire hidden in Sì that tempers it — the very image of raw ore refined into vessel. The wonder of the day lies in union: in the Xū month the Heavenly Virtue stands at Bǐng, and for the months of Yín, Wǔ and Xū the Lunar Virtue stands at Bǐng as well — and Bǐng and Xīn are one of the five unions of stems. Hence the Combined Heavenly Virtue and Combined Lunar Virtue descend together, a pairing the month allows but once or twice; virtue governs blessings and union governs covenants, and betrothal pledges, presenting gifts, drawing up contracts and transactions are all matters sealed by a single written instrument — the favored list answers itself. Yesterday the Month Breaker tore down the old, today the Hall of Light raises the new — the second spirit of the Yellow Path takes over from the Azure Dragon, two Yellow Path days in a row: the ground cleared yesterday by demolition and wall-razing is exactly the ground on which today a contract is signed and a new foundation laid. Each of the five avoided matters has its spirit behind it: the Wandering Calamity scatters the wandering spirit — prayers and blessings are unwise; the Blood Taboo guards against the shedding of blood — the grand rite of marriage is set aside, betrothal first and the wedding on a luckier day, for the old rites were always taken in several steps; the Repeat Day doubles what is done, and both Sì and Hài count among the repeat days — the clashing Pig offends against it too — so burial is avoided; the Heavenly Thief keeps the watch — though collecting wealth is favored, still guard the granary against theft once it is stored. Four days remain until Start of Winter (Nov 7): autumn metal about to fade — hold fast to the light of this double virtue while it lasts. Clash the Pig, the Sha lies East: Sì and Hài stand opposed — keep clear of contention with Pig people, and let eastern journeys take a slower step.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the breaking day closed its work; today the Hall of Light sets its seal — and the Hall is not the whole of it: heading the auspicious column stand the Combined Heavenly Virtue and Combined Lunar Virtue, the almanac's largest gesture toward the day of Xīn Sì. To speak of the combinations, first speak of virtue: the Heavenly Virtue is the chief of a month's blessed breaths, and it walks with the month's command — Dīng in the first month, Shēn in the second, Rén in the third, Xīn in the fourth... and in the ninth, the month of Xū, it stands at Bǐng. The Lunar Virtue is taken from the month branch's triple harmony — Yín, Wǔ and Xū form the fire trio, and fire's virtue is Bǐng — so the Xū month's Lunar Virtue also stands at Bǐng. Virtue is a breath of blessing and good, formless in itself, and it must be united into a day to work: Bǐng and Xīn are one of the five unions of the stems — on any day whose stem is Xīn, the Heavenly and Lunar Virtues both arrive in union, a double combination a month allows but once or twice. The Compendium of Calendars and Records reads the days of the Heavenly and Lunar Virtues as fit for raising palaces, opening ground and building homes, taking office and setting out — a hundred undertakings may be worked. And the day of combined virtue carries a further saying: "the hidden virtue enters the life" — the bright virtue joined to the day stem is as if a patron stood behind one's affairs unseen: undertakings that meet it succeed, afflictions that meet it scatter. The subtlety runs deeper still, into the pillars themselves: Xīn metal sits upon Sì, and Sì conceals Bǐng — Bǐng being the Officer Star of Xīn metal, the Officer joined to the stem is the joy of documents and covenants. The Heavenly Virtue's Bǐng, the Lunar Virtue's Bǐng, and the Bǐng hidden in Sì — three Bǐng answering one another around the same Bǐng–Xīn union — and so the whole favored list is a list of unions: betrothal pledges and presenting gifts are two families joined, contracts and transactions are two houses bound, gathering with kin and friends is affection united. Yet union stops short of wedding: marriages stand avoided — the Blood Taboo keeps the watch, and the grand rite is set aside; the betrothal pledges go first and the wedding follows on a luckier day, for the old Three Letters and Six Rites were always taken step by step, and the pledge is one of those steps. Yesterday broke the old, today establishes the new — the almanac's two-day serial, playing out the four characters: first break, then build.
