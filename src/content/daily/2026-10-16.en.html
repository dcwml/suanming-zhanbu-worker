On a Guǐ Hài day, Guǐ water reaches its Imperial Prosperity at Hài — the yin stems grow in reverse, and Guǐ, born at Mǎo, passes bathing, cap-and-sash and office to arrive at Hài, the seat of its utmost vigor; and with the two days Rén Xū and Guǐ Hài both carrying the Na Yin of Vast Sea Water, yesterday's swell continues in today's wave — autumn waters stretching sea to sky. The Four strengths and the Minister Day both attest to it: autumn metal holds the season, metal breeds water, and water enjoys the ninth month's thriving air — a day whose stem and branch are both water earns the title outright. Today the heavenly spirit is the Jade Hall on the Yellow Path — the pure, noble spirit of the jade palace, presiding over letters, patrons and passage; hence all twelve items in the favorable column are acts of smooth going: travel stands in the list, for the Yellow Path favors the crossing; haircuts and bathing for a fresh start; felling trees, demolition, raising the ridgepole and completing the roof ridge — the whole work of building proceeds together; setting the bed, kindling the stove and building livestock pens settle home and herd alike. Auspicious as the Jade Hall is, the eight avoids have reasons of their own: with the Earth Talisman on duty, breaking earth, moving earth and making stoves are all set aside; with the Double Day among the spirits, marriages, burial and funeral processions are not held, lest a grave matter be doubled; bridge-building is avoided, for on a day of surging water timber and earth do not contend with the current; lawsuits too are shunned — the Reverent Peace prizes harmony, and quarreling in court yields to making peace. And there is a fine pair to read across the two columns: the Five Wealth presides over plenty while the Five Voids preside over depletion, and the two share this one day — taking wealth in is well, but sending it out wants care; opening the granary to lend, or any large outlay, is poorly timed today.

The Five Wealth presides over plenty: tallying accounts, planning the livelihood and managing inflows all run smoothly; only the Five Voids appear with it, and wealth that comes may yet leak — today favors taking in over sending out; lending, guarantees and large outlays can wait a step. Haircuts and setting the bed stand in the favorable column: tidying one's appearance and putting one's rest in order suit the storing-away way of late autumn. The Jade Hall favors movement and travel stands in the list — short journeys, errands and visits all run smooth, only mind the direction: the day clashes with the Snake and the Sha lies West. Those born in the Year of the Snake are clashed today: avoid long journeys west and refrain from contending with Pigs. Eight days past Cold Dew, the water air deepens daily and Hài water stands at full tide — keep the back and legs warm, sleep early, and let no late-autumn chill trouble the water of your natal branch.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday's closing lines said it: after the White Tiger of the fifteenth, the sixteenth turns back to the Jade Hall — another Yellow Path day. Today the words hold true. In the rota of the twelve spirits, no sooner has the White Tiger gone by than the Jade Hall follows it: where the Black Path reaches its end, the Yellow Path ascends the hall — the ancients wrote the very words "fortune turning at the heart of calamity" straight into the order of the daily rotation. The Jade Hall was first the name of a Han palace: in Emperor Wu's Jianzhang Palace stood the Jade Hall, its steps and stairways made of jade, tradition says, and upon its roof a bronze phoenix sheathed in gold, hung on pivots so it turned with the wind like a bird in flight; later generations called the Hanlin Academy "the Jade Hall and the Golden Horse," for a scholar ascending the Jade Hall ascended the courts of the immortals. The almanac borrowed the palace's name for a spirit: on a Jade Hall day, patrons extend a hand and documents find their strength — hence the old saying that the Jade Hall's Yellow Path "favors meeting the great person." The twelve spirits march in two companies, bright and dark: the six of the Yellow Path — Azure Dragon, Hall of Light, Golden Locket, Treasured Light (the Heavenly Virtue), Jade Hall, Commander of Life; the six of the Black Path — Heavenly Punishment, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Heavenly Prison, Dark Warrior, Usher of Discord; six yellow, six black, turning day by day, a full circuit in twelve, round and round again. What is worth savoring is the palace name itself: the Hall of Light was where the Son of Heaven gave out governance — its weight lay in affairs of state; the Jade Hall was where the literati waited to be summoned — its weight lay in letters. So the Jade Hall day especially favors the work of pen and paper: submitting writings, sitting examinations, interviews, signing, calling on the mighty — all borrow its pure air. Set today's lists beside this and they answer one another: travel, haircuts and setting the bed in the favorable column answer the Jade Hall's passage, tidiness and settling; lawsuits in the avoid column suit exactly the Jade Hall's temper — meet friends with letters, not officials with disputes. Yesterday one dodged the fierce; today one seeks the auspicious. Between advancing and retiring, the almanac arranges every day into a living game of go — a move here, a retreat there, each in its season.
