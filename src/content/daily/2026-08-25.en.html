Today is Xīn Wèi 辛未 — the stem Xīn is yin metal, the metal of pearls, jade and hairpin ornaments, supple yet finely firm; the branch Wèi is yin earth, the soil of a late-summer garden, gentle and nourishing. Earth produces metal, branch supporting stem, so Xīn metal rests upon its own resource, roots firm and qi clear; and its Nayin is Roadside Earth, plain and unadorned, quietly bearing all things like the earth along the road. The presiding deity is Yùtáng 玉堂, the Jade Hall, one of the six auspicious spirits of the Yellow Path, governing brightness, nobility and good fortune; and the auspicious spirits Mǔcāng 母仓, Jīntáng 金堂 and Yùtáng 玉堂 attend together — Mother Granary presiding over storage and nurture, Golden Hall and Jade Hall over dignity and clarity, so the day's broad pattern is auspicious. Sixteen matters are therefore favored, most of them works of building and repair: installing ridge beams, removing old hearths, dismantling, construction, breaking ground, laying foundations, mending walls and sealing holes, together with sacrifice, travel, setting the bed, encoffining, breaking earth for graves, burial, moving coffins and increasing livestock — all may proceed. Six matters are banned: marriage heads the list, with moving into a house, ritual offerings and consecration likewise forbidden, while acupuncture touches bloodshed and digging wells cuts the earth's veins, both to be avoided. Among the inauspicious spirits, Tiānzéi 天贼, the Heavenly Thief, governs theft and loss, so guard your belongings when out; Xuèzhī 血支, the Blood Branch, governs bloodshed, which is why acupuncture stands among the bans; and Yuèxū 月虚 and Wǔxū 五虚 govern depletion and loss, so large movements of money deserve extra caution. Two days have passed since End of Heat — the autumn qi is rising and the metal qi gathers in; act today with steadiness and solidity: press forward with building work, hold back from celebration.

Zodiac Fortune · Sheep

Today's branch is Wèi (未), which corresponds to the Sheep — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Sheep year sit squarely on the day's own branch, and moreover enjoy a Jade Hall Yellow Path day — Sheep meeting a Wèi day is like coming home, movement and stillness alike at ease, a day good for taking charge of one's own affairs and for working with one's own hands. At work, the Jade Hall is bright and noble, governing documents and the written word — Sheep with papers, contracts, plans or creative writing on hand will find their thoughts clear and their pen sure today; with installing ridge beams, construction, breaking ground and laying foundations all on the favorable list, those in building, renovation or engineering may give full play to their craft; travel too is favored, so business trips and visits to clients go well. In wealth, the Wealth God stands in the due East where the sun rises — Sheep seeking money or doing business may favor the east; only since Tiānzéi 天贼, the Heavenly Thief, stands among the inauspicious spirits governing theft and loss, keep close watch on purse and phone when out; Yuèxū and Wǔxū govern depletion, so speculation, lending and large investments are all unfit for today. In love, marriage heads the list of bans and moving into a house is likewise forbidden — Sheep with marriage in mind should not fix the date or hold the ceremony today; preliminary discussions may proceed unhurried, with another auspicious day chosen for the rite itself. Mind, though, that today clashes with the Ox and the Sha lies West: yield a step to those born in an Ox year and keep important matters away from the due west; Xuèzhī governs bloodshed, and since acupuncture stands among the bans, any medical procedure involving blood should also wait. In health, End of Heat has passed and autumn dryness is rising; Wèi belongs to earth and governs spleen and stomach, so Sheep should eat plainly and regularly these days, avoiding cold, raw and greasy food — a settled stomach is what lets one welcome the gathering-in of autumn metal.

Folklore

The presiding deity today is Yùtáng 玉堂, the Jade Hall, one of the six auspicious spirits of the Yellow Path — so today's reading is about this name itself. The twelve presiding deities — Qīnglóng 青龙, Míngtáng 明堂, Tiānxíng 天刑, Zhūquè 朱雀, Jīnkuì 金匮, Tiāndé 天德, Báihǔ 白虎, Yùtáng 玉堂, Tiānláo 天牢, Xuánwǔ 玄武, Sīmìng 司命 and Gōuchén 勾陈 — take duty in fixed rotation day by day, and six of them — Qīnglóng, Míngtáng, Jīnkuì, Tiāndé, Yùtáng and Sīmìng — are auspicious, together called the Six Auspicious Spirits of the Yellow Path, and the days on which they preside are the Yellow Path auspicious days. Of these six, the Jade Hall carries the stateliest imagery: Yùtáng was the name of a great hall in the Han imperial palace, where the emperor feasted his ministers and received men of worth; under Han custom, men of letters awaiting appointment waited at the Gate of the Golden Horse — Dōngfāng Shuò 东方朔 waited there for years before his talents were at last employed — and so "Golden Horse and Jade Hall" became a byword for courtly eminence and the assembly of literary talent. Brought down into the shen-sha system, a Jade Hall day governs brightness, dignity and clear good fortune, favoring above all matters of documents, writing and ceremony; and today's favorable list happens to include installing ridge beams, construction, breaking ground and laying foundations — a hall is above all a work of building, so construction on a Jade Hall day gains a special resonance. The ancients chose auspicious days by drawing imagery from what lies near: seeing Qīnglóng they took the image of heavenly nobility, seeing Míngtáng the image of bright and orderly rule, seeing Yùtáng the image of the dignity of the palace. Heaven and earth do not speak; the sages drew images to make their teaching plain — and an old almanac is, in great part, learning of precisely this kind.
