On a Wù Shēn day, the Wù earth sits upon Shēn metal — earth begets metal and metal reigns in autumn, a smooth and graceful circulation; the Na Yin is Earth of the Great Post Road, and the post road runs everywhere, echoing a day given wholly to movement. The White Tiger of the Black Path would make an ominous seat, yet an autumn Wù Shēn day is precisely the seasonal Day of Heavenly Pardon — the pardon all malevolent spirits dread — with the King's Day and the Sagely Heart lending dignity and forbearance, so the menace dissolves from within. With the Heavenly Horse galloping upon the roads, travel, relocating, moving into a house, construction and groundbreaking all stand favored, and breaking ground and burial may proceed with composure. Yet the Wandering Misfortune and the Blood Branch stand in the column: prayers of sacrifice may wait a beat, and any matter that draws blood is best set aside; the Five Separations forbid union rather than parting, so marriage, betrothal and business openings should wait for another day, and grave repair avoids the White Tiger's awe.

The Five Wealth arrives alongside: means run full, and taking stock of savings and tidying accounts befits the day, with steady income flowing in; only with the Five Separations at hand and the White Tiger presiding, business openings, signings and large investments should wait — guard wealth rather than chase it. The Blood Branch warns of minor injury: take extra care at the wheel, keep blades and sharp edges at a distance, and tend small cuts promptly. The day clashes with the Tiger and the Sha lies South: those born in the Year of the Tiger should keep a low profile in all matters, avoid journeys toward the South, and refrain from quarrels with Monkeys.

Metaphysics Insight

Leading today's auspicious column is Tiān Shè, Heavenly Pardon — one of the most celebrated seats in the art of date selection. Each season holds exactly one Day of Heavenly Pardon: Wù Yín in spring, Jiǎ Wǔ in summer, Wù Shēn in autumn and Jiǎ Zǐ in winter. Today, a Wù Shēn day, is the autumn pardon that comes but once a season. As the old Almanac of the Heavenly Treasury explains, "Heaven begets life through Jiǎ and Wù; earth establishes it through Zǐ, Wǔ, Yín and Shēn" — spring's sprouting wood takes Wù Yín, summer's blazing fire takes Jiǎ Wǔ, autumn's ripening metal takes Wù Shēn and winter's returning waters take Jiǎ Zǐ, each an image of heaven and earth paired in ceaseless generation; hence on this day heaven shows mercy and the hundred spirits lie hidden. Folk custom takes the pardon day as a time of repentance and release: offering incense and prayers, freeing captive creatures, dissolving old grudges — a kindness done or a fault confessed on this day is reckoned to count manifold, and even small transgressions meet with leniency. Yet the saying "on the pardon day no taboo applies" is a distortion: today the White Tiger holds the Black Path and the Five Separations stand by, so marriage, business openings and sacrifices remain among the taboos. What the Pardon dissolves is calamity, not every rule of choosing a day — the majesty of the Black Path and the mercy of the auspicious spirits sharing one sky is precisely the almanac's character: fortune and misfortune lean upon each other, and the wise steer between them.
