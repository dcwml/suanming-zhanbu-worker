Inauspicious Spirits

Sǐqì 死气 · Yuánwǔ 元武

Planting 栽种 · Digging wells 掘井 · Acquiring property 置产

On a Xīn Mǎo day, Xīn is yin metal — the metal of jewels, hairpins and ornaments; Mǎo is the mid-season wood, the wood of flowers and leaves — within Mǎo hides yǐ, and yǐ wood is precisely Xīn metal's indirect wealth: a day of sitting in the land of unexpected riches. The day god arrives at Xuánwǔ 玄武, the Black Tortoise — a Black Path inauspicious day, governing thieves, dim-lit dealings and losses. (The list writes it 元武, Yuánwǔ: the Qing editors of the almanac shunned the taboo character of the Kangxi Emperor's name and turned 玄 into 元 — an old scruple the calendar still keeps.) Yet today the two lists stand more unevenly than they have in days: seven auspicious spirits face two inauspicious, and the Black Path's malice is pressed too low to lift its head. Sānhé 三合 heads the list — the day branch Mǎo half-joins the month branch Hài in the Wood Bureau of Hài–Mǎo–Wèi, month and day in accord, harmony filling the gate; Wǔhé 五合 follows, fit for both joining families and signing contracts; Bùjiàng 不将 holds its post a second day, and marriages stand firm at the head of the favorable list. Yīndé 阴德 guards hidden merit and Mírì 民日 is the day of the people's ease — sacrifice, prayer and praying for heirs all turn back into favor: yesterday the wandering calamity stood in the way and temple visits could wait; today it has withdrawn and the People's Day attends, the incense relights — yesterday's riddle finds its sequel today. Míngwùduì 鸣吠对 is the especial spirit of burial, whence the funeral at the list's end. Seventeen matters stand favorable — nearly everything may be done: opening a market, trading, signing contracts and hanging plaques all stand in the list, Wǔhé and the indirect wealth answering one another, and bargaining and signing are in their hour; consecration, kindling the hearth, moving in, relocating and setting the bed all stand wide open as before; felling timber too — yesterday Yín wood, fed by Hài water, was at the height of its vigor and timber-felling was avoided; today Xīn metal trims Mǎo wood, metal pruning wood, felling as harvest — a neat counterpart. The inauspicious pair number but two, each with a clear message: Sǐqì 死气 is the breath of death, the breath without life — planting seeks life and well-digging pierces the earth for its springs, both at odds with it, so planting and digging wells are avoided; Xuánwǔ governs thieving, and large purchases of property risk dim losses, so acquiring property is avoided — let big acquisitions take a slower step today, and mind the door and keep the papers, for the Black Tortoise's lesson lies in the single word "guard." The Na Yin Pine and Cypress Wood holds its post a second day: yesterday it planted roots on the cliff and kept green through winter, today its fiber does not change — seven auspicious spirits in full leaf like the pine's flourishing, the Black Tortoise a winter wind that cannot snap evergreen wood. Clash the Rooster, the Sha lies West: Mǎo and Yǒu stand opposed — keep clear of contention and partnership with Rooster people, and let western journeys take a slower step.

Zodiac Fortune · Rabbit 兔

Today's earthly branch is Mǎo, which corresponds to the Rabbit — making the Rabbit the protagonist of the day: the Rabbit of Mǎo keeps its watch, and in the Mǎo hours, five to seven in the morning, the gate of day opens of itself as the sun takes the shift. The day branch half-joins the month branch in the Wood Bureau, and the Rabbit stands in accord with the month: rapport and partnership are both at their best — negotiating ventures, joining hands in enterprise, finding helpers; when the harmony of the Three arrives, half the effort buys the whole result. Marriages stand most favorable: Bùjiàng, Wǔhé and Yīndé attend threefold — for the unwed Rabbit, confessions and betrothals are in their hour today; for the wed, sign the household's new covenants together and plan its affairs as one. Wealth is today's spectacle: yǐ hidden in Mǎo is Xīn metal's indirect wealth, and unexpected riches stand at the door — opening a market, trading, signing contracts and hanging plaques all stand favorable; grand openings, bargains struck, payments coming in, all in season. But Xuánwǔ governs thieving, and wealth that comes must be kept: count the day's takings before dark, watch the valuables and the account papers, and let large acquisitions wait — this is the Black Tortoise's homework for the Rabbit, spelled in the word "guard." Sacrifice, prayer and praying for heirs all stand favorable with the People's Day on duty: Rabbit folk who would fulfill a vow, light incense, or pray for a child to join the household may go today. Moving in, relocating and setting the bed stand wide open — moving house and arranging beds are both in season. Travel stands favorable, though the Sha lies West — give western routes a slower step. Clash the Rooster, Sha West: with Rooster people today, keep clear of contention and go slow on partnership. In the body, Mǎo answers the liver's wood, which delights in free flow and chafes at gloom — morning stretches, less anger, less brooding, that is the way to nourish it; the Mǎo hours are exactly when the Large Intestine meridian keeps watch — a cup of warm water at rising and keep the morning's regular hour; Xīn metal answers the lungs, and after the Start of Winter cold dryness deepens — moistening the lungs and generating fluids remains the work at hand. The Jade Rabbit pounds the elixir of long life; the Rabbit folk at morning tend the breath of life.

Metaphysics Insight

Yesterday the tiger kept the door; today the Rabbit of Mǎo takes its watch — so let us tell of the Jade Rabbit in the moon. First look at the character 卯 itself: the Shuowen Jiezi 说文解字, the Explanations of Graphs, says: "Mǎo means to break forth. In the second month the ten thousand things break through the earth and come out; its form is that of an open door." The graph 卯 is drawn as two door panels — yesterday we painted the tiger upon the door; today at the Mǎo hours the sun rises and the gate of day opens of itself, the door-panel image carrying on in one unbroken line. And how did that rabbit come to dwell in the moon? The Chuci Tianwen 天问, the Heavenly Questions of the Songs of Chu, put the asking more than two thousand years ago: "What virtue has the night-light, that dying it is born again? What profit lies in its belly, that the rabbit rests within?" The Han ballad Dongtao Xing 董逃行 gives the picture: "The Jade Rabbit, long kneeling, pounds the toad-pill" — kneeling in the lunar palace with pestle in hand, pounding the pill that is the elixir of deathlessness; and from there, through Chang'e's flight to the moon and the Mid-Autumn worship of the moon, the rabbit pounding its elixir became the emblem of the lunar court. Why should a rabbit be raised to the moon? The Great Tang Records on the Western Region keeps the story: Śakra, lord of heaven, made himself a hungry and worn old traveler to try the hearts of three beasts — fox, ape and rabbit. The ape gathered fruit and offered it; the fox found food as well; only the rabbit came back empty-handed, and said: "Kindle a fire." With that it flung itself into the flames, offering its own flesh for the meal. Śakra, moved by its sincerity, raised the rabbit up whole and lodged it in the disc of the moon — "that its fragrance might pass to later ages, and so the moon keeps a beast." And today's almanac echoes the tale at every turn: Xuánwǔ keeps the watch, master of night and of things hidden, and the Jade Rabbit is the essence of the moon's dark side — however dark the night, where there is moonlight thieves lie quiet, just as seven auspicious spirits press down two inauspicious and the Black Path cannot put out the light. The breath of death stands in the list and planting is avoided, while the Jade Rabbit pounds precisely the medicine of not-dying — what is avoided is sowing the breath of death, what is sought is praying for long life; between one "avoid" and one "favorable" hides the ancients' plainest view of life and death. Acquiring property is avoided today for fear of loss, and the crafty rabbit keeps three burrows of its own — look once more at large sums, keep one paper more, and that is the rabbit's wisdom. At the Mǎo hours the sun rises; tonight the moon climbs — by day the rabbit of Mǎo opens the door, by night the Jade Rabbit pounds the elixir, each at its post; heaven and earth keep their own good order.
