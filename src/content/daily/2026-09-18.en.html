On Yǐ Wèi day, the day stem Yǐ is the vine and flowering herb of plants, seated upon Wèi Earth — the storehouse of wood, where wood returns to rest. Tiāndé, the Heavenly Virtue, presides on the Yellow Path: merciful and life-nurturing, it turns ill fortune to good, and with Yuèdéhé (the Lunar Virtue in union) and Yángdé standing beside it, the virtue-spirits gather on every side — hence though funeral and ancestral rites fill today's favors, the day remains settled and safe. Mǔcāng shepherds nurture and compassion, Shēngqì keeps the current of life flowing, and Tiāncāng guards the storehouse: sacrificing in remembrance of ancestors, repairing graves, removing or donning mourning garments, reopening graves and moving coffins are all timely — and note the subtlety, that reopening graves and moving coffins stand in the favors while breaking ground for burial and burial itself are taboo: a day of "may open, may move, but not lay to rest," shaped for reburial. Yet Wǔxū (Five Emptinesses) and Jiǔkōng (Nine Hollows) render wealth airy and unsubstantial, so opening market, moving into a new home and wedding festivities are all barred; Tǔfú, the Earth Talisman, keeps the earth spirits on duty, so groundbreaking is also avoided; "no other undertakings," says the almanac — today favors stillness over motion, gathering over spending: beyond the rites of remembrance, let all else wait.

Zodiac Fortune · Goat 羊

Today's earthly branch is Wèi, which corresponds to the Goat — making the Goat the zodiac protagonist of the day. Those born in the Year of the Goat preside over the day under the light of Tiāndé on the Yellow Path, with Yuèdéhé lending support: hidden helpers stand near, and troubles turn to good. Should there be mourning garments in the family, donning or removing them today accords with rite — one whose term of mourning is fulfilled may remove the garments and close the rite, and funeral rites themselves may proceed; with Shēngqì and Tiāncāng beside, prayers and sacrificial rites find ready response, and money is best stored away — gathered, not scattered. Yet Wǔxū and Jiǔkōng make wealth airy: exposing money through opening market is taboo — investments, contracts and shop openings should wait, and large expenditures demand extra care; Tǔfú holds the earth, so renovations, groundbreaking and moving into a new home are all out of season; weddings too stand in the taboos — let joys await another day. The day clashes with the Ox and the Sha lies West: those born in the Year of the Ox should keep a low profile on all matters, avoid long journeys west, and refrain from quarrels with Goats; Goats themselves, though the virtue-spirits guard the day, should keep to stillness and rite — beyond honoring the departed, seek little and hold fast, for quietness itself is the opening of luck.

Metaphysics Insight

Among today's favors stand Chúfú and Chéngfú side by side — removing and donning the mourning garments — so let us speak of the Five Degrees of Mourning and the term of sorrow. The five degrees are Zhǎncuī, Qícuī, Dàgōng, Xiǎogōng and Sīmá, graded by the coarseness of the hemp cloth and the length of the term, each after the closeness of kin: zhǎncuī, of the coarsest raw hemp with its frayed edge left unhemmed, is worn by a son for his father through three years, the heaviest of all; qícuī is hemmed, its term running from three years down to three months; dàgōng lasts nine months, xiǎogōng five months, and sīmá three months — the more distant the kin, the lighter the garment. Chéngfú is the rite on the day of first lament, when each according to his station dons the garment proper to his grief, and the term of mourning begins to count; through the term there is no feasting, no marrying, no sitting for the examinations — this is called "dwelling in mourning." When the term is fulfilled, the rite of chúfú is performed: the mourning garment is put off and garments of good omen put on, and only then may celebrations come to the door again. The ancients honored this rite so highly that the almanac keeps a column of its own for each: choosing a day for donning, that sorrow may keep its measure, and for removing, that the mourning may turn to joy. Today is Yǐ Wèi: Wèi is the storehouse of wood, graced by Yuèdéhé; for one whose mourning term is full, removing the garments today completes the rite and gathers the virtue — "sorrow has its season, and the living their order," as the ancients held.
