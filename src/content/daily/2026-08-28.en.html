Today is Jiǎ Xū 甲戌 — the stem Jiǎ is yang wood, the wood of towering trees, upright and aspiring; the branch Xū is yang earth, the earth of hills and highlands, solid and weighty. Wood overcomes earth, stem restraining branch, so Jiǎ wood standing upon Xū earth is like a great tree rooted on a mountain summit — it may survey all directions, yet it needs deep soil and strong roots. Its Nayin is Mountain-Top Fire, fire upon the mountain, its light shining far and near, carrying the imagery of civilization. The presiding deity is Sīmìng 司命, one of the six auspicious spirits of the Yellow Path, governing human longevity and destiny; when Sīmìng presides, the day naturally favors health, longevity and peace. Eight auspicious spirits attend together — Mǔcāng governs abundant harvest, Yángdé hidden virtue, Shǒurì careful conservation, Tiānwū shamanic prayer, Fúdé lasting blessings, Liùyī ritual propriety, Jìng'ān reverent peace, and Sīmìng itself governs a full lifespan — eight spirits at once, auspicious qi overflowing. Yet four inauspicious spirits are also present: Yānduì governs clash and enmity, Zhāoyáo false alarm, Tiāngǒu devouring disturbance, and Jiǔkōng emptiness and void. Eleven matters are favored: bathing, haircutting, meeting friends and kin, sculpting and painting, consecration, planting, herding, marriage, channel therapy, repairing walls and filling holes — mostly matters of inward ordering, mending gaps and friendly gathering, fitting the conserving virtue of Sīmìng. Ten matters are banned: opening markets, moving into a house, breaking ground, breaking earth for graves, burial, installing a stove, raising beams, setting the bed, opening granaries and praying for blessings — for Jiǔkōng governs emptiness, so opening markets and granaries is unfit; Tiāngǒu governs disturbance, so breaking ground is unfit; Yānduì governs clash, so moving in and setting the bed are unfit. Five days have passed since End of Heat and White Dew is not yet come, autumn metal rising while wood qi begins to gather in — act today by borrowing the auspiciousness of the eight spirits to soften the four inauspicious ones: press forward with inward ordering, hold back from breaking ground and launching ventures.

Zodiac Fortune · Dog

Today's branch is Xū (戌), which corresponds to the Dog — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Dog year sit squarely on the day's own branch — Dog meeting a Xū day is like returning to its den; the presiding deity Sīmìng is auspicious and eight spirits attend together, so auspicious qi is abundant. At work, Sīmìng governs longevity and destiny, while Fúdé and Yángdé both attend, governing the hidden protection of benefactors and lasting blessings — Dogs should conserve rather than venture today, and may advance with matters such as meeting friends, sculpting and consecration, while personal relations go smoothly; yet opening markets and moving into a house stand among the bans, so launching a business or relocating is unfit today. In weddings, marriage stands among the favorable matters — Dogs with marriage in mind may hold the betrothal or fix the date today, and since Sīmìng governs longevity and blessings, marriage on this day favors a couple growing old together; bathing and haircutting are also favored, so grooming and refreshing oneself fits the clean auspiciousness of Sīmìng. Mind, though, that today clashes with the Dragon and the Sha lies North: yield a step to those born in a Dragon year; since both the Wealth God and Joy God stand in the Northeast, those seeking money or doing business may favor the northeast, yet the Sha lies in the North, so going north should be cautious and brief; breaking ground, breaking earth for graves and burial are all among the bans, so any matter of piercing the soil is unfit today; and setting the bed and opening granaries are likewise forbidden, so installing a new bed or opening storage should wait for another auspicious day. In health, autumn dryness rises after End of Heat, and on a Jiǎ-Xū day wood overcomes earth — Dogs should guard against spleen-stomach disharmony, favoring warm and soft food over cold and raw; Xū earth governs the stomach and Jiǎ wood restrains it, so sweet flavors nourish earth and warm food protects the center.

Folklore

The presiding deity today is Sīmìng 司命, one of the six auspicious spirits of the Yellow Path — so today's reading is about this deity. The twelve presiding deities take duty in fixed rotation day by day, and among them Sīmìng, Qīnglóng, Míngtáng, Jīnkuì, Tiāndé and Yùtáng are together called the Six Auspicious Spirits of the Yellow Path, and the days on which they preside are Yellow Path auspicious days. The name Sīmìng is ancient — the "Nine Songs" in the Songs of Chǔ contains two pieces, "Great Sīmìng" and "Lesser Sīmìng": the Great Sīmìng governs human lifespan and death, the Lesser Sīmìng governs offspring and posterity; Qū Yuán honored them with magnificent verse, showing their exalted station. Later almanacs incorporated Sīmìng into the twelve presiding deities, and whenever Sīmìng presides, the day favors health, longevity, sacrifice, prayer, marriage and praying for offspring. Among today's auspicious spirits, Mǔcāng, Yángdé, Shǒurì, Tiānwū, Fúdé, Liùyī, Jìng'ān and Sīmìng itself attend together — eight spirits gathered at once. Mǔcāng governs the harvest of the five grains, Yángdé hidden virtue, Shǒurì careful conservation, Tiānwū shamanic prayer, Fúdé lasting blessings, Liùyī ritual propriety, Jìng'ān reverent peace, and Sīmìng itself governs a full lifespan. Each spirit has its own charge, and taken together the image of today is one of "conserving and accumulating virtue, inwardly ordering and repairing": bathing and haircutting to cleanse the body, meeting friends to harmonize relations, marriage to fix an alliance, repairing walls and filling holes to mend the dwelling — all are matters of inward conservation, not outward expansion. Among the bans, opening markets, moving in, breaking ground and breaking earth for graves are matters of launching and piercing the soil, which run counter to the virtues of Shǒurì and Jìng'ān. The ancients said, "When Sīmìng is in the hall, all affairs prosper" — yet this prosperity prospers inwardly, not outwardly; it prospers in conservation, not in attack. To read an old almanac well, one must know the nature of the auspicious spirits, and only then may one act according to the time.
