Today is Wù Wǔ 戊午 — the stem Wù is yang earth, thick and weighty as city walls and high mounds, able to carry all things; the branch Wǔ is the fire of high noon, fire at its zenith. Wù earth rests upon Wǔ fire, fire generating earth, stem and branch producing one another — a firm and solid foundation. The Nayin is Heavenly Fire, the very sun hanging in the sky, its light falling upon all things. Tiāndé Hé 天德合 holds the day, dissolving misfortune and summoning noble helpers; Tiānmǎ 天马 governs swift movement, favoring journeys and errands; Shíyáng 时阳 and Shēngqì 生气 govern vigor and growth; Bùjiāng 不将 favors weddings above all, and Yùyǔ 玉宇 governs noble clarity — so marriage, sacrifice, praying for blessings, praying for offspring, traveling and setting beds are all on the favorable list. Yet the day god is Báihǔ 白虎, governing injury and mourning, and Sìhào 四耗 governs dissipation and loss — so encoffining, burial, moving into a house, moving house, purchasing property and acquiring livestock are all best postponed. Today's momentum is vigorous and substantial: favorable for pressing forward with weddings, travel and prayers for blessing, not for schemes of property and relocation.

Zodiac Fortune · Horse

Today's branch is Wǔ (午), which corresponds to the Horse — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Horse year sit squarely on the day's branch and hold the duty, with Tiānmǎ 天马 in attendance — a post horse given an extra whip: spirits high, movement forceful, a fine day for being on the go. In career and company, today favors meeting relatives and friends, traveling and removal — Horses negotiating, networking or running errands will find things flowing, and matters long stalled may be pushed forward today. In love and marriage, Bùjiāng 不将 presides over weddings and Tiāndé Hé 天德合 over harmony — Horses weighing a wedding or engagement could hardly pick a better day. For fortune, the Wealth God dwells due North, the Joy God Southeast and the Fortune God Northeast — seek wealth to the north. But take note: the Sha also lies North today and the clash falls on the Rat, so Horses planning a northbound journey should choose another direction or another day, and avoid direct confrontation with those born in a Rat year — Zǐ and Wǔ clash, and yielding a step resolves it. Mind three avoidances as well: moving into a house and moving house are unfavorable, so postpone any relocation; purchasing property and acquiring livestock are unfavorable too — large purchases of land or livestock tend to drain away, so delay them; and encoffining and burial are best avoided entirely, for Báihǔ 白虎 presides over mourning today. In health, with fire and earth both at full strength and dryness ruling, Horses should drink well, keep out of the blazing sun, and guard against a restless temper and mouth sores.

Folklore

Today's Nayin is Heavenly Fire — a good day to tell where this Nayin comes from. Among the sixty Nayin pairs of the sexagenary cycle, Wù Wǔ 戊午 and Jǐ Wèi 己未 together carry the Heavenly Fire — the fire of the sun, hung in the highest heavens, shining down upon all things, the most noble and luminous grade of all fires. Wǔ sits due south, the throne of fire at its zenith, and the Wù Wǔ pillar stands right at its height — the ancients likened it to the sun at high noon: light and heat at their utmost, shining upon nothing privately. For this reason, those born on a Wù Wǔ day are often cast as open-hearted and radiant, generous in giving light, for the virtue of fire lies in brightness, not in concealment. Back to today: the day stem Wù earth sits upon Wǔ fire, fire generating earth — a configuration called being generated from below. Heavenly Fire begets the earth above the ground, just as sunlight generously carries the great land. The old almanacs never judge a day by a single spirit, but by the whole weather woven from stems, branches, Nayin and spirits — today fire and earth generate one another, Tiānmǎ 天马 gallops, Tiāndé Hé 天德合 dissolves the fierce ones, so even with Báihǔ 白虎 presiding and Sìhào 四耗 lurking, it remains a day for pressing forward. Only the sun past noon begins to decline, and at the height of abundance, arrogance and waste are the sharpest dangers — those eight items on the unfavorable list are precisely the margin the ancients left for a day at full blaze.
