Nothing is suitable

On Xīn Mǎo day, the day stem Xīn (Metal) sits atop Mǎo (Wood); Metal overcomes Wood, forming indirect wealth, which governs keen profit instincts and business acumen, yet Xīn is yin Metal — soft yet resilient — and when clashed by Mǎo Wood, it gains the sharpness of a blade that can cut through iron. After White Dew, Yǒu Metal commands the season, and Xīn Metal receives the support of the monthly branch, making Metal qi quite strong and able to bear wealth and office. Today Míngtáng of the Yellow Path presides, normally governing brightness, integrity and hidden noble person support, and Wǔhé and Míngfèiduì gather as auspicious spirits. Wǔhé governs harmony and smooth interpersonal relations; Míngfèiduì governs ancestral blessings through sacrificial rites. Yet among inauspicious spirits, an overwhelming force of nine arrives together: Yuèpò, Dàhào, Zāishà, Tiānhuǒ, Yuèyàn, Dìhuǒ, Wǔxū, Fùrì and Dàhuì. Among them, Yuèpò (monthly clash) occurs when the monthly branch Yǒu clashes with the day branch Mǎo; it is also called a "broken day," governing ruin, loss and the failure of major undertakings. Dàhào governs major financial loss and property dispersal; Zāishà governs sudden calamities and unexpected misfortune; Tiānhuǒ and Dìhuǒ warn of fire hazards; Yuèyàn governs yin gloom and inauspiciousness; Wǔxū governs empty granaries; Fùrì governs repetition and entanglement; Dàhuì warns that gatherings may spawn change. The "Yi" column lists only sacrificial rites, healing, demolishing houses, tearing down walls and "No other matters" — all are matters of "removing the old and dispelling evil," while the "Ji" column states "Nothing is suitable," presenting an overall pattern of "auspicious and inauspicious forces severely mismatched, defense as the best offense." Today is only suitable for paying respects to ancestors, dispelling illness, and tearing down old structures; all undertakings of establishing the new such as opening for business, marriage, moving into a house, signing contracts or long journeys should be avoided.

Metaphysics Insight

"Yuèpò" (monthly clash) is one of the most ominous markers in traditional almanacs, also known as a "broken day." Its cause is extremely clear: when the monthly earthly branch clashes with the daily earthly branch, it becomes Yuèpò. Today, the eighth month (branch Yǒu) on the fourth day (branch Mǎo) — Mǎo and Yǒu clash — hence it is a Yuèpò day. In the Twelve Deities system, the broken day corresponds to the "Broken" (破 Pò) deity, governing ruin, loss, decomposition and dispersion — a day specially designated for "removing the old" rather than "establishing the new." The ancients believed that on Yuèpò days, the qi of heaven and earth clashes violently, yin and yang fail to intersect, and all things struggle to grow, hence major undertakings should be avoided. The "Yi" items on a broken day are often highly specific — sacrificial rites borrow ancestral power to resolve clashes, healing borrows the "decomposing" energy of the broken day to dispel illness, and demolishing houses and walls borrows the "ruining" force to tear down old structures — all are acts of going with the flow. But if one acts against this nature by undertaking "establishing the new" activities such as opening for business, marriage, moving into a house or signing contracts, they are prone to obstruction, financial loss and unexpected changes due to the clashing qi. Today on Xīn Mǎo Yuèpò, Dàhào arrives together, making the force of financial loss especially severe; Zāishà, Tiānhuǒ and Dìhuǒ gather together, multiplying the risk of sudden calamities. When the ancients encountered a Yuèpò day, they often closed their doors to visitors, rested and cultivated their spirits, waiting for the clash to pass before pursuing progress — this is the wisdom of顺应天时 (complying with heavenly timing).
