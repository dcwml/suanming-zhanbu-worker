Today is Gēng Shēn 庚申 — the stem Gēng is yang metal, the metal of swords and axes, firm and sharp; the branch Shēn is likewise metal, and metal sits in its flourishing position: this is a day when autumn metal is at its peak. The Nayin is again Pomegranate Wood — the pomegranate tree is tough enough to endure the pruning of metal, so luxuriant metal and graceful wood appear together, a picture of sharp qi ripening into fruit. The presiding deity is Tiānláo 天牢, an inauspicious spirit of the Black Path governing confinement and obstruction — so setting beds, breaking ground, burial, digging graves for the living and assembling coffin timber are all placed on the unfavorable list; yet the auspicious spirits Wáng Rì 王日, Tiāncāng 天仓, Chúshén 除神 and Míngfèi 鸣吠 attend alongside — Wáng Rì is the ruling day of the Yellow Path, Chúshén carries the Removal officer and governs cleansing away the old, Tiāncāng keeps the granaries and stores of wealth, and Míngfèi is the dawn-crow of good tidings — so sacrifice, cutting garments, installing doors, collecting wealth, sweeping the house, traveling, welcoming people into the household, installing a stove and acquiring livestock are all placed on the favorable list. The qi of this day favors gathering over expansion: metal at its peak is fit for cutting and shaping, the granary spirit presides over amassing wealth and stores — only ground-breaking and burial must be avoided; act with measure, and stop once things go well.

Zodiac Fortune · Monkey

Today's branch is Shēn (申), which corresponds to the Monkey — today's featured zodiac. Those born in a Monkey year sit squarely on the day's flourishing branch with metal qi in command, and the day favors collecting wealth, sweeping the house, installing doors and cutting garments — Monkeys will do especially well to settle accounts, put their affairs in order, sign contracts and tidy up shopfronts and paperwork; Tiāncāng governs stores of wealth, so seek fortune toward the Wealth God in the due East. Mind three cautions: first, today clashes with the Tiger and the Sha lies South — avoid direct confrontation with those born in a Tiger year, and postpone long journeys southward; second, setting beds, breaking ground and burial are unfavorable — bed placement, construction work and funeral matters should wait for another day; third, the presiding deity Tiānláo is an inauspicious spirit of the Black Path governing obstruction and confinement — Monkeys may meet blocked words and delayed paperwork today, so keep speech and conduct restrained and do not force matters; better to put your own house in order than to press for grand results. In health, metal qi runs high, and strong metal wounds wood — the liver and gallbladder qi of Monkeys tends toward stagnation and temper may run short; eat something sour to soothe the liver, take an evening stroll to stretch the body, and avoid staying up late in anger or agitation.

Folklore

Among today's auspicious spirits is Tiāncāng 天仓 — but who, exactly, is this spirit? In the old almanac's pantheon, Tiāncāng is the auspicious spirit in charge of granaries and stored wealth, and its origins reach back to ancient star-lore: the heavens bear constellations named for barns and storehouses, and Tiāncāng is the celestial treasury, governing the gathering and storage of grain, treasure and wealth in the human world. The Xiéjì Biànfāng Shū 协纪辨方书, the Qing-dynasty official compendium of date selection, lists Tiāncāng among the auspicious spirits to be sought, holding that on a day when Tiāncāng presides it is fitting to store up goods, collect rents and dues, open the granaries and settle households and storehouses. Folk date-selection likewise treats a Tiāncāng day as an auspicious day for filling the barn and taking in wealth — and with "collecting wealth" and "sweeping the house" on today's favorable list and the Wealth God dwelling due East, today's almanac shows wealth qi returning to the storehouse. One thing worth knowing: the virtue of a granary lies in guarding and storing rather than in aggressive acquisition — the ancients kept grain in the barn and drew on it only in due season, and the way of seeking wealth is the same: take in when it is time to take in, store away when it is time to store, keep the barn full without exhausting its use, and wealth can last. That is the wisdom handed down in the two characters of Tiāncāng: only those who know how to keep truly possess their wealth.
