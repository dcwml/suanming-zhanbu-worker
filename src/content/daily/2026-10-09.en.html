No matters are suitable 诸事不宜

On a Bǐng Chén day, Bǐng fire sits upon Chén — a damp earthen reservoir of water, where the warm sun sinks into marshland and the flame's vigor turns inward to store. The Na Yin is Earth in the Sand: earth gathered from loose sand, porous and unbearing. Today's most striking feature is the meeting of "breaking" and "virtue": the day branch Chén clashes head-on with the month's command Xū, and a branch that clashes the month's command becomes the Month Breaker — the Great Consumer, the Nine Emptinesses and the Departed Journey all spring from that single clash, hence the avoided column speaks plainly: no matters are suitable. Yet in a Xū month the Heavenly Virtue falls upon Bǐng, and so does the Lunar Virtue — a double virtue alighting on the day's own stem — with the Resolving Spirit to dissolve harm and the Mother's Store and the Benefactor of Posterity in support, while the Azure Dragon holds the Yellow Path. However thick the virtues, the breaker's force is singular: the qi count is the body and the spirits its ministers, so even under a host of guardian spirits only sacrifice, clearing away misfortune, demolition and wall-tearing are taken — turning the breaker's force upon itself, letting resolution dissolve the harm; pulling down the old and sweeping clean is precisely a Breaker day's proper work, and everything else may well wait.

Zodiac Fortune · Dragon 龙

Today's earthly branch is Chén, which corresponds to the Dragon — making the Dragon the zodiac protagonist of the day. Dragon people carry their natal branch today, and by rare fortune both the Heavenly and Lunar Virtues alight on the day stem Bǐng fire, with the Azure Dragon holding the Yellow Path beside them — a posture that might well soar. But Chén clashes Xū: the natal branch itself clashes the month's command to form the Month Breaker, and a clash scatters what it stirs — stored strength beats spent strength, exactly what "no matters are suitable" intends. What may be done today narrows to sacrifice, clearing, demolition and wall-tearing: honoring ancestors, offering incense and fulfilling vows, sweeping the house clean, sorting through long-stored old possessions and discarding the worn-out all fit the day. Opening ventures, signing contracts, investing and collecting money are best avoided outright — with the Great Consumer and the Nine Emptinesses at hand, guarding the purse beats growing it; travel and relocation should likewise wait, for the Departed Journey bodes ill for setting out, and a far journey invites snags. The day clashes with the Dog and the Sha lies South: those born in the Year of the Dog should avoid long journeys toward the South and refrain from quarrels with Dragons. Cold Dew has passed and the autumn chill deepens daily — add layers morning and night, and keep meals warm to guard the stomach.

Metaphysics Insight

Today's avoided column holds just four characters: no matters are suitable — an almanac verdict this blunt is rare, and its root lies in the chief of today's inauspicious spirits, the Month Breaker. When the day branch clashes the month branch, the day is "broken by the month": today's branch Chén runs head-on into the command of the Xū month, and that single Chén–Xū clash is like a boat rowing against the month's whole tide — hence the almanac's iron rule that on a Month Breaker day great undertakings are not to be used, with weddings, business openings, taking office and travel all set aside. What breaks, dissipates: the Great Consumer follows, the Nine Emptinesses leave the treasury gaping, the Departed Journey means perishing on the road, the Four Strikes forbid the clash of arms — every one of these spirits springs from the single word "break." Yet look closer at the favored column: demolishing dilapidated houses and tearing down walls stand plainly listed — destruction itself finds its day on the Breaker, a famous device of date-selection known as "turning the breaker upon the breaking." On a Breaker day the qi prefers release to gathering: pulling down the old, clearing away, sweeping out misfortune moves with heaven and earth instead of against them — the old must go before the new can come, so demolition earns its place on the very day of breaking. And note the auspicious column: the Heavenly and Lunar Virtues both alight on the day stem Bǐng — in a Xū month the Heavenly Virtue falls on Bǐng, and in the Yín–Wǔ–Xū months the Lunar Virtue falls on Bǐng too. With virtues guarding the person, even a crowd of baleful spirits finds its harm dissolved: such is the almanac's balancing art — the qi count is the body, the spirits its ministers; a breaker's day borne up by a double virtue keeps alive the one thread of sacrifice and clearing. Date-selection never simply pastes on labels of "lucky" and "unlucky": within breaking there is use, within luck there are limits — read the clashes and harmonies of stem and branch together with the checks and transformations among the spirits, and every line of the almanac finds its origin.
