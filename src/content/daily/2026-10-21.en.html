Yesterday the Peach Blossom stood in full flower; today the stems and branches turn over and Wù earth takes its seat upon Chén — Wù is the thick earth of city walls, Chén the damp earth of the reservoir, yet the Na Yin turns to Wood of the Great Forest: over deep soil a forest grows of itself. The sixty Jiazi trade their Na Yin every two days — the fire gathers in, and the wood steps forward. Qīnglóng, the Azure Dragon, first of the six Yellow Path spirits, attends the day, which should read as a page of gladness and noble help — but the day branch Chén runs headlong into the month branch Xū, and that single Chén–Xū clash makes a Month Breaker: the breaker's qi scatters and will not gather, the Great Consumer follows to drain the purse, the Nine Emptinesses leave the treasury gaping, the Departed Journey bodes ill for the road, and the Repeated Day forbids letting any affair begin twice — so even with the Azure Dragon in the seat, the almanac can hold open only such quiet matters as worship and bathing, and writes plainly "all other matters set aside." The breaker's proper use lies in the word itself: demolishing a dilapidated house and tearing down walls stand in the favored column — pull down the old wall, clear away what has piled up, close out what has hung unresolved, and the breaking qi turns to clearing ground for the new: the date-selector's famous device of "turning the breaker upon the breaking." Jiěshén attends to dissolve old grudges; Mǔcāng nurtures, Yìhòu blesses the generations to come, Tiān'ēn confers grace — every auspicious spirit keeps to its modest office, and each proves itself in small matters. Marriage rites, moving into a home, raising the beam, travel and burial — the five great matters all stand avoided: today favors stillness over motion, and closing over opening.

Zodiac Fortune · Dragon 龙

Today's earthly branch is Chén, which corresponds to the Dragon — making the Dragon the zodiac protagonist of the day. Dragon people stand on their own natal ground today, yet that ground is broken: the day branch Chén is met head-on by the month branch Xū, and fortune moves like surf against a seawall, rising and falling in a hurry, with mood quick to spill over alongside it — let no great undertaking be launched on this day. The good news is that Qīnglóng, the Azure Dragon, holds the Yellow Path and is kin to the Chén Dragon by very nature, with Tiān'ēn and Jiěshén at its side: old grievances may be released and deadlocks dissolved, and any matter long left hanging is well closed out today. Worship and offerings, bathing and quieting the mind are precisely the day's proper work — washing off the stale and gathering the spirit buys more than any new venture forced open. Guard wealth with special care: with the Great Consumer on duty and the Nine Emptinesses at the treasury, contracts, business openings, investments and loans all wait; watch your belongings out of doors, and let nothing beyond the shopping list leave your pocket. The day clashes with the Dog and the Sha lies South: keep clear of contention and major ventures with Dog people, and let long journeys or moves toward the South wait. Marriage rites, moving into a home, raising the beam, travel and burial all stand in the avoided column — today every major milestone of life gives way: the dragon coils in the deep, awaiting the rising wind, and once the breaker has passed, the qi will open of itself.

Metaphysics Insight

Today's celestial column seats the head of the six Yellow Path spirits — Qīnglóng, the Azure Dragon — while the favorable column offers only four matters, worship, bathing, demolition and wall-tearing, and tacks on "all other matters set aside" for good measure: the chief auspicious spirit attends, yet no great affair may open. The seeming contradiction is one of the old almanac's standing lessons — the spirits are the surface, the stems and branches the substance, and the surface must be read against the substance. First the surface: the Azure Dragon is one of the Four Symbols. The Records of the Grand Historian divides the sky into five palaces, and the Eastern Palace spreads the Azure Dragon across its seven mansions — Jiǎo for the horn, Kàng for the neck, Dī and Fáng for the body, Xīn for the heart, Wěi and Jī for the tail — a dragon's shape lying across the spring night's eastern sky. When the folk calendar says "on the second day of the second month the dragon raises its head," the head it raises is this dragon's: the Jiǎo mansion climbing above the eastern horizon at dusk. Date-selection took that living qi of the eastern wood virtue and installed it first among the twelve celestial spirits: the Azure Dragon presides over gladness, over noble helpers, over growth, and on any Azure Dragon day the almanac's weather turns bright. Now the substance: today the day branch Chén crashes straight into the month branch Xū, and one Chén–Xū collision makes the Month Breaker — the breaking qi scatters and will not gather, and no amount of the Dragon's gladness can underwrite every affair. So the favored column keeps only worship and bathing, and borrows the demolition entries to break with the breaking. And here is the day's finest turn: Chén is itself the Dragon's branch — the Azure Dragon holds the day, and the day belongs to the Dragon: a double-dragon meeting that should have staged a grand auspicious scene, undone by one vein of breaking qi running through it. There lies the old book's deepest habit: lucky and unlucky are never a single label — read the rotation of the spirits against the clashes and harmonies of stem and branch, and the true face of the day appears.
