Groundbreaking · Breaking ground for burial · Acquiring property · Digging wells

On Dīng Yǒu day, Dīng fire sits upon Yǒu metal — fire smelting autumn ore, its edge keen yet held within. Yùtáng, the Jade Hall, presides on the Yellow Path: an auspicious star of peace and gladness, attended by Guānrì, Liùyí, Yìhòu, Chúshén and Míngfèi — Míngfèi, the crowing rooster and barking dog, ministers to the affairs of burial, so burial stands in the favors; Chúshén, the Spirit of Removal, presides over washing the old away, making bathing and sweeping the house seasonable; on the Jade Hall's lucky day, travel and ancestral rites both go smoothly. Yet Yuèjiàn, Xiǎoshí and Tǔfǔ arrive together — the Month's Establishment sitting on its own branch in the Yǒu month, the Bureau of Earth keeping watch — so groundbreaking, breaking ground for burial, digging wells and acquiring property must all be avoided, with Yuèxíng, Yànduì and Zhāoyáo filing in behind. Hence even a Yellow Path day counsels "no other undertakings": today favors the few, not the many. The Autumn Equinox is but three days off, metal's vigor at its height and soon to divide — sweep the courtyard, gather in the heart: to move with the season is itself the seeking of good fortune.

Zodiac Fortune · Rooster 鸡

Today's earthly branch is Yǒu, which corresponds to the Rooster — making the Rooster the zodiac protagonist of the day. Roosters preside over their own branch and gain its vigor, with Yùtáng, the Jade Hall, shining from the Yellow Path above: clear of thought and composed of bearing, this is a day to win by stillness — ancestral rites, bathing and sweeping the house stand in the favors: pay respects and give thanks, wash the dust away, put the household in order. Travel goes smoothly too: take meetings and negotiations to the South (Joy God South), business and money matters to the Southwest (Wealth God Southwest), and calls upon elders and patrons to the Southeast (Fortune God Southeast). Only Yuèjiàn and Tǔfǔ keep their watch: groundbreaking, breaking ground for burial, digging wells and acquiring property stand in the taboos — buying real estate, starting renovation or sinking wells should wait; on the Jade Hall's own day, do not force the matter: keeping and tidying beat venturing and opening. The day clashes with the Rabbit and the Sha lies East: those born in the Year of the Rabbit should keep a low profile in all matters, avoid long journeys east, and refrain from quarrels with Roosters.

Metaphysics Insight

Head of today's inauspicious spirits is Yuèjiàn — the Month's Establishment — so let us speak of the twelve Jiànchú gods it brings with it. Yuèjiàn simply means the day's branch matching the month's branch: today is Dīng Yǒu in a Yǒu month, the very day the god Jiàn, the Establishment, keeps watch — a Jiàn day. The twelve Jiànchú gods — Establishment, Removal, Fullness, Level, Settlement, Grasp, Breaking, Peril, Completion, Harvest, Opening and Closing — begin their round at the Month's Establishment and rotate in turn: one of China's oldest arts of day-selection, recorded complete in the Rìshū almanacs of bamboo slips unearthed at Shuìhǔdì in Yúnmèng, Hubei, more than twenty-two centuries old, and set down in the Huáinánzǐ — "Yín is the Establishment, Mǎo the Removal, Chén the Fullness, Sì the Level"... Jiàn is first of the gods, the image of yang's first stirring and the sprouting of the ten thousand things, and the old almanac rule runs: on a Jiàn day, travel, taking office and calling upon the eminent are fitting, while stirring the earth is barred — today's favors hold "travel", today's taboos hold groundbreaking, breaking ground, digging wells and acquiring property: the rule of two thousand years ago and the almanac of this morning agree line for line. Why does the Jiàn day alone forbid earth? The old books say Jiàn is attended by Xiǎoshí and Tǔfǔ, the Lesser Hour and the Bureau of Earth — both standing in today's inauspicious list: with the Bureau of Earth on duty, earth's vigor is at its fullest, and to stir the soil is to go against the season and offend the exuberant element — so acquiring property and digging wells are set aside with the rest. And there is a pleasing twist: the almanac keeps a second company of twelve — the Yellow and Black Path gods, of which Qīnglóng, Míngtáng, Jīnkuì, Tiāndé, Yùtáng and Sīmìng rule the Yellow Path — today Yùtáng presides, and the page reads Yellow Path, auspicious; yet the folk rhyme counts "Jiàn, Mǎn, Píng, Shōu" among the black, so in that system the Jiàn day ranks dark. Two companies of twelve gods, each with its own system, sharing one page — such is the charm of the old almanac: many systems weighed together, and a caution tucked inside good fortune, teaching one when to advance and when to hold back.
